# Outing-Risk Taxonomy for Trans Travelers — Research Checklist

Companion to PLAN §5.2. **Scan this whole list for every country** so the score doesn't
tunnel-vision on a handful of parameters (the historical over-focus: border, bathrooms,
prison). Not every item will have evidence in every country — note the axes where the
country has no trans-specific signals at all, and flag low-confidence ones.

Two dimensions always apply: **probability of being outed** (documents routinely
checked? scanners? social cues? police spot-checks?) × **severity if outed**
(discretion-tolerant society vs. catastrophic exposure). Example: Gulf states are
low-probability/catastrophic-severity; some European countries are
higher-probability/low-severity. The score reflects both.

---

## Severity ladder (for weighting findings)
| Level | Examples |
|---|---|
| 5 — Catastrophic | State or honor killing; death penalty applied; torture in custody; lethal violence with impunity |
| 4 — Severe | Imprisonment (esp. misgendered facility); sexual violence in detention; forced medical procedures; deportation to danger; denial of life-saving care; forced marriage/conversion captivity |
| 3 — Major | Arrest & prosecution; violent assault by public; blackmail/extortion; forced outing to family/employer; denial of entry/stranding; medication confiscation without replacement |
| 2 — Moderate | Police harassment/humiliation; discrimination in services/hotels; hospital-ward misplacement; intimidation; invasive pat-downs/searches |
| 1 — Minor | Staring, invasive curiosity, misgendering, occasional service friction, online mockery |

## Axis checklist (trans-specific, traveler-specific)

### A. Border, transit & security screening
- Passport/visa gender-marker mismatch → questioning, refusal of entry, visa denial
- X or "diverse" markers not recognized
- Airport body scanners flagging body/presentation mismatch → "anomaly resolution" pat-downs, forced outing at security (recurring global pain point — research it!)
- Device/phone searches at the border revealing trans identity (socials, apps, photos, medical records)
- Airline ticket name/gender vs. passport mismatch
- Border-police humiliation (see France/Giovanna) — public outing at the counter
- Transit through third countries (transit-visa/document checks)

### B. Documents & everyday bureaucracy
- Hotel check-in with mismatched ID → outing to staff; refusal of accommodation
- Single-sex hostel dorms
- SIM-card registration (passport required in many countries)
- Card/payment name mismatch; banking/currency-exchange passport checks
- Police ID spot-checks forcing outing (documented in countries with street stop-and-check)

### C. Gendered spaces & facilities
- Bathrooms, locker rooms, changing rooms (legal right + social safety; criminal/civil exposure — e.g. US state bans, UK code)
- Saunas, spas, baths, onsen/sento (Japan case; German Sauna-Bund debates)
- Women-only transport (train carriages in e.g. Japan, Mexico, India, Indonesia), women-only beaches/pools/events
- Religious-site gender segregation (mosque visits etc.)
- **Hospital ward placement if injured/ill** (distinct from prison placement — a car accident can out you to medics)
- Beaches/swimwear-based outing

### D. Public presence & social reaction
- Staring, invasive questions, mockery, public humiliation
- Being filmed/photographed and posted online → doxxing (travelers are identifiable strangers)
- Refusals of service (taxis, cafés, tour operators, venues)
- Local-media attention in smaller communities ("foreign trans person" as spectacle)
- Whether foreigner status protects (locals don't expect conformity) or exacerbates (scapegoating)

### E. Violence & predation
- Hate violence from the public (trans-specific, not general-LGB)
- Targeted sexual violence ("corrective" assault patterns)
- Dating-app ambushes/robberies/assaults targeting trans users (documented pattern; travelers use apps abroad)
- Blackmail/extortion: "pay or I out you to your hotel/embassy/family" — travelers are soft targets (isolated, short-stay, no local network)
- Robbery premised on non-reporting (assumption trans victims won't go to police)

### F. Police interaction
- Stops/questioning → forced outing via documents or appearance
- Police harassment, mockery, physical abuse of trans people (not general brutality)
- Entrapment via apps (police-run dating-app profiles — documented in several countries)
- Extortion/bribery targeting trans people
- Whether police protect trans victims or worsen the situation when called

### G. Law & criminal exposure (if outed)
- Explicit cross-dressing/"posing"/impersonation laws (Brunei §198; some Sharia codes)
- Morality/indecency provisions (Saudi §197-style)
- Fraud/"misrepresentation" laws applied to documents or presentation (Florida DL analog)
- Same-sex-conduct laws triggered by traveling **with a partner** (trans woman + male partner in a criminalizing country)
- "Propaganda"/"justifying transition" laws (Russia art. 6.21-style) — online content, phone contents
- Dress codes (formal or enforced-by-custom)

### H. Arrest, detention & prison placement
- Police/immigration detention conditions for trans people
- Forced genital/medical "verification" exams (France prison cases; asylum detention patterns)
- Prison placement: legal gender vs. lived gender; men's-prison placement of trans women; isolation/segregation; abuse in custody
- Deportation / removal — and to where (danger of the destination)?
- Consular access for detained foreign trans travelers

### I. Health & medication
- HRT import: legality, prescription requirements, customs seizure
- Injectables/syringes at security
- Refills abroad if medication runs out/lost
- Emergency care discrimination when discovered trans
- Travel insurance excluding trans-related (or all) care once trans status known

### J. Family & diaspora exposure (distinct traveler pattern)
- Travelers **visiting relatives**: family-imposed violence, captivity, forced marriage, forced "conversion therapy" (the "taken abroad to be fixed" scenario — applies to diaspora youth visiting family's country)
- Family-arranged marriage to "correct" identity
- Community outing → honor-based violence risk (trans-specific honor violence)

### K. State & media climate (probability modifier, not standalone)
- Government/media demonization campaigns raising likelihood & severity of all axes
- Surveillance states (device/social monitoring raising discovery probability)
- Political trajectory: backsliding vs. reform (UK code; Portugal repeal attempt; Germany's Länder reforms)

## Explicitly out of scope (do not score; popup's out-of-scope section only)
General LGB rights (marriage, adoption, LGB criminalization per se), general crime/
terrorism/health risks, resident-only trans issues (domestic LGR regime, transition-
healthcare infrastructure, employment law, adoption by trans people), ILGA %/Equaldex/
Spartacus/FRA scores as direct inputs.

## Researcher guidance
1. For each country, **scan all axes A–K**; record evidence per axis even if "no
   signals found" — that absence is itself data.
2. Score = probability-of-outing × severity-if-outed, integrated across the axes the
   country's situation makes salient.
3. Rotate search queries across different axes across the 20-result batches — don't
   let every query be a "safety/laws" query (the pattern that produced the original
   over-focus). Suggested rotation: (A/B) border+documents → (C/E) facilities+violence
   → (D/F) reaction+police → (G/H) law+detention → (I/J) health+family → K climate.
4. Traveler-specific incidents (detained tourists, airport scanner cases, app
   ambushes of visitors, family-visiting cases) are the highest-value evidence —
   prioritize them over resident-rights coverage.
