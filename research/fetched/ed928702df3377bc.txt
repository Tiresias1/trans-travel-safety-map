												<div class="columns large-7">
							<p>After the breakdown of communist regimes in Eastern Europe in late 1989, Mongolia saw its own Democratic Revolution in early 1990, which led to a multi-party system, a new constitution in 1992, and the &#8211; rather rough &#8211; transition to a market economy. Mongolia is the most sparsely populated independent country in the world with a population of around 2.9 million people. It is also the world&#8217;s second-largest landlocked country after Kazakhstan. The country contains very little arable land, as much of its area is covered by arid and unproductive steppes. Mongolia&#8217;s economy is centered on agriculture and mining. The country has rich mineral resources, and copper, coal, molybdenum, tin, tungsten, and gold account for a large part of industrial production. Homosexuality in Mongolia has virtually no presence, no venues and no activism. There are no laws specifically against homosexuality in Mongolia, however &quot;satisfaction of immoral sexual needs&quot; may still be illegal. Accurate information is hard to find. However, one 2008 report said: &quot;There are two organizations which hold monthly parties for their hundreds of members. It is all very furtive. Unfortunately someone had a hidden camera at one party and the photos found their way on to the Internet. Now people are afraid of being outed.&quot;</p>
						 </div>

							<div class="country-info">Population: 2662000</div>
							<div class="country-info">Capital: Ulaanbaatar - Pop. 812000</div>
