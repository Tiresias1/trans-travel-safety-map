It was funded by the Secretariat of Pacific Community (SPC), the Regional Rights Resource Team through the Country Focal Officer in Vanuatu and supported by the Vanuatu Women Centre (VWC). The workshop ‘Introduction to LGBTI Human Rights, SOGIE, Health and Gender Based Violence Workshop’ was the first between the Vanuatu Women’s Centre and VPride Foundation. The VWC’s Ms. Vola Matas spearheaded the morning presentation with support from Ms. Merilyn Tahi and Jill Makikon. It included a group discussion about gender and equality and domestic violence. Ms. Matas encouraged the VPride members to workshop in small groups and asked them to explore gender stereotypes. As the groups wrote down the expectations of what a male and female is supposed to ‘be’ and ‘do’ in Ni-Vanuatu society, it became evident that not conforming to gender roles was the root of discrimination. ‘What do we tell little boys? We tell them not to cry, we make them wear trousers, we give them blue clothes and make them play with trucks. If they do cry, we think they are weak, and if they don’t like trucks, we think there is something wrong with them,’ says Ms. Matas. ‘What do we tell little girls? We tell them to wear a dress, we give them pink things, we make them cook and clean. And what happens when they don’t do it? We beat them.’ Ms. Matas went on to explain that gender-based violence against women could be applied to violence against the LGBTI community in Vanuatu. ‘Many women get beaten by their husbands for not cooking or cleaning. Because you don’t fit into either the ‘male’ or ‘female’ category, you will probably understand the discrimination first-hand,’ said Ms. Matas to the VPride members. ‘Gender is a learnt thing. We already discussed what we teach boys and girls from a young age and how if we don’t conform to the norm, we face discrimination. We can unlearn this gender stereotyping. The community is bias and it must be understood that we all have a right to life and we are all God’s creations.’ Ms. Tahi added, ‘Anyone who fights his wife is not strong. That is weak. Women can carry a baby for nine months – that is strong.’ She also encouraged any member of the LGBTI community who experiences violence to seek support from the VWC. Unfortunately if someone is in a same-sex relationship and experiencing violence, they are not protected under the Family Protection Act 2008 and have to apply under the penal code. Ms. Anita Jowitt, a USP Law Lecturer, further discussed the law, culture and politics of homophobia in Vanuatu. She offered interesting insight into the history of the LGBTI community in Vanuatu and revealed that there has only been one case of violence against lesbians being prosecuted. Besides that, the LGBTI presence in court is almost invisible. When the VPride Foundation was established in 2014, it was opposed by church leaders, government officials and chiefs. At the time, chairman of the Vanuatu Christian Council of Churches, Bishop James Ligo, said, ‘Vanuatu is a Melanesian country, with our diversity, we do not want to see the life that people are living in other parts of the world happening in our country. So VCCC is prepared to work with the chiefs, with the government, in order to see what can be done in addressing the situation.’ Despite the rhetoric, Vanuatu has in fact repealed criminalization of consensual homosexual acts with persons under 18 in 2006. It also supported the joint UN Human Rights Council statement in 2011 on ending acts of violence and related human rights violations based on sexual orientation and sexual identity. The workshop ended with a visit from the Ministry of Health who discussed rights to health access and testing for HIV/STIs. Gillio Baxter, who organized the workshop, says it was a successful day and is looking forward to making progress for the LGBT community in Vanuatu. MORE PHOTOS Follow VPride Foundation on Facebook . The call was made yesterday during Vanuatu’s celebration of International Literacy Day at Pango Centre School, where Minister of Education Simil Johnson joined education leaders, teachers, students and the Pango community to observe the Ministry’s Teaching and Learning Reform in practice. Minister Johnson said literacy is more than simply learning how to read and write, describing it as a foundation for children’s confidence, knowledge and future opportunities. “When we teach a child to read, we are not simply teaching a technical skill. We are building confidence, knowledge and opportunities for the future,” he said. He stressed that every child in Vanuatu should have the opportunity to develop literacy and numeracy skills, adding that improving learning outcomes cannot be left to teachers alone. “Literacy is not only the responsibility of teachers. It is the responsibility of all of us — government, schools, teachers, parents, communities and our partners,” Minister Johnson said. He encouraged parents to make reading part of everyday family life by reading with their children, asking questions, looking at books together and encouraging them to develop an interest in reading. The message was reinforced by Pango Centre School Grade 3 teacher Judith Tasso, who said parents can make an important contribution without needing expensive resources or being trained teachers. “Reading with children is very important because it helps them develop their language, vocabulary and understanding,” Tasso said. “When parents read with their children, listen to them and ask questions about what they are reading, they are helping children become more confident and interested in learning.” At Pango Centre School, the MoET showed how its Teaching and Learning Reform is being translated into practical classroom strategies. Teachers demonstrated high-impact approaches, including morning routines and read-aloud activities, which are designed to help children settle and focus while developing language, vocabulary, reading and comprehension. Pango Centre School Principal Joe Kalotap said the reform is giving teachers clearer direction and practical tools to strengthen their teaching. “The Teaching and Learning Reform gives our teachers practical strategies that they can use in their classrooms and opportunities to reflect on and improve their teaching,” Kalotap said. The reform covers education from Kindergarten through Year 10, with a particular emphasis on improving literacy and numeracy outcomes. It places teachers and school leaders at the centre of the process, supported through professional learning, coaching and collaboration. A new technology initiative was also highlighted during the celebration, with Minister Johnson presenting Pango Centre School with a Swivl recording device. The device can follow a teacher around the classroom while a lesson is being delivered, allowing teachers to record their teaching and later review their classroom practice. The Ministry says the recordings can help teachers identify areas for improvement, reflect on their teaching and share effective approaches with colleagues. The technology could also support e-learning and learning continuity when schools are affected by disasters or other emergencies. Following its use at Pango Centre School, the device will be made available to other schools so more teachers can benefit from the technology and share examples of effective teaching. The Ministry has reaffirmed its ambition for everyone in Vanuatu to have the opportunity to learn to read and write, with efforts continuing through partnerships involving teachers, school leaders, parents, communities and education partners. The Ministry acknowledged the support of Australia, New Zealand and the Global Partnership for Education in supporting the Teaching and Learning Reform. As Vanuatu marked International Literacy Day, the Ministry’s message was summed up by its campaign slogan: “Yumi ridim, yumi lanem, yumi gro” — Let us read, let us learn, let us grow. Allegations of serious maternity negligence at VNH under investigation September 7, 2026 The Office of the Opposition has conveyed that it is investigating reports that surgical gauze may have been left inside women following caesarean section operations at the Vanuatu National Hospital (VNH). The Opposition is also investigating reports of increased stillbirths, including an alleged case where a breech baby was delivered naturally despite claims that a caesarean section was required. These were conveyed by the Opposition Leader, Ishmael Kalsakau in a statement. He said that, if proven, the allegations would raise serious concerns about patient safety, medical standards and hospital management. He said surgical items such as gauze left inside patients can cause serious health problems, including infection, pain and the need for further surgery. In severe cases, they can be life-threatening. He warned that failure to address the allegations could undermine public confidence in Central Hospital and public maternity services. He stressed that fear of unsafe care could discourage women from giving birth in health facilities and lead some to choose private services or unsafe deliveries outside medical facilities. “Women must have confidence that public hospitals provide safe and respectful maternity care for them and their newborns, he said. “The right of women to safe, respectful and evidence-based care must be protected. Women also have the right to full information about their treatment and what happens to them.” He said judicial intervention and professional disciplinary action should be considered where negligence or medical malpractice is established. The Opposition is demanding that the Ministry of Health immediately identify and urgently screen all women who underwent caesarean sections at the hospital during the past 12 months. The Opposition also called for disciplinary measures against hospital staff found to have been negligent, including immediate suspension where warranted, and wants independent investigators appointed to conduct a thorough investigation. It is also calling on the VNH to establish a permanent external clinical-governance advisory panel to monitor reforms and publish quarterly reports. The Opposition is also demanding that the Minister of Health personally attend the hospital, publicly confirm the measures being implemented and lead a coordinated response to the concerns. “The public has a right to know what measures are being taken to address these concerns and to ensure that similar incidents do not happen again,” said Kalsakau in the statement. The Opposition is demanding an urgent independent investigation into these alleged serious allegations at the country’s main referral hospital. The Daily Post contacted the Superintendent Dr. Robert Vocor and reached out to the Director of Curative Services for comment but had not received a response prior to print. Efate families call for minimum wage rise as bus fares increase September 2, 2026 Higher Efate bus fares are prompting calls from some families for a review of the national minimum wage as rising transport costs put further pressure on household budgets. The new bus fares for Efate, which became effective yesterday, have prompted calls from some Efate families for a review of the national minimum wage to help workers cope with rising living expenses. The new fare structure starts at VT150 for the first five-kilometre zone established by the Public Land Transport Authority (PLTA) and increases by VT50 for every additional five kilometres. The Vanuatu Daily Post spoke with several families this morning about the impact of the new fares, with some expressing concern that the increase will place additional pressure on household budgets. Some families said the cost of daily transportation is becoming increasingly difficult to manage, particularly for households with children travelling to school and parents commuting to work. Families interviewed by the Daily Post said that if transportation and other living costs continue to rise, there should also be serious consideration given to increasing the national minimum wage to help workers cope with the rising cost of living. One family said that wages must be reviewed alongside increases in basic expenses, arguing that workers should not be expected to absorb rising transportation costs without an improvement in income. The Daily Post also witnessed some families from Teouma negotiating with bus drivers yesterday to allow them to travel using the former fare. However, passengers will be expected to pay the new rates under the revised system. For low-income households, the additional transport costs could become a significant weekly expense. Passengers travelling from areas in North Efate towards North-East Efate could spend more than VT1,000 on return bus fares, depending on their destination and the applicable fare zone. Higher bus fares could also contribute to increases in the cost of locally produced food at markets, as farmers and vendors pay more to transport their produce from rural communities into Port Vila. Pentecost woman buys ship to serve Vanuatu August 31, 2026 A ni-Vanuatu woman from Pentecost is bringing a new vessel to Vanuatu next week, with plans to begin operations in September to provide services to communities across the country. Bethina Bulebanne, 48, who currently lives in New Caledonia, said her decision to purchase the vessel was driven by her love for the ocean, her family’s connection to the sea and her desire to contribute something to Vanuatu. Bulebanne was born and raised in Fanafo, Santo, but her family originates from the coastal village of Bulhak in Central Pentecost. She said her grandfather on her mother’s side was a fisherman who spent much of his time at sea in a canoe, which helped develop her strong connection to the ocean. “I am 48 years old, so I thought it was about time to do something for my country,” she said. “I love my country and I want to see it move forward and provide a service. If not now, then when should I do it?” Bulebanne said she wanted to accomplish something meaningful during her lifetime and decided to invest in a vessel when she realised she could afford to purchase one. The vessel, named LADY VAILLANT, is expected to arrive in Vanuatu next Wednesday or Thursday, with Luganville to serve as its home port. According to Bulebanne, the vessel is expected to begin operating in the second week of September. She explained that the name LADY VAILLANT represents “a strong lady in front of danger”, reflecting the strength and determination behind the investment. Bulebanne also encouraged other ni-Vanuatu people to use their abilities and opportunities to contribute to their families, communities and country. “If you can do something for your family, community or country now, then do it to justify your identity as a ni-Vanuatu,” she said. She encouraged people to choose a field they are passionate about, saying this would help them succeed and remain comfortable when facing challenges. Bulebanne also expressed appreciation to the people and families of Vanuatu for their support, saying the investment began as a personal lifelong challenge that she had set for herself. “I never thought I would be in the media. It was a personal lifetime challenge and target that I set for myself, but now you guys have made it public and shown your appreciation,” she said. Labour Dep’t weighs review of long-term PALM participation over impact on children August 28, 2026 Vanuatu’s Labour Department is weighing a review of long-term participation in Australia’s Pacific Australia Labour Mobility (PALM) scheme, with the impact of workers’ prolonged absence on children and families emerging as a key concern. Commissioner of Labour (COL) James Willie said the department is currently holding internal discussions on the social impacts of labour mobility, particularly where parents remain overseas for extended periods while their children stay in Vanuatu. He said any future review of the programme needs to consider the experiences and concerns of children and families affected by long-term labour mobility. “We are now having in-house discussions,” Willie said. “The Department of Labour thinks we should focus on children and the impact on them.” Willie said the department is looking at long-term PALM workers, as well as workers who leave Vanuatu when they already have children or whose children are born while they are overseas. “There are some thoughts about reviewing long-term PALM participation, especially because we see the impact that a parent’s prolonged absence can have,” he said. The COL said the department was looking at two particular areas: long-term PALM participation and the circumstances of workers who leave Vanuatu while they have young children. He said the discussions are still at an internal stage and no final decision has been made to reduce or end participation in the programme. The Commissioner said the department intends to take the issue to a wider group and gather evidence and views from stakeholders, including the Child Desk, chiefs, churches and women’s organisations. “If there is anything to review, it is the impact on children,” he said. “What does the Child Desk register with us? What are the concerns of the Council of Women? We need to hear from them.” Willie said consultation with chiefs and churches would also form part of the department’s consideration as it examines the longer-term social impacts of labour mobility. The proposed review comes as Vanuatu continues to send workers to Australia and New Zealand through labour mobility programmes. Willie said the Government is also working on broader reforms to the country’s labour mobility framework, including a proposed replacement for the existing Seasonal Employment Act. He said the proposed legislation would seek to address some of the social issues that have emerged over the years of labour mobility. Among the measures being considered is a child support plan intended to address the impact of labour mobility on families. The Commissioner said the department would continue discussions and consultations before any review is taken forward. “The review, especially of long-term participation, is a priority when it comes to children,” Willie said.
