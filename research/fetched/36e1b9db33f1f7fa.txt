The representatives said that each participant at the venues will be welcomed with a ‘tilak’. The groups alleged that several garba programmes had turned into platforms of “obscenity and vulgarity,” disrespecting the sanctity of the festival. Vishwa Hindu Parishad’s district president Shankar Lal Gupta said complaints were earlier received about inappropriate behaviour and indecency at certain garba nights organized in lawns, hotels, and private venues in the past. They warned, if authorities fail to act, VHP members themselves will intervene to stop such programmes. VHP and Bajrang Dal leaders announced that in Jagdalpur, garba celebrations should begin only after applying tilak to participants and reciting the Hanuman Chalisa and devotional bhajans. “This is Navratri, a time of devotion. We will not tolerate vulgarity or distortion of culture in the name of garba,” Munna Kori of Bajrang Dal of Jagdalpur told TOI. Tickets for some of the upcoming garba events were reportedly cancelled, and written objections were submitted by local committees, said Kori, adding that at least three planned garba nights have already been called off following the controversy, he added. A police official said that a memorandum has been submitted by the organizations and deployment of cops at the venue will be ensured to prevent any law and order situation, besides continuous patrolling. The groups have also urged the administration and municipal authorities to impose restrictions on the sale of liquor, chicken, and fish in the city during the festival days, or shift such vendors to other areas. They argued that the presence of meat and alcohol stalls near temples and pandals undermines the religious spirit of Navratri. A poster displaying ‘Varaha avtar’ has been circulated among organizers to put it up at the venue, claiming it is a Hindu festival, the members of VHP and Bajrang Dal said.
