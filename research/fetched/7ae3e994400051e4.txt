<a class="cat-post-title" href="https://www.sista.com.vu/local-businesses-look-to-vanuatu-made-show-to-connect-producers-with-buyers/" rel="bookmark">Local Businesses Look to Vanuatu Made Show to Connect Producers with Buyers</a>
<span class="cat-post-date post-date">September 29, 2026</span>
<p class="cpwp-excerpt-text">The eighth edition of the Vanuatu Made Trade Show will bring together local producers, businesses and potential buyers in Port Vila next week, under the theme “Weaving Pride in Vanuatu”. The two-day trade show will be held from 29 to 30 September 2026 at Independence Park, giving local producers an opportunity to showcase Vanuatu-made products and explore new domestic, regional and international markets. Ahead of the main event, local producers gathered at the Melanesian Hotel this week for a business-to-business engagement organised as part of the Vanuatu Made programme. The one-day activity allowed producers to pitch their businesses and products to potential buyers, network with other local businesses and learn more about what buyers are looking for. Phalguni James, Vanuatu Chamber of Commerce and Industry (VCCI) Manager for the Export Ready Programme, said the organisation was pleased to facilitate the workshop. She said the session focused on preparing local businesses to engage more effectively with potential buyers and understand the types of questions they may face when presenting their products. “The business-to-business event is just a one-day event happening this week, but it has been a really great one,” James said. She said the workshop also allowed producers to interact, network and learn more about each other&#8217;s products, while potential buyers had the opportunity to view locally produced goods. James said VCCI wanted producers to be prepared not only for retail customers but also to develop longer-term business relationships with buyers. She said follow-up activities would be undertaken in the coming months to assess the impact of the workshop and the business connections established through the event. Many locally produced products are currently sold within Vanuatu, particularly through handicraft markets, tourism businesses and other local outlets. However, the Vanuatu Made Trade Show is intended to help producers expand beyond these existing markets and establish relationships with buyers from the region and overseas. One of the producers participating this year is Ericka Mabon, Support Officer at the TAFEA Handicraft Market on Tanna. Mabon said the TAFEA Handicraft Market does not produce the goods itself but sells products supplied by local producers from communities across Tanna and the outer islands of TAFEA. She said the organisation works with associations of mothers across Tanna to support them in producing and selling their goods. This is Mabon’s first year participating in the Vanuatu Made Trade Show. She said the recent VCCI workshop had helped her better understand what customers look for when purchasing products and how presentation can influence a customer&#8217;s interest. She said some of the products sourced through the TAFEA network are already being exported to international markets, providing an opportunity for producers to learn what is required to reach overseas customers. Mabon said she planned to take the lessons from the workshop back to the producers and mothers in TAFEA so they could improve their products and better target international markets. She described the Vanuatu Made Trade Show as an important opportunity for producers from across the country to come together and showcase locally made products. Mabon encouraged members of the public to attend the trade show and support local producers, while also encouraging students to visit the exhibition and learn from the businesses and producers taking part. Another participant is Lindon Liu, representing Tanmaeto Products from Maewo in PENAMA Province. Liu said the business produces a range of fruit jams, including around 17 varieties, as well as root crop and fruit powders. He said most of the business&#8217;s customers are currently local, particularly in PENAMA, although products are also sent to Port Vila when customers request them. Liu said the business-to-business workshop had given him a better understanding of how such engagements work and helped him improve his knowledge of marketing and communicating with potential customers. He said one of the main priorities for the business was finding wider markets, increasing the number of buyers and securing opportunities to have its products stocked in existing shops. He also said establishing networks with other local businesses would help the company expand. However, transportation remains a major challenge for producers based outside Efate. Liu said getting products from Maewo to Santo and Port Vila can be difficult, particularly when volcanic activity on Ambae affects flights or marine weather warnings prevent ships from travelling. He said reliable transportation was therefore an important factor in helping businesses from the outer islands access larger markets. Sherolyn Galomule, owner of Sherolyn&#8217;s Fancylicious Cakes, is another local producer preparing to participate in this year&#8217;s trade show. Galomule, who is originally from Ambae and married to Tanna, specialises in cakes and desserts. One of her products is “Pretty Mix”, a chocolate cake product packaged in a container with a spoon, using locally produced Vanuatu cocoa and mixed fruits. The product is available in different varieties and is one of her business&#8217;s specialities. Galomule said one of the key lessons from the VCCI workshop was learning how to negotiate and communicate more effectively with customers and potential buyers. She said the training helped her understand how to start conversations with buyers, discuss prices and negotiate business arrangements. Galomule said she sometimes found herself simply agreeing when customers requested something, but the workshop helped her understand how to handle difficult conversations and negotiate more confidently. She said she hopes to use the Vanuatu Made Trade Show to meet new customers and establish future business relationships. While she already has regular customers, Galomule said she was particularly interested in connecting with cafés, hotels and other businesses that could become future buyers of her products. The 2026 Vanuatu Made Trade Show will feature returning participants and new local producers, with the event providing a platform to showcase locally produced goods and explore potential partnerships and new markets. The trade show will take place on 29 and 30 September at Independence Park in Port Vila. SOURCE: VANUATU DAILY POST</p></div></div><div></div>

<a class="cat-post-title" href="https://www.sista.com.vu/partnership-to-develop-next-generation-of-pacific-media-leaders/" rel="bookmark">Partnership to develop next generation of Pacific media leaders</a>
<span class="cat-post-date post-date">September 29, 2026</span>
<p class="cpwp-excerpt-text">A partnership was signed at the 8th Pacific Media Summit for the establishment of a Pacific Centre for Communications and Journalism in Fiji to develop the next generation of Pacific media leaders. Vice-Chancellor of the Fiji National University (FNU), Professor Unaisi Nabobo-Baba, and the President of the Fiji Media Association (FMA), Rosi Tamani-Doviverata, signed the partnership in the presence of media leaders, journalists, communication specialists and development partners. Fiji’s Prime Minister (PM) Sitiveni Rabuka said the Centre can provide a platform for professional training, research, regional cooperation and the development of the next generation of Pacific media leaders. It can also help strengthen digital journalism, media literacy and the responsible use of emerging technologies, including artificial intelligence, he said. “Our objective should be clear. We want a media sector that is independent, professional, credit and trusted. A sector that can adapt to technological change while maintaining the standards that give journalism its value.” FMA President Tamani-Doviverata acknowledged the university for the opportunity to partner and said that it will benefit Fiji and other Pacific countries. “The Pacific media is changing faster than it had at any point. The people best placed to say what a journalist needs to know are the people doing the job every day. “That is what makes this different. “The industry is not a guest in the program. It is a founding partner. Its National Media Association sits at the head of the table shaping its causes and putting working editors in front of the class. And it is built for the whole region,” she said. “For years when news rooms in one of our smaller Pacific nations needed an experienced journalist, the answer was often to recruit from Fiji. So, here’s a better offer. Don’t only hire our journalists. Send us yours. Come and train with us. Come and grow with us. “Fiji is ready to lead the way. And we would rather lead together than alone. And none of this would have been possible without unity. This partnership exists because newsrooms that compete for the same stories every single day still choose to sit at one table as an association. So, to my fellow national media associations across the Pacific, a friendly challenge: We will always achieve more together. “To our development partners, sponsor a cadet, sponsor a seat in that classroom. For a journalist for a country that cannot afford to send one, it cost no more than another workshop and it lasts a career.” Vice-Chancellor Nabobo-Baba assured that the university will be used to do good for the people in the region. President of the Media Association of Vanuatu (MAV), Charles Sumbe, congratulated FMA on the initiative but said it should not prevent Vanuatu from developing a similar initiative to help train and grow its own journalists at home. SOURCE: VANUATU DAILY POST</p></div></div><div></div>

<a class="cat-post-title" href="https://www.sista.com.vu/vanuatus-mary-jack-wins-global-diversity-and-inclusion-award/" rel="bookmark">Vanuatu’s Mary Jack wins global diversity and inclusion award</a>
<span class="cat-post-date post-date">September 29, 2026</span>
<p class="cpwp-excerpt-text">Mary Jack, founder and Board Chair of the Mitingar Women and Girls Association, has been named the ‘Diversity and Inclusion Champion’ at the inaugural Spicy Awards 2026, held this week in Nairobi, Kenya. The international awards recognise grassroots leaders, activists and innovators from around the world. They are based on the core values of SPICE &#8211; Strength, Purpose, Inclusivity, Creativism and Equity. Unable to travel to Nairobi for the ceremony, Jack delivered a video address to the event, speaking about the challenges faced by ni-Vanuatu women and the work being done through Mitingar. “I am greatly honoured to be nominated for the inaugural Spicy Award ‘Diversity and Inclusion Champion’,” Jack said in her address. “For 40 years, I have been tirelessly dedicated to uplifting the grassroots women of Vanuatu. In our island nation in the Pacific, the women face many hardships&#8230; But we are strong and resilient and determined.” Jack founded Mitingar in 2020, explaining that the organisation was established because “we decided we could no longer wait for help to arrive from outside, and we started to build our own national grassroots women’s representative body.” Mitingar works to connect women across Vanuatu’s 65 remote inhabited islands, supporting women through social leadership development, project building, community-led disaster preparedness and advocacy. Jack thanked the Spicy Awards founders and congratulating them for their work in promoting diversity and inclusion. The award follows Jack’s visit to Australia earlier this year, where she spoke about the experiences of ni-Vanuatu women to federal ministers at Parliament House and at events including Women Deliver and Climate Action Week Sydney. The Spicy Award recognises Jack’s four decades of work with grassroots women in Vanuatu. SOURCE: VANUATU DAILY POST</p></div></div><div></div>

<a class="cat-post-title" href="https://www.sista.com.vu/vanuatu-prepares-pledge-to-end-violence-against-children/" rel="bookmark">Vanuatu prepares pledge to end violence against children</a>
<span class="cat-post-date post-date">September 29, 2026</span>
<p class="cpwp-excerpt-text">Vanuatu is preparing a national pledge with specific actions and targets to strengthen protection against violence towards children, following a national consultation held in Port Vila last Thursday. The half-day National Preparatory Consultation for the Second Global Ministerial Conference on Ending Violence Against Children was held at the Warwick Hotel on 24 September 2026, bringing together government representatives and key stakeholders to review progress, identify gaps and agree on priority areas. The consultation forms part of Vanuatu’s preparations for the Second Global Ministerial Conference on Ending Violence Against Children, which will be held in Manila, Philippines, from 18 to 19 November 2026. The conference is being hosted by the Government of the Philippines together with the World Health Organization (WHO) and UNICEF. Vanuatu and the Federated States of Micronesia (FSM) have been selected to represent Oceania at the conference. Vanuatu will have an opportunity to share its national experience, challenges and commitments while contributing to international efforts to prevent and end violence against children. The global conference will bring together ministers, senior government officials, United Nations agencies and representatives of children, youth, civil society and other partners to advance prevention and strengthen national commitments to protecting children. During the Port Vila consultation, participants reviewed Vanuatu’s national story, including progress in strengthening the child protection system, available evidence on violence against children and areas where further action is required. A key discussion, “Who Are We Still Not Reaching?”, encouraged participants to examine children, communities, locations and forms of violence that may remain less visible or underserved by existing protection and response mechanisms. Vanuatu’s representative will present the National Pledge at the Manila conference and reaffirm the Government’s commitment to translating it into practical measures over the following two years, with the aim of achieving agreed national targets by 2028. The consultation also reinforced that preventing violence against children requires coordinated efforts across government ministries, provincial authorities, police, health and education services, communities, churches, civil society, development partners, families and children themselves. Through the national consultation and preparations for the Manila conference, Vanuatu is working to identify priority areas and strengthen a child protection system that reaches children across the country. SOURCE: VANUATU DAILY POST</p></div></div><div></div>

<a class="cat-post-title" href="https://www.sista.com.vu/former-pikinini-playtime-teacher-launches-school-to-tackle-torbas-literacy-challenge/" rel="bookmark">Former Pikinini Playtime teacher launches school to tackle TORBA’s literacy challenge</a>
<span class="cat-post-date post-date">September 17, 2026</span>
<p class="cpwp-excerpt-text">A former Pikinini Playtime teacher with more than 11 years of experience in early childhood education has opened a private school at the TORBA provincial headquarters in Sola, with the goal of helping address the province’s low literacy rate. Little Footprints founder and education entrepreneur Florence Nalet said her years of working with children, combined with the literacy challenges she observed in TORBA, inspired her to start the school. Originally from Malekula, Nalet said she saw a need to support children and communities in TORBA. “I saw that the literacy of TORBA Province is very low, so that’s why I came to help the communities around TORBA Province in terms of literacy,” she said. TORBA has been identified by the Ministry of Education and Training (MoET) as having some of the lowest adult literacy and language proficiency indices in the country, prompting specialised regional adult training programmes. Nalet said Little Footprints aims to help address the challenge by starting with children at an early age. The school provides childcare for children from six months of age, as well as kindergarten and Class 1 education. She said its vision is to provide a safe environment while giving children a strong foundation for future learning. “The foundation of learning starts when they are still children,” Nalet said. Little Footprints currently has over 30 children enrolled across its different levels. About 18 are in childcare, around 13 are in kindergarten, and another four to five are attending Class 1. However, Nalet said establishing the school was not easy, particularly because private early childhood education was a new concept for some communities in TORBA. She said it initially faced backlash from community members who did not fully understand its purpose and goals. “Since it’s a really new thing to establish in TORBA Province, the challenge I faced was receiving backlash from the community since they still had zero knowledge about the school and its exact purpose,” she said. Nalet said the community response gradually changed as parents began seeing the benefits and the development of their children. “Once they know how a child is developing, like its quality, they start getting their kids to school,” she said. She believes the growing acceptance demonstrates the importance of early childhood education in preparing children for future learning. Beyond educating children, Little Footprints also aims to strengthen teachers through training and professional development. Nalet said it hopes to improve teachers’ knowledge and skills so they can become resourceful members of their communities. It currently has one administrator, one chef, one childcare teacher, two kindergarten teachers and one Class 1 teacher. Healthy food is also part of its approach to childcare, with a chef employed to prepare meals for the children. Despite its progress, Nalet said the school continues to face challenges, including limited space and suitable buildings to accommodate the children and provide facilities such as a playground. Little Footprints has also recently established a small stationery and printing service. Nalet said accessing stationery and other school supplies in TORBA can be difficult, so the service was created to support both the school and the wider community. The school operates privately and has not received funding from the Government or any organisation. Nalet said she hopes to see greater cooperation between the school, community and Government as Little Footprints works towards its goal of improving literacy in TORBA. For Nalet, establishing the school represents a new chapter after more than a decade working in early childhood education. “To start off something is really difficult, but once you started and things go according to your plans and goals, and you worked hard for it, you can achieve it,” she said. She also thanked parents for trusting the school and enrolling their children. Nalet said her long-term goal is to work in partnership with the people of TORBA to improve children’s access to education and help raise literacy levels across the province. “We established here to work together as partnership to help our children, for their learning and to fight the literacy rate which is very low,” she said. SOURCE: VANUATU DAILY POST</p></div></div><div></div>
