<p id="mwCQ">Lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender, and queer (LGBTQ) people in <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinea" title="Guinea" id="mwCg">Guinea</a> face legal challenges not experienced by non-<a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ" title="LGBTQ" class="mw-redirect" id="mwCw">LGBTQ</a> residents. Both male and female expressions of same-sex sexual activity are illegal in Guinea, and discriminatory attitudes towards LGBTQ people are generally tolerated in the nation.<sup about="#mwt7" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-1" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-1"}}'><a href="#cite_note-1" id="mwDA"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwDQ"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwDg">[</span>1<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwDw">]</span></span></a></sup></p>

<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" id="mwEA" data-mw='{"autoGenerated":true}'/></section><section data-mw-section-id="1" id="mwEQ" aria-labelledby="Law_regarding_same-sex_sexual_activity"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="Law_regarding_same-sex_sexual_activity">Law regarding same-sex sexual activity</h2><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=LGBTQ_rights_in_Guinea&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Law regarding same-sex sexual activity"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<section data-mw-section-id="2" id="mwEg" aria-labelledby="1998_Penal_Code"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading3"><h3 id="1998_Penal_Code">1998 Penal Code</h3><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=LGBTQ_rights_in_Guinea&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: 1998 Penal Code"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p id="mwEw">The 1998 Guinean Penal Code provides as follows:<sup about="#mwt10" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-2" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-2"}}'><a href="#cite_note-2" id="mwFA"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwFQ"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwFg">[</span>2<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwFw">]</span></span></a></sup></p>

<ul id="mwGA"><li id="mwGQ">Article 325.</li></ul>
<blockquote id="mwGg"><p id="mwGw">Any indecent act or act against nature committed with an individual of the same sex will be punished by imprisonment of six months to three years and a fine of 100,000 to 1,000,000 <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinean_franc" title="Guinean franc" id="mwHA">Guinean francs</a>. If the act was committed with a minor under 21 years of age, the maximum sentence must be pronounced. If the act was consummated or attempted with violence or attempted violence, the guilty person will be condemned to five to ten years of imprisonment.</p></blockquote>

<ul id="mwHQ"><li id="mwHg">Article 326.</li></ul>
<blockquote id="mwHw"><p id="mwIA">A public indecency is defined as any intentional act committed publicly and likely to offend the decency and the moral sentiments of those who are its inadvertent witnesses.</p></blockquote>

<ul id="mwIQ"><li id="mwIg">Article 327.</li></ul>
<blockquote id="mwIw"><p id="mwJA">Any person that has committed a public indecency will be punished by three months to two years of imprisonment and a fine of 50,000 to 450,000 Guinean francs or simply by one of these two punishments.</p></blockquote>

<blockquote id="mwJQ"><p id="mwJg">When an indecent act is committed by a group of individuals, the penalties described in the first paragraph of the current article will be doubled.</p></blockquote>
</section><section data-mw-section-id="3" id="mwJw" aria-labelledby="2016_Penal_Code"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading3"><h3 id="2016_Penal_Code">2016 Penal Code</h3><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=LGBTQ_rights_in_Guinea&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: 2016 Penal Code"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p id="mwKA">The Guinean Penal Code was revised in 2016, but homosexuality remains criminalized. The minimum fine is greater comparer to the 1998 Penal Code. Article 274 of the 2016 Penal Code allows same-sex sexual conduct to be punished by either six months to three years in prison, a fine of 500,000 to 1,000,000 Guinean francs, or both.<sup about="#mwt13" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-3" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-3"}}'><a href="#cite_note-3" id="mwKQ"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwKg"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwKw">[</span>3<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwLA">]</span></span></a></sup></p>

</section><section data-mw-section-id="4" id="mwLQ" aria-labelledby="Enforcement"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading3"><h3 id="Enforcement">Enforcement</h3><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=LGBTQ_rights_in_Guinea&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Enforcement"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p id="mwLg">These laws are used to justify arrests, and police officers will normally try to judge if a person is LGBTQ+ based on their clothing and appearance. Arrests rarely result in formal convictions since arrestees pay for their release. For years, police in Guinea have used the law as a means of <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extortion" title="Extortion" id="mwLw">extortion</a>. In 2016 and 2017, the LGBTQ+ organization Afrique Arc-en-Ciel Conakry reported small numbers of gay men being taken to jail being fined for their release. A 2024 <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Periodic_Review" title="Universal Periodic Review" id="mwMA">Universal Periodic Review</a> stakeholder submission describes that LGBTQ+ people are held in custody for up to five days despite the 48 hour legal limit. They typically pay bribes or fines to escape to avoid stigma during legal proceedings.<sup about="#mwt16" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-4" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-4"}}'><a href="#cite_note-4" id="mwMQ"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwMg"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwMw">[</span>4<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwNA">]</span></span></a></sup><sup about="#mwt19" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-5" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-5"}}'><a href="#cite_note-5" id="mwNQ"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwNg"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwNw">[</span>5<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwOA">]</span></span></a></sup></p>

<p id="mwOQ">In 2023, The Office for the Protection of Women, Children, and Morals added a unit to invesitage same-sex sexual conduct and other perceived moral offenses.<sup about="#mwt22" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-:0_6-0" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{"name":":0"},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-:0-6"}}'><a href="#cite_note-:0-6" id="mwOg"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwOw"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwPA">[</span>6<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwPQ">]</span></span></a></sup></p>

</section></section><section data-mw-section-id="5" id="mwPg" aria-labelledby="Recognition_of_same-sex_unions"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="Recognition_of_same-sex_unions">Recognition of same-sex unions</h2><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=LGBTQ_rights_in_Guinea&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Recognition of same-sex unions"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>

<p id="mwPw">There is no recognition of same-sex unions.</p>

</section><section data-mw-section-id="6" id="mwQA" aria-labelledby="Adoption_and_family_planning"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="Adoption_and_family_planning">Adoption and family planning</h2><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=LGBTQ_rights_in_Guinea&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Adoption and family planning"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p id="mwQQ">A couple married for a minimum of five years or an unmarried person who is at least 30 years of age is eligible to adopt a Guinean child if there is at least 15 years between the age of the child and the age of the adopting parent. Guinean law does not specifically make LGBTQ persons ineligible to adopt.<sup about="#mwt26" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-7" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-7"}}'><a href="#cite_note-7" id="mwQg"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwQw"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwRA">[</span>7<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwRQ">]</span></span></a></sup></p>

</section><section data-mw-section-id="7" id="mwRg" aria-labelledby="Discrimination_protections"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="Discrimination_protections">Discrimination protections</h2><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=LGBTQ_rights_in_Guinea&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Discrimination protections"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p id="mwRw">There are no laws protecting LGBTQ people from discrimination in areas such as health, employment, and access to goods and services. However, there are other limited legal provisions that protect sexual orientation and sexual minorities.</p>

<ul id="mwSA"><li id="mwSQ">Law L/2016/037/AN Relating to Cybersecurity and the Protection of Personal Data in the Republic of Guinea, protects <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_orientation" title="Sexual orientation" id="mwSg">sexual orientation</a> and sexual life as personal and sensitive data. Article 56 states: "Whoever, outside the cases provided for by this law, places or stores on a computer medium or memory, without the express consent of the person concerned, personal data which, directly or indirectly, reveal the origin, race, ethnicity, political, philosophical or religious opinions, or trade union membership, or which relate to the health or sexual orientation of the person concerned or to any other information inherent to them and of a sensitive or strictly personal nature, shall be punished by imprisonment of one (1) year to seven (7) years and a fine of 30,000,000 to 150,000,000 Guinean Francs or one of these two penalties only."<sup about="#mwt29" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-8" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-8"}}'><a href="#cite_note-8" id="mwSw"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwTA"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwTQ">[</span>8<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwTg">]</span></span></a></sup></li>
<li id="mwTw">Law L/2024/001/CNT/SGG of January 12, 2024, Relating to HIV Prevention, Care, and Protection of people living with HIV defines "Members of sexual minorities" as "individuals whose sexual orientation or identity falls outside the framework of heterosexuality, such as gays, lesbians, bisexuals, <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersex" title="Intersex" id="mwUA">intersex</a>, and transgender people, whether they identify as such or not." Also, it states that <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transgender" title="Transgender" id="mwUQ">transgender</a> is a "person whose sexual identity and expression do not conform to their birth sex and traditionally accepted norms."<sup about="#mwt33" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-hiv_law_2024_9-0" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{"name":"hiv law 2024"},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-hiv_law_2024-9"}}'><a href="#cite_note-hiv_law_2024-9" id="mwUg"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwUw"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwVA">[</span>9<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwVQ">]</span></span></a></sup></li></ul>

<p id="mwVg">Section 9.9.30(b) of the Guinea Aeronautical Regulations (2022) issued by the Ministry of Civil Aviation states that the competent government agencies will not use Passenger Name Record Data that reveals an individual's racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious or philosophical beliefs, trade union membership, or data concerning their health, sex life, or sexual orientation.<sup about="#mwt36" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-Aeronautical_Regulations_2022_10-0" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{"name":"Aeronautical Regulations 2022"},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-Aeronautical_Regulations_2022-10"}}'><a href="#cite_note-Aeronautical_Regulations_2022-10" id="mwVw"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwWA"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwWQ">[</span>10<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwWg">]</span></span></a></sup></p>

<p id="mwWw">Ordinance No. 056/2009/PRG/SGG on the Prevention, Care, and Control of HIV/AIDS in the Republic of Guinea states that sexual minorities are individuals whose sexual orientation does not correspond to the heterosexual framework, such as men who have sex with men, lesbians, bisexuals, and intersex people and includes them as vulnerable and marginalized groups.<sup about="#mwt39" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-11" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-11"}}'><a href="#cite_note-11" id="mwXA"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwXQ"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwXg">[</span>11<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwXw">]</span></span></a></sup></p>

</section><section data-mw-section-id="8" id="mwYA" aria-labelledby="Living_conditions"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="Living_conditions">Living conditions</h2><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=LGBTQ_rights_in_Guinea&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Living conditions"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p id="mwYQ">The <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Department_of_State" title="U.S. Department of State" class="mw-redirect" id="mwYg">U.S. Department of State</a>'s 2011 Human Rights Report found that there were no LGBTQ+ organizations in the country. Furthermore, homosexuality was largely perceived as socially, religiously, and culturally taboo. Homosexuality was disliked politically as well. In 2010, Prime Minister <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lansana_Kouyaté" title="Lansana Kouyaté" id="mwYw">Lansana Kouyate</a> said during the opening of the Office of the High Commissioner for Human Rights in Conakry that he believed consentual same-sex sexual activity was wrong and should be illegal. He went on to say that sexual orientation should not be considered a human right.<sup about="#mwt42" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-12" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-12"}}'><a href="#cite_note-12" id="mwZA"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwZQ"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwZg">[</span>12<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwZw">]</span></span></a></sup></p>

<p id="mwaA">The more recent 2023 Human Rights Report found that country did have groups representing LGBTQ+ people. The groups were unable to obtain legal recognition despite there being no law against such an organization being recognized. Cultural stigma persists and most people who are LGBTQ+ do not openly disclose that. Subsequently, there is no substantial political presence of openly LGBTQ+ people. LGBTQ+ people face discrimination when seeking housing, education, healthcare, or employment.<sup about="#mwt43" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-:0_6-1" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{"name":":0"}}'><a href="#cite_note-:0-6" id="mwaQ"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwag"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwaw">[</span>6<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwbA">]</span></span></a></sup></p> 

<p id="mwbQ">Public sentiment towards LGBTQ+ people is largely negative. A survey taken from 2021 to 2023 found that 87 percent of Guineans would either dislike or stongly dislike having a gay neighbor. LGBTQ+ people are seen as part of a Western <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperialism" title="Imperialism" id="mwbg">imperalist</a> agenda by the United States and France. Religious, cultural, and familial is pressure used in an attempt to <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conversion_therapy" title="Conversion therapy" id="mwbw">convert</a> LGBTQ+ people. This includes families arranging forced heterosexual marriages.<sup about="#mwt46" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-13" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-13"}}'><a href="#cite_note-13" id="mwcA"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwcQ"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwcg">[</span>13<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwcw">]</span></span></a></sup><sup about="#mwt47" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-:0_6-2" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{"name":":0"}}'><a href="#cite_note-:0-6" id="mwdA"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwdQ"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwdg">[</span>6<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwdw">]</span></span></a></sup><sup about="#mwt50" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-14" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-14"}}'><a href="#cite_note-14" id="mweA"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mweQ"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mweg">[</span>14<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwew">]</span></span></a></sup></p>

<p id="mwfA">Security forces have arrested, harassed and been violent towards LGBTQ+ people, accusing them of disrupting the social order. Incarcerated LGBTQ+ people are frequently abused. Transgender women are incarcerated under the pretext of protecting them from community violence. They would then be put in male prisons where they often experienced sexual violence. LGBTQ+ Guineans have been <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internally_displaced_person" title="Internally displaced person" id="mwfQ">internally displaced.</a>They leave to avoid areas with religious and cultural norms that put their lives and well-being in danger.<sup about="#mwt51" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-:0_6-3" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{"name":":0"}}'><a href="#cite_note-:0-6" id="mwfg"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwfw"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwgA">[</span>6<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwgQ">]</span></span></a></sup></p>
