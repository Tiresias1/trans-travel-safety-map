04:22:56 AM Proposed Bangsamoro Shari’ah Justice System Code seeks broader legal framework for Shari’ah justice July 14, 2026 Bangsamoro Parliament Session | Bangsamoro Parliament Session | Features | News | Press Release | Publications | Recent News COTABATO CITY — A proposed measure in the Bangsamoro Parliament seeks to establish the Bangsamoro Shari’ah Justice System Code, a comprehensive legal framework aimed at strengthening the administration of the Shari’ah justice system in the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region. Parliament Bill No. 473 seeks to provide legal rules governing Shari’ah civil actions, Islamic commercial transactions, supplementary Muslim family relations, ta’zir offenses, alternative dispute resolution (ADR), Agama Arbitration Council proceedings, and other matters necessary for the effective administration and development of the Bangsamoro Shari’ah justice system. The measure, authored by Members of the Parliament Romeo Sema, Abdulkarim Misuari, Randolph Parcasio, Saripuddin Jikiri, and Nurredha Misuari, is anchored on Article X of Republic Act No. 11054, or the Bangsamoro Organic Law (BOL), which authorizes the Bangsamoro Parliament to enact laws on Shari’ah involving personal, family, property, commercial, civil, and criminal matters, consistent with the Constitution and the national legal system. In the bill’s explanatory note, Sema said Presidential Decree No. 1083, or the Code of Muslim Personal Laws of the Philippines, has served as the principal legal framework governing Muslim personal laws on marriage, divorce, succession, family relations, and related matters for more than four decades. However, he said the decree does not comprehensively address Shari’ah civil actions, Islamic commercial transactions, supplementary Muslim family relations, ta’zir offenses, and institutionalized Shari’ah-based alternative dispute resolution mechanisms. Sema noted that since the enactment of PD 1083 in 1977, the development of Shari’ah law within the Philippine legal system has remained largely confined to personal status and family relations despite the growing legal, commercial, and social needs of Muslim communities. He said the enactment of the Bangsamoro Organic Law in 2018 gave the Bangsamoro Parliament the authority to supplement and develop Muslim personal laws and enact legislation governing Shari’ah civil actions, commercial transactions, property relations, alternative dispute resolution, and ta’zir offenses, providing the legal foundation for a more responsive and comprehensive Bangsamoro Shari’ah justice system. The proposed code is organized into 10 books covering general provisions, the jurisdiction of Shari’ah courts, Shari’ah civil actions and commercial relations, supplementary Muslim family relations, ta’zir offenses, alternative dispute resolution, Sulh and Agama Arbitration Council proceedings, procedural safeguards, and administrative and final provisions. Under the bill, the proposed code will primarily apply to Muslims within BARMM. However, non-Muslims and juridical entities may also voluntarily submit to the jurisdiction of Shari’ah courts or other mechanisms established under the code through written agreements, contracts, or other lawful expressions of consent. The measure recognizes the Hanafi, Hanbali, Maliki, and Shafi’i schools of Islamic jurisprudence in interpreting the proposed code. Where these schools differ, interpretations should be guided by the Constitution, the Bangsamoro Organic Law, Presidential Decree No. 1083, public policy, justice, and the objectives of Shari’ah. The proposed measure expands the jurisdiction of Shari’ah Circuit and District Courts over civil disputes involving marriage, divorce, communal property, inheritance, custody, guardianship, Shari’ah-compliant contracts, certain real property disputes, commercial transactions, and ta’zir offenses enacted by the Bangsamoro Parliament within the limits authorized by law. The bill also establishes Shari’ah civil causes of action, including claims involving breaches of Shari’ah-compliant agreements, breaches of Sulh agreements, fraudulent Halal representation, abuse of amanah or fiduciary obligations, and disputes arising from Shari’ah-compliant commercial contracts and transactions. The proposed code introduces supplementary provisions on Muslim family relations not expressly provided under PD 1083, including the recognition of Mubara’at, or divorce by mutual agreement. It likewise seeks to strengthen legal protections governing Islamic commercial transactions by promoting fairness, transparency, and accountability consistent with the Islamic principle of hisbah. Another feature of the measure is the institutionalization of Shari’ah-based alternative dispute resolution mechanisms. It promotes sulh, mediation, conciliation, reconciliation, arbitration, and proceedings before the Agama Arbitration Council as culturally appropriate and faith-sensitive means of resolving disputes. Sema said that these mechanisms are intended to promote restorative justice, preserve family and community harmony, encourage the peaceful settlement of disputes, reduce unnecessary litigation, and provide accessible modes of dispute resolution. The measure also requires parties, whenever practicable or allowed by law, to first undergo mediation, Sulh, or customary reconciliation before filing actions under the code. The proposed code emphasizes that its provisions are intended to supplement—not repeal—the Code of Muslim Personal Laws unless expressly provided by law. It also requires that all provisions be interpreted in harmony with the Constitution, Republic Act No. 11054, or the Bangsamoro Organic Law, Presidential Decree No. 1083, the Revised Penal Code, existing national laws, and internationally recognized human rights. The measure further provides for parliamentary oversight of the implementation of the proposed code through the Bangsamoro Parliament committee responsible for justice matters. The authors said the bill seeks to strengthen access to justice, provide greater legal certainty in Shari’ah civil and commercial matters, encourage the peaceful settlement of disputes, and support the continued development of the Bangsamoro Shari’ah justice system within the framework of the Philippine Constitution and national laws.
