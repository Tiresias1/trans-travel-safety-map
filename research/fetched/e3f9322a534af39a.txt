LGBTQ+ Action Plan Supreme Court Judgment update 19 December 2025
LGBTQ+ Action Plan Supreme Court Judgment Update
After the UK Supreme Court judgment in the case of For Women Scotland Ltd v The Scottish Ministers, “woman” in the
Equality Act 2010 refers to someone registered as female at birth.
Similarly, “man” in the Equality Act 2010 refers to someone registered as male at birth.
The judgment also makes clear that the word “sex” in the Equality Act 2010 means a person’s sex registered at birth.
Those definitions also mean the same thing in the LGBTQ+ Action Plan in so far as it engages the Equality Act 2010.
Further amendments to the Plan will be made to bring it into line with the judgment.

About the plan
Many things have improved for LGBTQ+ people. But
some people still face barriers — in the workplace,
education, healthcare, social care, and more.
Some LGBTQ+ people not only face discrimination
for being LGBTQ+ but also because of their age, race,
religion or disability. This makes their lives even more
difficult.
We want to make sure all LGBTQ+ people have their
rights and are safe, no matter who they are.

Involving people in
the plan
The LGBTQ+ Action Plan has been
developed with communities and
organisations across all parts of
Wales.
Our consultation received over
1,300 responses.

This plan will help to:

● improve the lives of LGBTQ+ people
● challenge discrimination and make life fairer
● create a society where LGBTQ+ people are safe
to live and be themselves.

Discrimination — is when someone is treated
differently because of who they are.

Rights — are for everyone, no matter who they
are, where they live or what they believe
in. Adults have rights set out in The
Human Rights Act 1998. Children’s and
young people’s rights are in the United
Nations Convention on the Rights of
the Child (UNCRC).

Human Rights
LGBTQ+ rights should be respected. We don’t want anyone
being hurt, bullied or treated badly because of who they are.

Our actions:
We will make sure everyone knows about human
rights and understands how important they are for
LGBTQ+ people.
We will make sure everyone knows how to
claim their human rights.
We will use our available powers to ban
Conversion Practices.*
We will make sure LGBTQ+ people are listened
to and represented more on forums.
We will involve LGBTQ+ people to help us plan and
improve services.
We will raise awareness and acceptance of
non-binary and intersex people.
We will try to move decisions about Gender
Recognition to the Senedd so we have the power
to do what’s right for Wales.
We will show the world how Wales values and
supports LGBTQ+ communities.
*Conversion Practices — Some groups think it’s possible to
change or ‘cure’ someone from being LGBTQ+ and offer harmful
practices. They can be violent and damaging.

Safety and freedom from discrimination
Lots of things have improved in Wales but some LGBTQ+ people
still feel unsafe. Hate crimes have gone up. This needs to stop!

Our actions:
We will make it easier for LGBTQ+ people to report
hate crimes.
We will continue to invest in projects that stop
hate crime across Wales.
We will do whatever we can to deal LGBTQ+
bullying and hate crimes.
We will work with the Police to improve
relationships with LGBTQ+ communities.
We will deal with violence against women,
domestic abuse and sexual violence (VAWDASV)
among LGBTQ+ communities.
We will make sure homelessness services are
easy to use and meet the needs of LGBTQ+
people.

You should be able to say…
I know what hate
crime is and know
how to report it.

I am listened
to and
respected.

The Police and staff working
in services understand my
needs and support me.

If I face
violence at
home, I can
report it and
get support.

A Nation of Sanctuary
Wales is a Nation of Sanctuary for everyone, no matter where they
come from. We don’t have the power over decisions about asylum
seekers or refugees, the UK Government does. We disagree with many
of the UK Government’s policies because we think they put LGBTQ+
refugees and asylum at risk of harm and abuse. We’ll continue to work
to improve how LGBTQ+ asylum seekers or refugees are treated.

Our actions:
We will make sure LGBTQ+ people claiming asylum
are identified, kept safe and given support.
We will encourage and support LGBTQ+ asylum
housing in Wales.
We will make sure Wales is a Nation of Sanctuary
for LGBTQ+ people.

You should be able to say…
I feel safe
to be me.

I feel welcome in my
community and people
are friendly.

I’m safe from harm
and can build a new
life.

I don’t have
to hide my
relationships or
who I am.

Healthcare, social care, and welfare
Some LGBTQ+ people find it difficult to get health and social care support.
Some feel unsafe or frightened to talk about their needs. This includes LGBTQ+
disabled people and LGBTQ+ older people in care homes.

Our actions:
We will improve health and social care services so they
meet the needs of LGBTQ+ people better. This includes
LGBTQ+ patients and LGBTQ+ staff.
We will make sure maternity and fertility services are
available and easy to use for LGBTQ+ people.
We will make sure our new Mental Health Strategy includes
the needs of LGBTQ+ people.
We will publish and deliver a new HIV Action Plan.

We will reduce barriers for LGBTQ+ people using sexual
health services.
We will review the Gender Identity Development
Pathway for young people in Wales.

You should be able to say…
Services treat me
with respect.

Doctors and other NHS
staff are well trained
and know how to meet
my needs.

I can get
fertility
support to start
a family.

I have support
for my mental
health!

We will keep developing the Wales Gender Service.

We will improve how information is recorded for trans,
non-binary and intersex people’s medical records.

Workplaces
Lots of workplaces and employers across Wales are safe and give
equal opportunities for LGBTQ+ people... but some aren’t.

Our actions:
We will remove personal identifiers
like gender from job applications
and other employment forms.
We will provide LGBTQ+
equalities training to public
sector organisations.
We will encourage employers to
support the LGBTQ+ people who
work for them.

You should be able to say…

I can develop

I feel respected
