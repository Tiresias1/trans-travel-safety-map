            <div property="schema:text" class="content story">        <div property="schema:text"><p>The United Kingdom has warned LGBT+ travellers to Nigeria of the risk of arrest, violence, attacks, blackmail and intimidation, noting that same-sex sexual activity is illegal and can attract up to 14 years in prison.</p>
<p>The warning was contained in the latest travel advice issued by the UK Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) on Nigeria.</p>

<p>The British government said LGBT+ travellers and people perceived to be members of the community could face heightened security and legal risks in the country.</p>
<p>“Same-sex sexual activity is illegal in Nigeria with penalties of up to 14 years in prison,” the FCDO stated.</p>
<p>It further warned that some northern states in Nigeria operate under Sharia law, under which same-sex sexual activity can attract the death penalty.</p>

<p>“Some northern states observe Sharia Law which can prescribe the death penalty for same-sex sexual activity,” the advisory said.</p>
<p>The FCDO also highlighted provisions of Nigeria’s Same Sex Marriage Prohibition Act, which it said provides for lengthy prison sentences for people involved in same-sex marriages.</p>
<p>According to the British government, the law extends beyond those entering same-sex marriages to people who help arrange or conduct such events, as well as those who attend events perceived to be same-sex weddings.</p>
<p>The law also criminalises activities involving gay groups and organisations, including helping to register or organise such groups or publicly promoting same-sex relationships, the FCDO said.</p>
<p>Warning Of Attacks, Blackmail</p>
<p>Beyond the legal restrictions, the UK government warned LGBT+ travellers of social hostility and the possibility of physical attacks and intimidation.</p>
<p>“Same-sex relationships are generally viewed as socially unacceptable in Nigerian society,” it stated.</p>
<p>The FCDO said this social hostility could expose people perceived to be members of the LGBT+ community, or those supporting LGBT+ rights, to increased risks.</p>
<p>“There is an increased risk of violence, attacks and threats, such as blackmail and intimidation against anyone being thought to be part of the LGBT+ community or supporting their rights,” the advisory warned.</p>
<p>Warning Over Network Shutdowns</p>
<p>The UK government also warned travellers that communications could be disrupted without notice, particularly during periods of unrest or when authorities impose restrictions.</p>
<p>“Network operators in Nigeria have in the past been ordered to suspend mobile networks and wi-fi with very little notice,” the FCDO said.</p>
<p>It also warned that Nigerian authorities could impose, amend or lift curfews and restrictions on vehicle movement at short notice.</p>
<p>“The authorities impose, amend and lift curfews and restrictions on the movement of vehicles at short notice throughout Nigeria,” it stated.</p>
<p>The FCDO warned that failure to comply with curfews and movement restrictions could expose travellers to arrest and detention.</p>
<p>“If you fail to observe curfews and restrictions, you could be at significant risk of arrest and detention,” it said.</p>
<p>Travellers were advised to obtain up-to-date information from local authorities or people with local knowledge.</p>
<p>UK Warns Of Protests, Communal Violence</p>
<p>The FCDO also cautioned British nationals about political rallies, protests and violent demonstrations, which it said could occur with little warning.</p>
<p>“Political rallies, protests and violent demonstrations can take place with little notice,” the advisory stated.</p>
<p>It urged travellers to avoid large gatherings and crowded public places and to move away from threatening situations rather than attempting to pass through them.</p>
<p>“If you see a threatening or intimidating situation, do not try to make your way through it. Turn around and move to safety,” the UK government said.</p>
<p>The FCDO said intercommunal violence occurred frequently and without warning in different parts of Nigeria.</p>
<p>“Although foreign nationals are not normally targeted, there is a risk you could be caught in an attack,” it stated.</p>
<p>The British government specifically mentioned Abuja and Lagos, saying widespread demonstrations periodically take place in the two major cities.</p>
<p>It said previous protests had resulted in disruption and violence, with security agencies having used tear gas, rubber bullets and live fire on some occasions.</p>
<p>“Exercise caution when travelling, limit travel after dark, avoid large crowds and gatherings, and monitor local media reporting, as well as UK Travel Advice,” the FCDO advised.</p>
<p>Warning Over Deadly Methanol In Alcohol</p>
<p>The UK government also warned British travellers about deaths and serious illnesses linked to alcoholic drinks containing methanol in Nigeria.</p>
<p>“There have been deaths and cases of serious illness caused by alcoholic drinks containing methanol in Nigeria,” the FCDO stated.</p>
<p>It said Nigerian authorities had attempted to shut down production centres following reported incidents, particularly those producing unlicensed traditional gin.</p>
<p>The UK government advised travellers to avoid unlicensed alcoholic products, warning that methanol could not be detected by taste or smell.</p>
<p>“Avoid drinking unlicensed products,” it said.</p>
<p>“Even small amounts of methanol can kill,” the FCDO warned.</p>
<p>“It is not possible to identify methanol in alcoholic drinks by taste or smell.”</p>
<p>It advised travellers who develop symptoms of methanol poisoning after consuming alcohol to seek urgent medical attention.</p>
<p>“Seek urgent medical attention if you or someone you are travelling with show the signs of methanol poisoning after drinking,” the advisory stated.</p>
