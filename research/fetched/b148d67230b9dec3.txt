Home About Us News & Analysis Publications Reports Annual Reports Webinars Facts & Key Issues Human Rights Society and Conflict Social Climate Change Peru Issues Links Support Us Select Page A Week of Setbacks: Gender, LGBTQ+ and Reproductive Rights Under Attack in Peru Apr 5, 2025 | Gender Peru has seen a series of moves this week aimed at rolling back progress in gender equality, LGBTQ+ rights, and reproductive rights. During a hearing in the congressional commission on science and technology, Congressman Ernesto Bustamante from Fuerza Popular caused outrage by contending that women lack a “biological condition” that would encourage them to involve themselves in science. His remark implied that the gaps in the ratio of women to men in the sciences, compared to other disciplines, is physically predetermined, not the result of systemic discrimination. Feminist organisations have denounced his remark as sexist and backward, highlighting the need to tackle systemic discrimination against women in educational institutions. Meanwhile, Congress approved bills 6505/2023-CR and 8457/2023-CR that, among other things, ban people from using public bathrooms that run counter to the sex they were born with. The bill, put forth by Alejandro Muñante and Jorge Montoya, also bans the use of inclusive signage such as “women in their diversity”. The bill had already been criticised by the Ombudsman’s Office Report N o 002-2023-DP/ADHPD and LGBTQ+ organizations as it incurs transphobic stereotypes that present transgender people as threats. Adding to the already tense environment, Willax TV news journalist Ana María Salcedo harassed Congresswoman Susel Paredes , the first openly lesbian Peruvian congresswoman and a vocal advocate for LGBTQ+ rights, by asking if she “felt like a man” and telling her “go use the men’s room”. Finally, Congresswoman Milagros Aguayo of Renovación Popular continues her campaign to stop the practice of therapeutic abortion in the National Maternal Perinatal Institute (INMP) on the grounds that the institution has been performing “illegal abortions” since 2009. This campaign, backed by the group “Padres Peruanos”, ignores the fact that INMP complies with national law as well as international standards. The protocol of the institute recognises that pregnancy – especially when caused by rape or accompanied by foetal abnormalities – can cause serious physical and psychological damage and thus constitutes grounds for therapeutic abortion.
