No Last Update: March 12, 2026 In the Republic of Cabo Verde, or Cape Verde, same-sex sexual relations are not criminalized. People have employment protections based on their sexual orientation, “and other discriminatory motives,” although the law does not explicitly reference gender identity, gender expression, or sex characteristics. In 2013, Cabo Verde held its first public Pride parade , making it the second African country ever to officially host a Pride event. In public opinion polls (2019-2021), 82 percent of respondents said they would accept having a “homosexual” neighbor. LGBTIQ organizations are also able to register freely in the country. However, discrimination still occurs, and there is no legal framework recognizing same-sex unions. Cabo Verde committed in its 2024 Universal Periodic Review to improve the legislative framework to protect LGBTIQ people from discrimination. Aside from South Africa, Cabo Verde is the only other African member of the UN LGBTI Core Group .
