<p id="mwCw"><b id="mwDA">Lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender and queer (LGBTQ) rights</b> in the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Virgin_Islands" title="U.S. Virgin Islands" class="mw-redirect" id="mwDQ">U.S. Virgin Islands</a> have evolved substantially in recent years. Same-sex sexual activity has been legal since 1985. The region also provides explicit legal protections against discrimination for <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ" title="LGBTQ" class="mw-redirect" id="mwDg">LGBTQ</a> residents since December 2022. Following the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_the_United_States" title="Supreme Court of the United States" id="mwDw">Supreme Court's</a> ruling in <i id="mwEA"><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obergefell_v._Hodges" title="Obergefell v. Hodges" id="mwEQ">Obergefell v. Hodges</a></i> on June 26, 2015, which found the denial of marriage rights to same-sex couples unconstitutional, same-sex marriage became legal in the islands.</p>

<meta property="mw:PageProp/toc" id="mwEg" data-mw='{"autoGenerated":true}'/></section><section data-mw-section-id="1" id="mwEw" aria-labelledby="Laws_regarding_same-sex_sexual_activity"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="Laws_regarding_same-sex_sexual_activity">Laws regarding same-sex sexual activity</h2><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=LGBTQ_rights_in_the_United_States_Virgin_Islands&amp;action=edit&amp;section=1" title="Edit section: Laws regarding same-sex sexual activity"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p id="mwFA">The first anti-gay criminal law was imposed by <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark" title="Denmark" id="mwFQ">Denmark</a>. All Danish laws remained in force following the purchase of the islands by the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States" title="United States" id="mwFg">United States</a> in 1917 until specifically changed. In 1921, a new law was passed, establishing a maximum penalty of 10 years' imprisonment for sodomy. A separate law prohibited an assault "with intent to commit ... sodomy" with a penalty of up to 15 years' imprisonment. In 1957, the law's application was extended to include <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oral_sex" title="Oral sex" id="mwFw">oral sex</a>, whether heterosexual or homosexual. In 1978, the law was challenged in <i id="mwGA">Government v. John</i> as being too vague, but the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_Court_of_the_Virgin_Islands" title="District Court of the Virgin Islands" id="mwGQ">District Court of the Virgin Islands</a> rejected this argument.<sup about="#mwt11" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-2" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-2"}}'><a href="#cite_note-2" id="mwGg"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwGw"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwHA">[</span>2<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwHQ">]</span></span></a></sup></p>

<p id="mwHg">In 1984, the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature_of_the_Virgin_Islands" title="Legislature of the Virgin Islands" id="mwHw">Legislature of the Virgin Islands</a> repealed its sodomy law. This revision formally took effect a year later. Same-sex sexual activity has been legal in the U.S. Virgin Islands since 1985, as long as it occurs between consenting adults in private. Initially, the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_consent" title="Age of consent" id="mwIA">age of consent</a> was set at 16, but this was raised to 18 in 2017.<sup about="#mwt14" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-3" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-3"}}'><a href="#cite_note-3" id="mwIQ"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwIg"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwIw">[</span>3<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwJA">]</span></span></a></sup></p>

</section><section data-mw-section-id="2" id="mwJQ" aria-labelledby="Recognition_of_same-sex_relationships"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="Recognition_of_same-sex_relationships">Recognition of same-sex relationships</h2><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=LGBTQ_rights_in_the_United_States_Virgin_Islands&amp;action=edit&amp;section=2" title="Edit section: Recognition of same-sex relationships"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<span class="mw-empty-elt" about="#mwt15" typeof="mw:Transclusion" id="mwJg" data-mw='{"parts":[{"template":{"target":{"wt":"Main","href":"./Template:Main"},"params":{"1":{"wt":"Same-sex marriage in the United States Virgin Islands"}},"i":0}}]}'><style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1368532237" typeof="mw:Extension/templatestyles" about="#mwt16" data-mw='{"name":"templatestyles","attrs":{"src":"Module:Hatnote/styles.css"},"body":{"extsrc":""}}'>.mw-parser-output .hatnote{font-style:italic}.mw-parser-output div.hatnote{padding-left:1.6em;margin-bottom:0.5em}.mw-parser-output .hatnote i{font-style:normal}.mw-parser-output .hatnote+.mw-empty-elt+.hatnote{margin-top:-0.5em}@media print{body.ns-0 .mw-parser-output .hatnote{display:none!important}}</style></span><div role="note" class="hatnote navigation-not-searchable" about="#mwt15" id="mwJw">Main article: <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_the_United_States_Virgin_Islands" title="Same-sex marriage in the United States Virgin Islands">Same-sex marriage in the United States Virgin Islands</a></div>
<p id="mwKA">The statutes of the Virgin Islands state that "Marriage is hereby declared to be a civil contract which may be entered into between a male and a female in accordance with the law."<sup about="#mwt19" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-4" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-4"}}'><a href="#cite_note-4" id="mwKQ"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwKg"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwKw">[</span>4<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwLA">]</span></span></a></sup> However, on June 30, 2015, Governor <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Mapp" title="Kenneth Mapp" id="mwLQ">Kenneth Mapp</a> announced that the islands would comply with the U.S. Supreme Court ruling in <i id="mwLg"><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obergefell_v._Hodges" title="Obergefell v. Hodges" id="mwLw">Obergefell v. Hodges</a></i>, recognizing marriage as a fundamental right that cannot be denied to same-sex couples.<sup about="#mwt22" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-5" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-5"}}'><a href="#cite_note-5" id="mwMA"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwMQ"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwMg">[</span>5<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwMw">]</span></span></a></sup></p>

<section data-mw-section-id="3" id="mwNA" aria-labelledby="History"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading3"><h3 id="History">History</h3><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=LGBTQ_rights_in_the_United_States_Virgin_Islands&amp;action=edit&amp;section=3" title="Edit section: History"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p id="mwNQ">In May 2014, Senator Judi Buckley introduced draft legislation in the <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature_of_the_Virgin_Islands" title="Legislature of the Virgin Islands" id="mwNg">Legislature of the Virgin Islands</a> to establish same-sex marriage. Called the <i id="mwNw">Civil Marriage Equality Act</i>, it would have replaced the Code's "between a male and a female" with "between two persons". It included language that would have allowed anyone authorized to perform a wedding ceremony to decline to do so for any reason. She anticipated that it would take several months for its language to be reviewed.<sup about="#mwt25" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-fierce_6-0" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{"name":"fierce"},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-fierce-6"}}'><a href="#cite_note-fierce-6" id="mwOA"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwOQ"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwOg">[</span>6<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwOw">]</span></span></a></sup> She expected that she and Governor <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_de_Jongh" title="John de Jongh" class="mw-redirect" id="mwPA">John de Jongh</a>, who she said would sign the legislation, would leave office in January 2015 before the legislation came to a vote.<sup about="#mwt28" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-unlikely_7-0" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{"name":"unlikely"},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-unlikely-7"}}'><a href="#cite_note-unlikely-7" id="mwPQ"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwPg"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwPw">[</span>7<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwQA">]</span></span></a></sup> Supporters of the legislation include Liberty Place, an LGBT advocacy organization based on St. Croix.<sup about="#mwt29" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-fierce_6-1" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{"name":"fierce"}}'><a href="#cite_note-fierce-6" id="mwQQ"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwQg"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwQw">[</span>6<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwRA">]</span></span></a></sup></p>

<p id="mwRQ">In response, a group of church leaders organized <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Voice_Virgin_Islands" title="One Voice Virgin Islands" id="mwRg">One Voice Virgin Islands</a> to oppose the legislation and plan a petition drive that aimed to collect 50,000 signatures.<sup about="#mwt30" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-fierce_6-2" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{"name":"fierce"}}'><a href="#cite_note-fierce-6" id="mwRw"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwSA"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwSQ">[</span>6<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwSg">]</span></span></a></sup> The group authored a letter to V.I. officials that some of its members found objectionable because it included the suggestion that some government officials were homosexual.<sup about="#mwt31" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-fierce_6-3" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{"name":"fierce"}}'><a href="#cite_note-fierce-6" id="mwSw"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwTA"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwTQ">[</span>6<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwTg">]</span></span></a></sup><sup about="#mwt32" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-unlikely_7-1" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{"name":"unlikely"}}'><a href="#cite_note-unlikely-7" id="mwTw"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwUA"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwUQ">[</span>7<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwUg">]</span></span></a></sup> The group's president, New Vision Ministries Pastor James Petty of St. Thomas, said: "We do not wish to be America's same-sex paradise".<sup about="#mwt33" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-fierce_6-4" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{"name":"fierce"}}'><a href="#cite_note-fierce-6" id="mwUw"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwVA"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwVQ">[</span>6<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwVg">]</span></span></a></sup> Pastor Lennox Zamore said that he rejected the argument that legalizing same-sex marriage would benefit the local economy: "We don't want to balance our books by bringing the sex industry – whether it is same sex or not – to the Virgin Islands".<sup about="#mwt36" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-8" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-8"}}'><a href="#cite_note-8" id="mwVw"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwWA"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwWQ">[</span>8<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwWg">]</span></span></a></sup> Senator Buckley expressed "disappointment" in the churches, stating that they invoke the Bible only when same-sex marriage is the topic, but not for murder, rape or abuse.<sup about="#mwt37" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-unlikely_7-2" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{"name":"unlikely"}}'><a href="#cite_note-unlikely-7" id="mwWw"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwXA"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwXQ">[</span>7<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwXg">]</span></span></a></sup> Eventually, the bill did not advance before Buckley left office in January 2015.</p>

</section></section><section data-mw-section-id="4" id="mwXw" aria-labelledby="Adoption_and_parenting"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="Adoption_and_parenting">Adoption and parenting</h2><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=LGBTQ_rights_in_the_United_States_Virgin_Islands&amp;action=edit&amp;section=4" title="Edit section: Adoption and parenting"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p id="mwYA">Following <i id="mwYQ">Obergefell v. Hodges</i>, same-sex couples are permitted to adopt in the Virgin Islands. Additionally, lesbian couples may access <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_vitro_fertilisation" title="In vitro fertilisation" id="mwYg">in vitro fertilisation</a> and other assisted reproduction services. State law recognizes the non-gestational, non-genetic parent as a legal parent of a child born via donor insemination, but only if the parents are in a legally recognized relationship (e.g. marriage).<sup about="#mwt40" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-9" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-9"}}'><a href="#cite_note-9" id="mwYw"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwZA"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwZQ">[</span>9<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwZg">]</span></span></a></sup></p>

</section><section data-mw-section-id="5" id="mwZw" aria-labelledby="Discrimination_protections"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="Discrimination_protections">Discrimination protections</h2><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=LGBTQ_rights_in_the_United_States_Virgin_Islands&amp;action=edit&amp;section=5" title="Edit section: Discrimination protections"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p id="mwaA">In September 2022, a bill was introduced to explicitly include "sexual orientation and gender identity" to the US Virgin Islands Code (regarding discrimination protections). There has also been a debate when introduced as to it is actually needed - because under federal law that discrimination based on sex, automatically includes sexual orientation during a 2020 SCOTUS ruling.<sup about="#mwt43" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-10" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-10"}}'><a href="#cite_note-10" id="mwaQ"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwag"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwaw">[</span>10<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwbA">]</span></span></a></sup> The bill passed the committee and floor stages and went to the Governor's desk. Then in December 2022, the Governor formally signed a bill into law (effective immediately) that passed recently to explicitly include both sexual orientation and gender identity - to legally protect individuals against discrimination on the US Virgin Islands.<sup about="#mwt46" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-11" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-11"}}'><a href="#cite_note-11" id="mwbQ"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwbg"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwbw">[</span>11<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwcA">]</span></span></a></sup> The US Virgin Islands law addresses discrimination in employment, housing and public accommodations on account of sexual orientation or gender identity. The <i id="mwcQ">Virgin Islands Civil Rights Act</i> prohibits such discrimination based on "race, color, religion, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, national origin, age, or disability".<sup about="#mwt49" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-12" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-12"}}'><a href="#cite_note-12" id="mwcg"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwcw"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwdA">[</span>12<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwdQ">]</span></span></a></sup></p>

<p id="mwdg">Discrimination on account of sexual orientation is prohibited by <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_service_agreement" title="Master service agreement" id="mwdw">Master Service Providers</a>, who must "provide consistent and equal telecommunication services to all licensees and shall not discriminate" based on a variety of reasons.<sup about="#mwt52" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-13" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-13"}}'><a href="#cite_note-13" id="mweA"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mweQ"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mweg">[</span>13<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwew">]</span></span></a></sup> In addition, the Department of Health has a personnel non-discrimination policy, forbidding discrimination on the basis of sexual orientation and gender identity.<sup about="#mwt55" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-14" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-14"}}'><a href="#cite_note-14" id="mwfA"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwfQ"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwfg">[</span>14<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwfw">]</span></span></a></sup></p>

<section data-mw-section-id="6" id="mwgA" aria-labelledby="Hate_crime_law"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading3"><h3 id="Hate_crime_law">Hate crime law</h3><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=LGBTQ_rights_in_the_United_States_Virgin_Islands&amp;action=edit&amp;section=6" title="Edit section: Hate crime law"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p id="mwgQ">The <i id="mwgg">Hate-Motivated Crimes Act</i>, passed in 2014, provides for enhanced penalties for crimes committed based on the victim's "race, color, religion, national origin, sex, ancestry, age, disability, sexual orientation or gender identity".<sup about="#mwt58" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-15" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-15"}}'><a href="#cite_note-15" id="mwgw"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwhA"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwhQ">[</span>15<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwhg">]</span></span></a></sup></p> 

</section><section data-mw-section-id="7" id="mwhw" aria-labelledby="Bullying_and_discrimination_in_schools"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading3"><h3 id="Bullying_and_discrimination_in_schools">Bullying and discrimination in schools</h3><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=LGBTQ_rights_in_the_United_States_Virgin_Islands&amp;action=edit&amp;section=7" title="Edit section: Bullying and discrimination in schools"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p id="mwiA">Virgin Islands law requires each school district to "make suitable provisions for instruction in bullying prevention and gang resistance training". "Bullying prevention" is defined as "prevention and strategies for student-centered problem solving all of the following: (1) Intimidation; (2) Student victimization; (3) Sexual harassment; (4) Sexual violence; (5) Sexual, discrimination due to sexual orientation; and (6) Harassment."<sup about="#mwt61" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-16" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-16"}}'><a href="#cite_note-16" id="mwiQ"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwig"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwiw">[</span>16<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwjA">]</span></span></a></sup></p>

<p id="mwjQ">In March 2017, Attorney General Claude Walker interpreted the term "sex" as used in the <i id="mwjg">Civil Rights Act</i> to cover transgender students. As the term is not defined in Virgin Islands law, Walker instead chose to follow case law, particularly <i id="mwjw"><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Price_Waterhouse_v._Hopkins" title="Price Waterhouse v. Hopkins" id="mwkA">Price Waterhouse v. Hopkins</a></i> and <i id="mwkQ"><a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_v._Brumby" title="Glenn v. Brumby" id="mwkg">Glenn v. Brumby</a></i>. Walker said:</p>
<style data-mw-deduplicate="TemplateStyles:r1244412712" typeof="mw:Extension/templatestyles mw:Transclusion" about="#mwt62" id="mwkw" data-mw='{"name":"templatestyles","attrs":{"src":"Template:Blockquote/styles.css"},"parts":[{"template":{"target":{"wt":"blockquote","href":"./Template:Blockquote"},"params":{"1":{"wt":"Given the courts and agency decisions … it appears clear that the term \"sex,\" as used in … Virgin Islands Code, also should be interpreted to mean that in the U.S. Virgin Islands, discrimination against a transgender individual based on that person&apos;s transgender status is sex discrimination under the Virgin Islands Civil Rights statute."}},"i":0}}]}'>.mw-parser-output .templatequote{overflow:hidden;margin:1em 0;padding:0 32px}.mw-parser-output .templatequotecite{line-height:1.5em;text-align:left;margin-top:0}@media(min-width:500px){.mw-parser-output .templatequotecite{padding-left:1.6em}}</style><blockquote class="templatequote " about="#mwt62" id="mwlA"><p>Given the courts and agency decisions … it appears clear that the term "sex," as used in … Virgin Islands Code, also should be interpreted to mean that in the U.S. Virgin Islands, discrimination against a transgender individual based on that person's transgender status is sex discrimination under the Virgin Islands Civil Rights statute.</p></blockquote>

<p id="mwlQ">Walker instructed the Department of Education to permit transgender students to use bathrooms and locker rooms matching their gender identity.<sup about="#mwt66" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-17" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-17"}}'><a href="#cite_note-17" id="mwlg"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwlw"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwmA">[</span>17<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwmQ">]</span></span></a></sup></p>

</section></section><section data-mw-section-id="8" id="mwmg" aria-labelledby="Gender_identity_and_expression"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="Gender_identity_and_expression">Gender identity and expression</h2><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=LGBTQ_rights_in_the_United_States_Virgin_Islands&amp;action=edit&amp;section=8" title="Edit section: Gender identity and expression"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p id="mwmw">There exists no statutory ban on changing the gender marker on birth certificates, though no legal regulations are known to exist to do so. The National Center for Transgender Equality reports that "multiple representatives of the Superior Court of USVI stated that they are unaware of a single instance of a petition being received for changing a gender marker. The court representatives were unable to speculate on the outcome of a petition for changing a gender marker." Transgender individuals are permitted to change their name to reflect their gender identity on official documents, including driver's licenses and birth certificates.<sup about="#mwt69" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-18" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-18"}}'><a href="#cite_note-18" id="mwnA"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwnQ"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwng">[</span>18<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwnw">]</span></span></a></sup></p>

</section><section data-mw-section-id="9" id="mwoA" aria-labelledby="Living_conditions"><div class="mw-heading mw-heading2"><h2 id="Living_conditions">Living conditions</h2><span class="mw-editsection"><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">[</span><a href="/w/index.php?title=LGBTQ_rights_in_the_United_States_Virgin_Islands&amp;action=edit&amp;section=9" title="Edit section: Living conditions"><span>edit</span></a><span class="mw-editsection-bracket">]</span></span></div>
<p id="mwoQ">The U.S. Virgin Islands, and particularly <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Thomas,_U.S._Virgin_Islands" title="Saint Thomas, U.S. Virgin Islands" id="mwog">St. Thomas</a>, had a large LGBT scene from the mid 1960s to the 1980s, even been referred in the media as a "gay mecca". Numerous clubs and bar would openly cater to LGBT clientele. However, with <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Hugo" title="Hurricane Hugo" id="mwow">Hurricane Hugo</a> and <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Marilyn" title="Hurricane Marilyn" id="mwpA">Hurricane Marilyn</a>, which had massive economic impacts on the islands, as well as the AIDS pandemic, many of these businesses closed. Today, "homosexuality is a very hush hush thing in the Virgin Islands … to identify as openly gay, lesbian, or transgender [is] extremely taboo". While LGBT people may be out to varying degrees, many report occasional or common harassment or abuse, though often non-violent.<sup about="#mwt72" class="mw-ref reference" id="cite_ref-19" rel="dc:references" typeof="mw:Extension/ref" data-mw='{"name":"ref","attrs":{},"body":{"id":"mw-reference-text-cite_note-19"}}'><a href="#cite_note-19" id="mwpQ"><span class="mw-reflink-text" id="mwpg"><span class="cite-bracket" id="mwpw">[</span>19<span class="cite-bracket" id="mwqA">]</span></span></a></sup></p>

<p id="mwqQ">Famous LGBT people from the Virgin Islands include professional boxer <a rel="mw:WikiLink" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emile_Griffith" title="Emile Griffith" id="mwqg">Emile Griffith</a>.</p>
