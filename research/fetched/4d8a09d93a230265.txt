No Same-sex Relations for Women Legal Throughout the Country? No Legal Gender Recognition Possible? No LGBTI Orgs Able to Register? No Last Update: March 12, 2026 Bhutan decriminalized consensual same-sex intimacy in 2021, marking a significant legal turning point for sexual and gender minorities. Since then, LGBTIQ organizations have been able to register and operate legally in the country, although activists report that the registration and administrative process is lengthy and burdensome. Broader legal protections and policy inclusion for sexual and gender minorities nevertheless remain limited. Bhutan does not have a stand-alone antidiscrimination law , nor does current legislation explicitly prohibit discrimination based on sexual orientation, gender identity, gender expression, or sex characteristics. Legal protections against discrimination remain fragmented across constitutional provisions, employment law, data protection legislation, and other sector-specific policies addressing prejudice-motivated conduct. Legal gender recognition for transgender and gender-diverse people is currently unavailable in practice. While authorities previously allowed changes to legal gender markers through administrative procedures, these processes have been suspended due to the absence of standardized guidelines, leaving transgender people without legal recognition or certainty. Discrimination and social stigma persist , particularly affecting transgender people and access to essential services such as health care. In 2025, Bhutan’s InfoComm and Media Authority explicitly prohibited incitement to hatred or discrimination based on sexual orientation and gender in films, representing a regulatory development in the protection against hate speech.
