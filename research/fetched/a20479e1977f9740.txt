        <section class="section wysiwyg" id="summary">
        <div class="container content small">
                            <div class="section-title">Summary</div>
                                        <div class="wysiwyg-content"><p>Same-sex sexual activity is prohibited under the Penal Code 2015, which criminalises ‘immodest or unnatural acts’. This provision carries a maximum penalty of three years’ imprisonment and a fine. Both men and women are criminalised under this law.</p>
<p>In 1960, Eastern Togo officially gained independence from France, which had long since decriminalised same-sex sexual activity. As such the criminalising law is of local origin, having been present in the 1980 Penal Code and maintained in the 2015 Penal Code.</p>
<p>There is limited evidence of the law being enforced in recent years, with LGBT people being occasionally subject to arrest, though reports suggest that they are often arrested and charged under offences other than the same-sex sexual activity provision. There is some evidence of discrimination and violence against LGBT people in Togo in recent years, however the real situation is likely more severe and unknown due to a lack of reporting.</p>
</div>
                                </div>
    </section>
        <section class="section wysiwyg" id="law-and-legal-developments">
        <div class="container content small">
                            <div class="section-title">Law and Legal Developments</div>
                                        <div class="wysiwyg-content"><h4><b><span data-contrast="auto">2022</span></b><span data-ccp-props="{&quot;335559739&quot;:240}"> </span></h4>
<p>In July, the Minister for Communication and Media, Professor Akodah Ayewouadan, <a href="https://www.togobreakingnews.info/lgbt-la-position-du-togo-ne-variera-pas/">reiterated</a> that Togo’s position on decriminalising consensual same-sex sexual activity had not changed.</p>
<h4><b><span data-contrast="auto">2021</span></b><span data-ccp-props="{&quot;335559739&quot;:240}"> </span></h4>
<p>In a report to the UN Human Rights Committee, the Minister for Human Rights, Christian EninamTrimua, <a href="https://www.ohchr.org/en/press-releases/2021/07/human-rights-committee-raises-concerns-about-torture-transitional-justice">noted</a> that Togo did not plan to repeal the provisions that criminalised consensual same-sex sexual activity, as this was not in line with Togolese social values.</p>
<h4><strong>2015</strong></h4>
<p>In 2015 a new <a href="https://natlex.ilo.org/dyn/natlex2/r/natlex/fe/details?p3_isn=104616">Penal Code</a> was adopted. The new Penal Code retained the criminalising provision with a different phrasing, dropping the ‘crime against nature’ formulation and instead criminalising any ‘indecent or unnatural act committed with a person of the same sex’. The same provision also criminalises LGBT advocacy. In terms of the available punishment, the possible prison sentence remained the same, though the fine available was increased.</p>
</div>
                                        <div class="wysiwyg-legal">
                    <div class="section-title-secondary">Criminal Provisions</div>
                    <div class="wysiwyg-laws">
                                                    <div class="wysiwyg-law">
                                <div class="wysiwyg-law-title">Penal Code 2015, Articles 392 to 393 Unnatural Acts</div>
                                <div class="wysiwyg-law-description">Article 392 criminalises any ‘immodest or unnatural act’ with someone of the same sex as an affront to public decency. Article 393 sets the penalty at between one to three years’ imprisonment and a fine of between one to three million Francs. It is applicable to both men and women.
</div>
                            </div>
                                            </div>
                </div>
                    </div>
    </section>
        <section class="section wysiwyg" id="enforcement">
        <div class="container content small">
                            <div class="section-title">Enforcement</div>
                                        <div class="wysiwyg-content"><h4><strong>2023</strong></h4>
<p>The US Department of <a href="https://www.state.gov/reports/2023-country-reports-on-human-rights-practices/togo/">State report </a>noted that although LGBT people were arrested, it was rarely specifically under article 392, but usually under another legal provision, for instance as a ‘shameless or unnatural act’. Prosecutions of same-sex sexual activity were often not pursued subsequent to arrest.</p>
<p>In May, the TikTok influencer Bobo Périta, known for cross-dressing on social media, <a href="https://24heureinfo.com/justice/togo-lhomo-kossi-elikplim-alias-bobo-perita-depose-en-prison/">was arrested</a> by the national police on charges of ‘affront to public decency’. He was charged and reportedly convicted on 1 June, and he is reportedly being held in the civil prison of Lomé.</p>
<h4><b><span data-contrast="auto">2021</span></b></h4>
<p>In August, the concluding observations on Togo’s fifth periodic report by UN Human Rights Committee <a href="https://www.ohchr.org/en/documents/concluding-observations/ccprctgoco5-concluding-observations-fifth-periodic-report-togo">noted</a> that there had been reports of security forces subjecting people to harassment, attacks, ill-treatment and arbitrary detention on the basis of their actual or presumed sexual orientation or gender identity.</p>
<h4><strong>2018</strong></h4>
<p>An <a href="https://religionnews.com/2018/01/12/for-togos-lgbtq-community-justice-remains-just-out-of-reach/">article</a> published in January quoted a transgender woman who recounted a story of two transgender friends being arrested and detained for three days on the basis of the clothes they were wearing. They were only released upon paying a fee to authorities. Another trans person was arrested by a patrol and taken to her family in order to forcibly reveal her identity to them.</p>
<h4><b><span data-contrast="auto">2014</span></b><span data-ccp-props="{&quot;335559739&quot;:240}"> </span></h4>
<p>It was reported that a gay man who was on his way home from a party wearing female clothing was <a href="https://www.amnesty.org/en/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/AFR5782022018ENGLISH.pdf">arrested and detained</a> without charge for five days in a police station. The police officers forced him to take off his clothing, insulted him, and took videos and photographs and threatened to leak the images to the press if he did not comply with their instructions, which included forcing him to dance in front of the other detainees and officers. He was released without charge after five days.</p>
<h4><strong>2012</strong></h4>
<p>The US Department of State <a href="https://acjr.org.za/resource-centre/us-department-of-state-human-rights-report-togo-2012">report</a> noted that a Ghanaian man was arrested during the year for same-sex sexual activity, however the judge, wanting to avoid public attention, charged the man with ‘disrupting public order’.</p>
<h4><strong>2010</strong></h4>
<p>It was <a href="https://www.refworld.org/docid/4fc75a562.html">reported</a> that eight people were arrested for same-sex sexual activity in 2010.</p>
</div>
                                </div>
    </section>
        <section class="section wysiwyg" id="discrimination-and-violence">
        <div class="container content small">
                            <div class="section-title">Discrimination and Violence</div>
                                        <div class="wysiwyg-content"><h4><strong>2023</strong></h4>
<p>The US Department of <a href="https://www.state.gov/reports/2023-country-reports-on-human-rights-practices/togo/">State report </a>noted that LGBT people faced societal discrimination across employment, housing, education, and healthcare. Most anti-LGBT discrimination was not reported due to fear of criminalisation and stigma.</p>
<h4><strong>2022</strong></h4>
<p>In April, a group of gay men <a href="https://www.adheos.org/togo-lome-des-homosexuels-chasses-a-coups-de-poing-de-la-plage/">were beaten up</a> and chased out of the beach of Lomé by a mob of angry youth for displaying “effeminate” behaviour. Media and social media widely <a href="https://www.state.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/415610_TOGO-2022-HUMAN-RIGHTS-REPORT.pdf">reported</a> on the attack, leading to anti-LGBT statements and threats from government officials and the public.</p>
<p>Abotsi Ablamvi, one of the men assaulted at Lomé beach, <a href="https://netafrique.net/togo-un-homosexuel-frappe-a-la-plage-porte-disparu/">went missing</a> after the incident and, according to his family, had not been heard from as of April 2023. His family reported that he had been receiving death threats and had been regularly harassed in the streets.</p>
<p>In May, a presumed gay man <a href="https://beninwebtv.com/togo-emoi-suite-a-la-video-dun-homosxuel-battu-et-deshabille/">was stripped naked and beaten up</a> by a group of unknown assailants who later shared a video of their assault on social media.</p>
<h4><b><span data-contrast="auto">2021</span></b><span data-ccp-props="{&quot;335559739&quot;:240}"> </span></h4>
<p>A <a href="https://ccprcentre.org/files/documents/INT_CCPR_CSS_TGO_45147_F.pdf">report</a> submitted to the UN Human Rights Committee in July noted that local LGBT organisations documented 37 instances of violence and abuse in 2017, 42 in 2018, 35 in 2019, 33 in 2020 and around 10 between January and June 2021.</p>
<p>A <a href="https://www.opendemocracy.net/en/5050/tiktok-lgbt-togo-community/">report</a> by OpenDemocracy sets out that LGBT people also face harassment online.</p>
<h4><strong>2020</strong></h4>
<p>According to the US Department of State <a href="https://www.state.gov/reports/2020-country-reports-on-human-rights-practices/togo/">report</a>, a media code is in place that prevents the ‘promotion of immorality’ which includes same-sex sexual activity.</p>
<h4><strong>2018</strong></h4>
<p>An <a href="https://religionnews.com/2018/01/12/for-togos-lgbtq-community-justice-remains-just-out-of-reach/">article</a> published in January notes that the LGBT community face discrimination and violence, but are afraid to report incidents to police due to fear of reprisals.</p>

<script>
  // https://github.com/senff/Sticky-Anything
  $('.sticky-nav').stickThis({
    zindex: 10
  });

  // https://stackoverflow.com/questions/9979827/change-active-menu-item-on-page-scroll
  // Cache selectors
  var lastId,
          topMenu = $("#sticky-nav"),
          topMenuHeight = topMenu.outerHeight(),
          // All list items
          menuItems = topMenu.find("a"),
          // Anchors corresponding to menu items
          scrollItems = menuItems.map(function(){
            var item = $($(this).attr("href"));
            if (item.length) { return item; }
          });

  // Bind click handler to menu items
  // so we can get a fancy scroll animation
  menuItems.click(function(e){
    var href = $(this).attr("href"),
            offsetTop = href === "#" ? 0 : $(href).offset().top-topMenuHeight+1;
    $('html, body').stop().animate({
      scrollTop: offsetTop
    }, 500);
    e.preventDefault();
  });

  // Bind to scroll
  $(window).scroll(function(){
    // Get container scroll position
    var fromTop = $(this).scrollTop()+topMenuHeight;

    // Get id of current scroll item
    var cur = scrollItems.map(function(){
      if ($(this).offset().top < fromTop)
        return this;
    });
    // Get the id of the current element
    cur = cur[cur.length-1];
    var id = cur && cur.length ? cur[0].id : "";

    if (lastId !== id) {
      lastId = id;
      // Set/remove active class
      menuItems
              .parent().removeClass("active")
              .end().filter("[href='#"+id+"']").parent().addClass("active");
    }
  });
</script>
