# COL — Colombia · ADM1 research
Researched: 2026-09-17 · Units in boundary data: 33 usable, 0 excluded

## Richness classification
RICH — Colombia's two main monitoring bodies (the Ombudsman's office, Defensoría del Pueblo, and the
NGO Caribe Afirmativo) publish **homicide counts attributed to departments**, so per-unit violence
data exists for 2023, 2024 and 2025 and shows a clear geographic hierarchy (Antioquia ≫ Atlántico,
Bogotá, Bolívar, Valle). On top of that there is a genuine **capital-city policing finding**:
repeated police beatings and mass identity checks of trans people in Bogotá's Santa Fe/Los Mártires
tolerance zone. No Colombian department has anti-trans legislation — the entire divergence is
lethality plus policing practice.

## Different-legal-system regions
**None in the mandatory-clause sense:** the penal code, criminal procedure and the gender-identity
notaría route are national, and no department has religious or customary criminal law. Two
documented qualifications:
- **Frontier and port departments** (La Guajira, Norte de Santander, Arauca, Vichada, Guainía,
  Vaupés, Chocó, Putumayo, the San Andrés archipelago) have "orden público" regimes where armed
  groups, not the departmental police, effectively regulate conduct. Caribe Afirmativo's 2025 report
  explicitly notes the *territorial expansion* of violence and the insufficiency of the state
  response, and Colombian trans women describe being displaced from their regions **because of their
  gender identity** by the same armed actors that displace others. — https://www.caribeafirmativo.lgbt/colombia-enfrenta-una-crisis-estructural-de-violencia-contra-personas-lgbtiq-una-persona-fue-asesinada-cada-32-horas-en-2025/ · http://hdl.handle.net/1992/7497
- **Bogotá D.C. as capital district** has its own district administration, district police
  (Policía Metropolitana de Bogotá) and its own district policy instruments — which is why the Santa
  Fe policing pattern below is a district-level, not national, phenomenon. This is an
  administrative-competence difference, not a separate criminal law.

## Regions with findings

### Antioquia (`13589884B3780741375954`) — incl. Medellín and Bello
Direction: worse than national — by a wide margin the most lethal department.
- **45 of the 270 LGBTIQ+ killings recorded in Colombia in 2025 happened in Antioquia**, and Caribe
  Afirmativo's director Wilson Castañeda presented the department as "one of the most dangerous for
  LGBTIQ+ people in Colombia". Reporting on the same year states that **more than half of the
  country's murders of the population occurred in the department** for part of the year, with three
  of the cases in the municipality of **Bello** alone. — https://www.lafm.com.co/sociedad/lgbtiq-antioquia-entre-los-departamentos-con-mayor-violencia-contra-la-poblacion-403502 · https://www.elcolombiano.com/medellin/antioquia-homicidios-lgbtiq-crimen-sara-millerey-2025-AD27068901
- Emblematic within a series: **Sara Millerey González Borja, 32**, a trans woman who worked as a
  recycler, was brutally beaten and thrown into the Quebrada La García in Bello on **4 April 2025**;
  onlookers filmed her struggling in the water without intervening. Her death triggered a vigil at
  Bogotá's Parque de los Hippies and a national alert about the rise in transfeminicides. El Tiempo
  situates it among **13 trans women murdered in 2025** at that point and asks who is behind them. — https://www.bbc.com/mundo/articles/cg41195wv6no · https://www.eltiempo.com/justicia/investigacion/violencia-por-prejuicio-sara-millerey-y-las-13-mujeres-trans-asesinadas-en-2025-quienes-estan-detras-3444188 · https://www.lafm.com.co/orden-publico/mas-de-20-transfeminicidios-en-2025-agravan-la-violencia-de-genero-en-colombia-386422
- No institutional protection finding for Antioquia appears in the monitoring reports; the framing is
  "prejudice, violence and impunity".

### Bogotá Capital District (`13589884B40908771570386`)
Direction: mixed — the best-documented trans community and services in Colombia, and a documented police-violation pattern in its tolerance zone.
- **Police violence, pattern:** the Red Comunitaria Trans reported that at **3:50 a.m. on calle 22 con
  Caracas**, Bogotá police shouted at trans sex workers, beat them with batons, fired shots and chased
  them; the district mayor's office took up the case. — https://www.eltiempo.com/bogota/trabajadoras-sexuales-trans-denuncian-violencia-de-la-policia-en-el-centro-de-bogota-509300
- **Killing cluster:** on the night of **2 January 2026** two trans women were killed in Bogotá within
  hours of each other — "Catia", 24, beaten to death with a metal object by a man arriving on a
  motorcycle in the Engativá locality (suspect still sought a week later), and a 20-year-old beaten to
  death in the Florida Blanca neighbourhood, the attacker escaping with an accomplice on a motorcycle.
  Caribe Afirmativo treats these as part of the national series, and a friend of Catia's described "a
  pattern of systematic violence in the area". — https://www.infobae.com/colombia/2026/01/08/nuevos-videos-muestran-detalles-clave-del-asesinato-de-catia-mujer-trans-de-24-anos-en-engativa/ · https://www.infobae.com/colombia/2026/01/07/consternacion-en-bogota-por-mujer-trans-de-20-anos-que-fue-asesinada-a-golpes/ · https://www.elespectador.com/bogota/video-sacude-el-caso-de-catia-mujer-trans-asesinada-a-golpes-en-bogota-buscan-al-atacante/
- In December 2023 **Roxanna Delgado Trujillo, 32, a trans activist**, was found dead in the cerros
  orientales; El Espectador used the case to describe "the recurring mechanisms in crimes against
  trans women", i.e. the press itself frames this as a pattern, not an incident. — https://www.elespectador.com/genero-y-diversidad/la-disidencia/caso-roxanna-delgado-el-patron-en-las-violencias-contra-mujeres-trans/
- **Structural, district-published:** the Bogotá Mayor's Office's own law journal carries a study of
  "violence against the trans community in the tolerance zone of the Santa Fe neighbourhood",
  cataloguing the forms of violence and the applicable policy; the Santa Fe/Los Mártires sector is
  described as a refuge where trans sex workers "don't keep count of their dead sisters", with
  expulsions and displacement pushing them into other localities. — https://doctrinadistrital.com/ojs2/index.php/RevistaDoctrinaDistrital/article/view/76 · https://www.elespectador.com/bogota/ser-trans-en-el-barrio-santa-fe-el-refugio-de-la-comunidad-transgenero-en-bogota/ · http://hdl.handle.net/1992/7497
- **Mass identity checks:** district "mega-operativos" in the same area — 25 April 2025 in San Bernardo
  (200+ police, city hall, ICBF and Migration Colombia officers) and January 2026 in Santa Fe (**more
  than 1,200 uniformed officers** conducting registration and control, background checks and
  **migrant control**, with arrests). A visibly gender-nonconforming person in central Bogotá is
  therefore statistically likely to be in the path of a sweep whose declared logic is background and
  migration checks. — https://bogota.gov.co/mi-ciudad/seguridad/megaoperativo-en-el-barrio-san-bernardo-en-el-centro-de-bogota · https://bogota.gov.co/mi-ciudad/seguridad/operativos-de-la-policia-pagadiarios-en-barrio-santa-fe-de-bogota

### Atlántico (`13589884B77991835419453`) — Barranquilla
Direction: worse than national.
- Caribe Afirmativo's 2025 report specifically warns of **increasing violence against LGBTIQ+ people in
  the Atlántico department** alongside the national rise, and the department's 270-victim national
  total is presented "including Atlántico". Barranquilla is the Caribbean-coast hub for trans sex work
  and the region where the NGO's documentation work is based. — https://www.publimetro.co/barranquilla/2026/05/18/caribe-afirmativo-alerta-aumento-de-violencia-contra-poblacion-lgbtiq-en-atlantico-y-colombia-en-informe/

### Bolívar (`13589884B96168520946689`)
Direction: worse than national — targeted killing of a community leader.
- **Nawar Jiménez, a trans social leader**, was murdered in Bolívar on 22 May; she had campaigned with
  the "No Es Hora De Callar" movement and taken part in its sixth symbolic return march with 500+
  survivors of sexual violence. Her killing is documented in national-press data work on the gap
  between official and NGO figures. — https://www.eltiempo.com/datos/crimenes-lgbtiq-las-contradictorias-cifras-entre-el-estado-y-las-organizaciones-sociales-3461249

### Valle del Cauca (`13589884B83777567082486`), Córdoba (`13589884B54743798138783`), Santander (`13589884B55612798068582`), Sucre/Magdalena/other Caribbean departments
Direction: worse than national — the report's "territorial expansion" finding, in departments where
state response is weakest.
- Caribe Afirmativo's 2025 analysis is that violence has **expanded territorially** while the state
  response stayed insufficient; nationally **one LGBTIQ+ person was killed every 32 hours in 2025**, a
  **63% increase** in killings on 2024 (270 vs 164). For 2020–2024 the average was one killing every
  two days, with **18% of the 164 homicides in 2024 being trans women**. — https://www.caribeafirmativo.lgbt/colombia-enfrenta-una-crisis-estructural-de-violencia-contra-personas-lgbtiq-una-persona-fue-asesinada-cada-32-horas-en-2025/ · https://www.infobae.com/colombia/2026/05/19/asesinatos-contra-personas-lgbtiq-aumentaron-63-en-colombia-hay-un-caso-cada-32-horas/ · https://www.caribeafirmativo.lgbt/comunicado-conjunto-sobre-la-situacion-de-violencia-contra-personas-trans-en-colombia/
- No department-specific ordinance, statute or enforcement series was found for these units beyond
  their place in the departmental counts — recorded as counts, not as local findings.

### National homicide baseline for scoring
- **Defensoría del Pueblo (the state ombudsman), official figures:** at least **25 trans women murdered
  between January and September 2025**, five more than in the same period of 2024; **55 trans women
  killed in 2023**; and the Defensoría's annual bulletin identifies **transgender women as the primary
  victims of violence against the LGBTI population in Colombia**. This is the state's own count, which
  makes it the most defensible per-year anchor for the national score. — https://efeminista.com/25-transfeminicidios-colombia-hasta-septiembre/ · https://www.defensoria.gov.co/-/mujeres-transg%C3%A9nero-las-principales-v%C3%ADctimas-de-la-violencia-contra-la-poblaci%C3%B3n-con-orientaci%C3%B3n-sexual-e-identidad-de-g%C3%A9nero-diversa
- Colombia reported the situation to the **Inter-American Commission (CIDH)** in a joint submission
  stating that violence against trans people "responds to patterns of hatred, prejudice and exclusion
  deeply rooted in Colombian society **and reproduced by the State**" — an official-impunity finding
  rather than a cultural one. — https://www.caribeafirmativo.lgbt/comunicado-conjunto-sobre-la-situacion-de-violencia-contra-personas-trans-en-colombia/
- **Official vs NGO numbers diverge** (Fiscalía data versus Caribe Afirmativo/Defensoría), so a
  department's absence from one list is weak evidence of safety. — https://www.eltiempo.com/datos/crimenes-lgbtiq-las-contradictorias-cifras-entre-el-estado-y-las-organizaciones-sociales-3461249

## Urban / rural and general notes
- **Colombia's variation is metro-shaped, not region-shaped in the European sense.** The lethal
  concentration is Antioquia (Medellín and its satellite Bello), Bogotá's central localities, and the
  Barranquilla/Cartagena coast — i.e. specific urban zones with trans sex-work economies. Outside
  those, what raises risk is armed-group control rather than trans-specific hostility, which matters
  for a visitor mainly on intercity routes and in border departments.
- **The mechanism of the killings is repeatedly the same and should be read as a pattern, not as
  individual tragedies:** motorcycle-borne attackers, public streets at night, victims who are trans
  women in or associated with sex work, no arrests weeks later (Catia, Bogotá, Jan 2026; the Florida
  Blanca killing the same night), onlookers who film rather than help (Sara Millerey, Bello, Apr 2025).
- **For a foreign visitor, the two distinct risks are different:** (1) being a visibly trans person in
  the Santa Fe/tolerance-zone geography of Bogotá or in Barranquilla/Medellín's sex-work corridors,
  where both predators and police are documented hazards; (2) being caught in general armed conflict
  or in the mass checkpoint sweeps that Colombia runs in those same districts. National tourism
  marketing (Medellín, Cartagena, San Andrés, Bogotá gay districts) actively attracts LGBTQ+ visitors,
  which raises exposure without changing the underlying risk.
- **No Colombian department criminalises anything about being trans**, and nothing in this pass
  supports a regional *legal-exposure* pattern at all — Colombia's regional pattern is entirely about
  violence and policing. (National-level gender-identity recognition exists and is administered
  through notaría/registry procedures; I did not source its history here and it is a
  resident-facing matter outside this file's scope.)
- Sentencing/naming caveat: the "travesticidio"/"transfeminicidio" figures cited come from the
  Defensoría and Caribe Afirmativo; press figures for the same year vary (13 vs 20+ vs 25 trans women)
  depending on window and definition. Use ranges, not point values.
