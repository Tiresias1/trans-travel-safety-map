# USA — United States · ADM1 research
Researched: 2026-09-17 · Units in boundary data: 51 usable, 5 excluded (ASM, MNP, GUM, PRI, VIR)

## Richness classification
RICH — every one of the 51 units has genuine, current sub-national evidence: ~20 states have
 enacted facility/bathroom bans (several with criminal penalties), a further set have statewide
 "sex = biological sex" statutes, 15 states have formal shield/refuge policies, and three
 independent datasets give per-state violence/incident counts. This is the best-documented
 country on the list; the discriminating work is grading severity, not finding evidence.

## Different-legal-system regions
**None at ADM1 level.** All 51 units sit in one federal/constitutional system; there are no
religious or customary courts with moral jurisdiction and no autonomous region with its own
criminal law. Two caveats an assessor should know:
- **Indian tribal lands / tribal courts** are a genuinely separate legal system (Public Law 280
  jurisdictions, e.g. large parts of Oklahoma, Arizona, New Mexico, South Dakota, North Dakota),
  and tribal law on gender recognition varies nation by nation. Tribal nations are **not** ADM1
  units in this boundary data, so this cannot be scored at ADM1 — it is noted only so the map is
  not read as implying legal uniformity inside state borders.
- **Washington DC** is not a state: it has no voting representation and its local law is subject
  to congressional override — a partially federally-administered jurisdiction. This actually
  matters (see the DC entry), but it is not a different legal system in the sense of the clause.
- Municipal ordinances (e.g. Bossier Parish/Benton, Louisiana, 2026) are sub-ADM1 and are
  delegated state power, not a separate system — but they are the sharpest local signals.

## Regions with findings

### Idaho (`66186276B48953429733047`)
Direction: worse than national — the strictest criminal bathroom ban in the country, the only one reaching private businesses.
- HB 752 (2026), effective 1 July 2026: crime for a trans person to enter a restroom/changing room aligned with their gender in government buildings **and all private businesses open to the public** (gas stations, libraries, hospitals, restaurants, museums). 1st offence misdemeanour up to 1 year; 2nd within 5 years a felony — up to 5 years. — https://www.acluidaho.org/legislation/2026-hb-752-criminalizing-bathroom-use-for-trans-people/
- AP: "Idaho will have the strictest bathroom ban in the nation, subjecting people to time behind bars." — https://apnews.com/article/idaho-legislature-transgender-bathroom-ban-jail-ee10cda1df43979e0cf92cb73352187e
- Six trans residents sued 30 April 2026; a federal court partially blocked it, but per Erin Reed it **remains in effect anywhere a gender-neutral or family restroom exists** — so exposure persists. — https://19thnews.org/2026/04/idaho-transgender-bathroom-ban-lawsuit/
- Also: 2025 bathroom ban H 264 (government buildings), LGBTQ pride-flag ban at public buildings/schools (first wave, 2025), plus a 2023 facility ban — i.e. four successive legislative rounds (2023, 2025, 2026, 2026). — https://mapresearch.org/equality-map/bans-on-transgender-people-using-public-bathrooms-and-facilities-according-to-their-gender-identity/ · https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2025/may/29/pride-month-trump
- Rated "Do Not Travel" (up from "Worst Laws") in the July 2026 adult legal-risk map; Idaho also moved to Do Not Travel on the youth map. — https://www.stonewallnews.net/anti-trans-national-legal-risk-assessment-map-july-2026/

### Kansas (`66186276B64014601259366`)
Direction: worse than national — private "bounty" lawsuits against trans people, plus ID invalidation.
- SB 244 enacted 18 Feb 2026 **over Governor Kelly's veto**; trans people confined to birth-sex facilities in government buildings; creates a private right of action allowing anyone to sue a person they suspect of violating it for ~$1,000 in damages — described as a bounty-hunter system. — https://www.aclukansas.org/publications/sb244faq/ · https://www.stonewallnews.net/anti-trans-national-legal-risk-assessment-map-july-2026/
- Also invalidates existing driver's licences/state IDs with updated gender markers and bars future updates (so an ID carried by a visitor/resident becomes legally void). — https://kslegislature.gov/b2025_26/documents/view-leg/?apn=b2025_26%2Fyear2%2Fready_for_publication%2Fsb_244%2Fsb244_02_0000.pdf
- Doe v. State of Kansas filed late Feb 2026 (ACLU + Ballard Spahr, Douglas County District Court) by two trans men citing "fear of violence". — https://kansasreflector.com/2026/02/27/trans-men-file-lawsuit-over-kansas-law-that-restricts-bathroom-use-and-invalidates-drivers-licenses/

### Florida (`66186276B65591139260314`)

### Tennessee (`66186276B52324970173776`)
Direction: worse than national — the only state with a standing criminal ban on gender-expressive performance, in force after losing its appeal.
- Adult Entertainment Act (SB 3 / HB 9, 2023) criminalises "adult cabaret entertainment" in public or where a child might see it, defined to include "male or female impersonators" — i.e. an expression ban reaching a visitor presenting in drag. — https://legalclarity.org/tennessee-drag-ban-rules-penalties-and-legal-status/
- It is **settled law**: the 6th Circuit reversed the invalidating ruling in July 2024 and the US Supreme Court declined review in Feb 2025, leaving the statute enforceable. — https://apnews.com/article/tennessee-drag-shows-ban-1bd25d455d13f81c0cab43674a054dd9 · https://www.advocate.com/news/tennessee-drag-ban-supreme-court
- Bathroom/facility: 2021 law (updated 2026) requiring facilities that allow gender-identity access to post a sign in bold capitals; sponsor said non-compliance is a Class B misdemeanour, up to $500 / 6 months. Sued by the ACLU; Nashville's DA publicly declined to commit to enforcement. — https://apnews.com/article/tennessee-lifestyle-business-371dce50dc2373291a5583e30dac9ff2 · https://wpln.org/post/tennessees-transgender-bathroom-signage-requirement-challenged-in-court-and-in-public-on-day-1/
- 2019 HB 1151 widened Tennessee's indecent-exposure statute — the mechanism by which expression becomes a sex-offence-style charge. — https://www.findlaw.com/lgbtq-law/transgender-people-and-bathroom-access-laws.html
- 7.8 killings per 100k trans women/yr, above the national general homicide rate of 6.0. — https://theneedlenews.com/2026/07/safe-states-dont-make-safer-people/

## Regions with no sub-national signal
None in the sense of "no data": every remaining unit has at least a tier rating from the national
legal-risk map and a place in the homicide-rate dataset. Units with no **country-unique** finding
beyond that shared evidence: Delaware, Hawaii, Illinois, Massachusetts, New Mexico, Oregon,
Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, Vermont, Wisconsin (i.e. the protective states whose only recorded
differences from their peers are insurance-coverage details a visitor never encounters).

## Urban / rural and general notes
- **A large share of the variation here is city-vs-town, not state-vs-state.** Tampa (FL),
  Arlington (TX), Tucson (AZ) and Columbus (GA) lost or cancelled Pride in 2025–26 while
  Orlando residents defied the state's crosswalk erasure and Austin, Atlanta, Miami and New
  Orleans kept visible communities. Inside hostile states, the metros are materially safer;
  inside protective states, rural counties are materially less so. An assessor should not read a
  state score as uniform. https://truthout.org/articles/as-anti-lgbtq-censorship-expands-these-cities-have-cancelled-pride-this-year/ · https://prismreports.org/2026/06/03/where-pride-wont-be-happening-this-year/
- **The biggest single risk item is federal, not state-level, and applies to every unit**: since
  January 2025 the State Department stopped allowing sex-marker changes on passports and issues
  passports showing sex assigned at birth; and a CBP Carrier Liaison Bulletin (7 July 2025)
  ordered airlines to submit only "M"/"F" in pre-departure API data, erasing "X" markers for
  international travellers. This is where a foreign trans visitor's risk actually concentrates —
  at the port of entry, before any state law applies. https://www.hrc.org/resources/travel-passport-rights-for-transgender-people · https://standingrecord.com/entries/2025/07/07/federal-targeting-marginalized-communities-aa9167ba/
- **Foreign-advisory evidence is national, not regional.** At least nine countries have issued
  guidance to trans travellers about the US (Denmark 21 Mar 2025, Finland, Germany, then others;
  Canada added 2SLGBTQI+ border-risk language to its US advisory on 23 Sept 2025; Equality
  Australia issued a US Travel Alert on 17 Mar 2026). None of them names specific states — so
  these support a national downgrade, not a regional one. https://www.advocate.com/news/trans-travel-advisories-us · https://www.france24.com/en/live-news/20250321-denmark-travel-warning-for-transgender-people-going-to-us · https://ctcnews.ca/2025/09/26/canada-urgent-us-travel-warning/ · https://stunmagazine.com.au/equality-australia-issues-us-travel-warning/
- **Law does not track violence.** The Needle's July 2026 state-by-state analysis is the key
  caution: the highest killing rates for trans women are found in both worst-law states
  (Louisiana) and safest-law states (Maryland 11.1, New Jersey 3.4), and "neither group sorts by
  law" — its headline is "Safe States Don't Make People Safer". Trans women are killed above the
  general US rate (6.0/100k) in 21 of 26 states where it can be measured. Use state law for the
  *legal-exposure* dimension and this dataset for the *violence* dimension; do not let one proxy
  the other. https://theneedlenews.com/2026/07/safe-states-dont-make-safer-people/
- **Volume base rates for context**: 1,042 anti-LGBTQ incidents across 47 states + DC in 2025
  (up 5% YoY; 532 of them, >half, targeting trans and gender-nonconforming people; 76 violent
  assaults, 22 mass-violence threats, 15 arsons), on 932 in 2024 across 49 states + DC. 27 violent
  deaths of trans people in the year to Nov 2025, ~399 since 2013. So the incident data is
  genuinely national in coverage; per-state counts are the right unit of comparison.
  https://glaad.org/anti-lgbtq-extremism-reporting-tracker/ · https://www.hrc.org/press-releases/remembrance-is-not-enough-hrcs-annual-report-outlines-ongoing-onslaught-of-violence-against-trans-people-amid-relentless-political-attacks
- **Trend direction is down, not stable.** The July 2026 assessment explicitly labels the
  nationwide level "Worst Policies In Effect" and designates the US as a Do-Not-Travel zone for
  non-essential trans travel; MAP's own summary is that the 2026 legislative season produced
  "continued degradation". https://www.stonewallnews.net/anti-trans-national-legal-risk-assessment-map-july-2026/
- Courts are a live moderator: Idaho's felony ban is partially blocked, Montana's repeatedly
  blocked, Tennessee's drag ban *upheld*. Enforcement risk therefore differs from statutory risk
  state by state — check the block status before treating a ban as live.
