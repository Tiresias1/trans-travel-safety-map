# ARG — Argentina · ADM1 research
Researched: 2026-09-17 · Units in boundary data: 23 usable, 0 excluded

## Richness classification
MEDIUM — Argentina has one of the world's most protective national frameworks (Gender Identity Law
26.743 since 2012), so there are no hostile provincial statutes to find, and recorded
travesticidios/transfemicidios are low in absolute terms (3 in ten months of 2025). The real
sub-national divergence that is **visitor-relevant** is provincial **police detention law**: the
provinces' *códigos de faltas/contravencionales* and the practice of detaining people for
"averiguación de antecedentes", which is documented as the tool that replaced the old anti-trans
edicts — with concrete, patterned cases in the Province of Buenos Aires and in the City of Buenos
Aires. Provincial statistics exist for Santa Fe. That is 3–4 units with genuine evidence; the rest
have none.

## Different-legal-system regions
**None.** Criminal law is federal-provincial but uniform in substance; no religious or customary
criminal jurisdiction exists. Two structural points that matter for this map:
- **Provincial police-justice codes are a genuine legal divergence.** Argentina's provinces each keep
  their own *código contravencional* or *código de faltas*, a body of administrative-offence law that
  sits outside the penal code and authorises detention without a crime. An NGO survey of these codes
  across all provinces and CABA found they are the instrument behind most arrests of trans and
  travesti people, used "indirectly" to criminalise travesti identity after the 2012 law removed the
  explicit criminalisation — i.e. **which province you are stopped in determines which administrative
  offence you can be detained under.** — https://lgbt.org.ar/archivos/codigos_contravencionalesyfaltas.pdf · https://www.cels.org.ar/web/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/INT_CEDAW_NGO_ARG_25486_S.pdf
- **CABA is the exception with its own criminal-adjacent jurisdiction:** the City has its own Código
  Contravencional (its own *tribunales contravencionales*, not police judges), which cuts both ways —
  stronger guarantees, but still used to write up trans women (art. 81).

## Regions with findings

### Buenos Aires (`19177384B97722443848736`)
Direction: worse than national — the most documented police-persecution record in the country, in three named cities.
- Argentina's federal human-rights reporting records **acts of violence, persecution and harassment by
  Buenos Aires province police — or by third parties acting under their cover — against trans and
  travesti people during police operations in La Plata, Mar del Plata and Esteban Echeverría**,
  carried out under "preventive policing" frameworks and procedures. Submitted as information to the
  UN Human Rights Committee. — https://www.defensorba.org.ar/micrositios/ovg/pdfs/Informe-Comite-Derechos-Humanos-ONU-2016.pdf
- Academic legal analysis of the same province: after the 2012 Gender Identity Law formally decriminalised
  trans presence, **security forces increased use of laws that criminalise travesti/trans identity
  indirectly**, associating it with drug-sale and street sex-work offences; in the Province of Buenos
  Aires the 2005 devolution of drug-law prosecution to provincial justice, plus provincial "anti-narcotics"
  security policy, measurably increased police persecution, violence and criminalisation of trans women —
  with the drug law as the operative instrument. — https://www.cels.org.ar/web/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/INT_CEDAW_NGO_ARG_25486_S.pdf · http://sedici.unlp.edu.ar/bitstream/handle/10915/186938/Documento_completo.pdf-PDFA.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1 · https://www.pensamientopenal.com.ar/system/files/CF220040F1.PDF
- The province's public-defender office maintains an **official guide to registry rectification under
  the Gender Identity Law** (2024) and documents the *pin-parental*/discrimination caseload — evidence
  of a functioning protective bureaucracy alongside the policing problem. — https://www.defensorba.org.ar/pdfs/guia-para-la-rectificacion-por-reconocimiento-identidad-de-genero-2024.pdf

### Ciudad Autónoma de Buenos Aires (`19177384B49506124846693`)
Direction: mixed — the most legally protective jurisdiction in Latin America, with a repeating cycle of police round-ups of travestis and trans women in one neighbourhood.
- **Documented repeat operations:** on 18 September (six months after the unlawful detention of
  **Yhajaira Falcón**), uniformed and plain-clothes officers from **comisaría 18** carried out fresh
  "detentions, arrests and arbitrary identifications" against travesti and trans women **in the
  Constitución neighbourhood**, writing up several under **article 81 of the City's Contravention
  Code** and arresting one for lacking documents. Activists characterise it as "a new illegal
  procedure against travestis and trans" — i.e. the third-plus iteration of the same operation in the
  same barrio, which is why it qualifies as a pattern rather than a single incident. — https://www.anred.org/caba-un-nuevo-procedimiento-ilegal-contra-travestis-y-trans-en-constitucion/
- Institutional counterweights that a visitor can actually use: CABA has an anti-discrimination law and
  INADI/defensoría apparatus, the national "Diana Sacayán–Lohana Berkins" employment quota law's
  administration is concentrated here, and MuMaLa's national femicide/travesticidio registry runs out
  of the city — CABA is where state response is most likely to be *present*. — https://consejomagistratura.gov.ar/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Ley-27636-Promocion-del-acceso-al-empleo-formal-para-personas-travestis-transexuales-y-transgenero.pdf · https://elresaltador.com.ar/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Registro-femicidios-Observatorio-Nacional-MuMaLa-2024.docx.pdf

### Santa Fe (`19177384B6836410786482`)
Direction: better than the regional average on official measurement, worse on outcomes.
- Santa Fe's provincial government publishes its own **preliminary report on violent deaths of women
  with gender-motivated elements — explicitly including transfemicidios and travesticidios** — through
  the Ministry of Equality, Gender and Diversity and its Observatorio de violencias y Desigualdades de
  Género. Few Argentine provinces count trans deaths officially. — https://www.santafe.gob.ar/index.php/web/content/download/270460/1410371/file/Informe%20Preliminar%20sobre%20muertes%20violentas%20de%20mujeres,%20femicidios,%20transfemicidios%20y%20travesticidios.pdf
- Rosario hosts the **Observatorio Mercedes Pagnutti**, which issues provincial-plus-national
  travesticidio/femicidio monitoring (e.g. the 1 January–29 May 2024 report) — named after a woman killed
  in the region, i.e. the civic infrastructure exists. — https://elciudadanoweb.com/el-observatorio-mercedes-pagnutti-aporto-un-minucioso-informe-sobre-la-violencia-de-genero/

### Chaco (`19177384B38635755405886`) and Río Negro (`19177384B64043309462999`)
Direction: better than national on official posture (climate signal only — see the resident-matter caveat).
- Chaco is described as **the most advanced NEA province on gender policy**, and Río Negro has its own
  provincial statute requiring **at least 1%** of public-administration posts be filled by travesti,
  transsexual and transgender people. Both were among the handful of provinces with such laws,
  and **none of them was being complied with** as of the surveys reviewed. — https://neahoy.com/2022/06/30/leyes-no-faltan-falta-que-se-cumplan-como-se-implementa-el-cupo-laboral-trans-en-el-nea/ · https://web.legisrn.gov.ar/legislativa/legislacion/documento?id=9985 · https://www.anred.org/1m-mapa-del-cupo-laboral-travesti-trans-en-argentina/
- **Caveat, in line with the brief:** employment quotas are a resident-only matter and a visitor never
  uses them. They are recorded here **only** as evidence of which provincial governments were willing
  to legislate visibly on trans inclusion, because that is the closest thing Argentina has to a
  province-level official-will index.

### National violence frame (for all 23 units)
- The Supreme Court's Oficina de la Mujer has counted **travesticidios/transfemicidios separately since
  2016** in the Registro Nacional de Femicidios de la Justicia Argentina. — https://om.csjn.gob.ar/consultaTalleresWeb/public/documentoConsulta/verDocumentoById?idDocumento=336
- NGO registries: MuMaLa's national register (2024 full-year, and 2026 quarterly editions) lists named
  trans/travesti victims **by province** — e.g. Guadalupe Arce (Córdoba), Mailén Martínez (Santa Fe and
  Entre Ríos), Analía Urretavizcaya, Nerina Córdoba, Camila Fresco (Buenos Aires), M. Luisa Martínez
  (Río Negro and La Pampa), Maria Jose Zochi (Santa Fe). — https://elresaltador.com.ar/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Registro-femicidios-Observatorio-Nacional-MuMaLa-2024.docx.pdf · https://elresaltador.com.ar/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/7_an%CC%83os_del_NiUnaMenos_Registro_femicidios_Observatorio_Nacional.pdf · http://libresdelsur.org.ar/wp-content/uploads/Reporte-femicidios-Observatorio-Nacional-MuMaLa-Marzo-2026.docx.pdf
- Observatorio Ahora Que Sí Nos Ven: of **208 gender-motivated deaths 1 Jan–30 Oct 2025**, **3 were
  travesticidios/transfemicidios** (174 direct femicides, 23 linked, 8 instigations to suicide) — i.e.
  trans deaths are a small share of Argentina's very large gendered-homicide total, and one year's
  provincial distribution is statistically thin. — https://ahoraquesinosven.com.ar/reports/208-femicidios-transfemicidios-y-travesticidios-en-2025

## Regions with no sub-national signal

list), Formosa, Jujuy, La Pampa, La Roja [La Rioja], Mendoza, Misiones, Neuquén, Salta, San Juan, San

Note that most of these retain codes-of-faltas police powers of the type described above, so
"no signal" here means "no documentation", not "no exposure".

## Urban / rural and general notes
- **The right axis for Argentina is "which province's police and which province's contravention code",
  not "which province's law".** No province criminalises gender identity; several provinces retain
  administrative-offence regimes and preventive-policing practices that produce the same effect, and
  the province of Buenos Aires is the best-documented case (La Plata, Mar del Plata, Esteban
  Echeverría). CABA's Constitution neighbourhood is the best-documented urban case.
- **National-level deterioration is the headline risk item and it is not regional:** in February 2025
  the executive issued **DNU 62/2025 amending the Gender Identity Law** (banning hormonal/surgical
  transition treatments for under-18s) and **a second decree on the housing of trans people in
  prisons**; legal experts called both decrees unlawful and unconstitutional, the courts were asked to
  intervene, and the prison-placement change matters directly to anyone detained. Because the Registry
  (RENAPER) and the federal prison service are national, this shifts **all 23 units** together rather
  than creating divergence — but an assessor reading the 2012-law-as-written will overstate Argentina's
  current protection. — https://www.tiempoar.com.ar/ta_article/milei-modifico-por-decreto-la-ley-de-identidad-de-genero/ · https://agenciapresentes.org/2025/02/06/milei-modifico-por-decreto-la-ley-de-identidad-de-genero-que-cambio-y-por-que-piden-intervenga-la-justicia/ · https://revistaquorum.com.ar/2025/02/06/milei-modifica-la-ley-de-identidad-de-genero-y-prohibe-tratamientos-de-transicion-en-menores/
- **Argentina's numbers are small enough that a single provincial case is a real signal** (the
  small-region clause): 3 travesticidios nationally in ten months means that one documented case in
  Chaco or Tucumán is not noise. That cuts both ways — do not treat a province's zero as safety.
- City-vs-province-countryside: almost all documented incidents occur in the great metros (AMBA,
  Rosario, Córdoba capital, Mendoza's centre) and in the Atlantic resort cities (Mar del Plata) where
  trans sex work is policed seasonally. Interior provinces have neither the community presence nor the
  reporting infrastructure, which is why they are absent from every dataset.
- Buenos Aires province's registry guidance remains openly available and its defensoría actively
  documents discrimination cases — protective institutions coexisting with abusive policing, in the
  same jurisdiction.
