# AUS — Australia · ADM1 research
Researched: 2026-09-17 · Units in boundary data: 9 usable, 0 excluded

## Richness classification
RICH — 6 of 9 units carry real, sourced, statute-level divergence (NSW, Victoria, Queensland,
Western Australia, Northern Territory, and the four-state prison-placement split), plus a
coronial finding of police failure toward missing trans women in Victoria and a sentencing
pattern in NSW. Australia has no state expression ban; the divergence is in **what hate speech
and coercion laws cover gender identity, which way those laws moved in 2022–2026, and how
detention is handled** — all of which a detained or reporting-touching visitor can meet.

## Different-legal-system regions
**None.** All nine units sit under one federal Constitution, one Migration Act and one Criminal
Code; there is no religious or customary criminal jurisdiction. Relevant structural notes:
- New South Wales, Victoria, Queensland, South Australia and Tasmania are **common-law states with
  no federal-equivalent statutory bill of rights**, while Victoria (Charter of Human Rights and
  Responsibilities 2006), Queensland (Human Rights Act 2019) and the ACT (Human Rights Act 2004)
  have their own rights statutes that bind public authorities — so the *same* conduct is legally
  reviewable in some states and not others.
- **Northern Territory** is the only jurisdiction whose parliament repealed protections rather than
  adding them (see below) — functionally a different legal environment for public speech about
  gender, though not a different legal system.
- Norfolk Island, Christmas Island and the other parts of "Other Territories" are governed by
  Commonwealth law with no local SOGIE-specific provisions; nothing scoreable was found.

## Regions with findings

### Northern Territory (`82085004B70364588595557`)
Direction: worse than national — the only Australian jurisdiction actively removing anti-discrimination protection.
- The Labor government's **Anti-Discrimination Amendment Act 2022** (assented 9 Dec 2022, key parts
  in force 2023/Jan 2024) had strengthened s.20A so that it was unlawful to "offend, insult,
  humiliate or intimidate" someone in public because of a protected attribute — **including gender
  identity** — and banned religious schools from faith-based hiring tests. — https://legislation.nt.gov.au/en/Bills/Anti-Discrimination-Amendment-Bill-2022-S-64?format=assented · https://www.abc.net.au/news/2025/03/02/nt-anti-discrimination-hate-speech-laws-repealed/104991288
- The Country Liberal Party government carried out a pre-election commitment to **reverse it**: the
  Anti-Discrimination Amendment Act 2025 (No. 27 of 2025, assented 30 Oct 2025; Part 3 in force
  27 July 2026) means it is **no longer unlawful to offend, insult, humiliate or intimidate a
  person in public on the basis of a protected attribute**, with the government framing this as
  "restoring freedom of speech and religion". — https://legislation.nt.gov.au/en/Bills/Anti-Discrimination-Amendment-Bill-2025-S28?format=assented · https://www.abc.net.au/news/2025/10/17/anti-discrimination-bill-passes-nt-parliament-free-speech/105903490 · https://vvv.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/nt/num_act/aaa202527o2025330/s4.html
- Practical effect for a visitor: in the NT, public verbal abuse and humiliation directed at
  someone's gender expression has been deliberately moved outside the law, while other states
  brought it inside. Hate-crime penalty enhancements for SO/GI orientation remain (2022 amendment,
  effective 2023). — https://www.equaldex.com/region/northern-territory
- Violence signal (weak, single case, recorded for completeness only): a Tiwi Sistergirl was
  stabbed to death in her own backyard in Darwin on 7 Oct 2024, allegedly by a relative; the
  community mourned her without publishing her identity for cultural reasons. This is one domestic
  incident and does **not** support a regional finding on its own. — https://tdor.translivesmatter.info/reports/2024/10/07/name-withheld_darwin-northern-territory-australia_1dea8903

### Victoria (`82085004B60194602145168`)
Direction: mixed — the country's strongest new speech protections, alongside a coroner's finding that police failed missing trans women.
- **Strongest vilification law in Australia:** the Justice Legislation Amendment (Anti-Vilification
  and Social Cohesion) Act 2025 (No. 11 of 2025), passed April 2025, created both **civil and
  criminal** vilification offences and extended protection beyond race and religion to
  **disability, gender identity, sex, sex characteristics, sexual orientation and personal
  association**, folding the old Racial and Religious Tolerance Act into the Equal Opportunity Act
  2010 (new Part 6A, s.102B). Public incitement against a trans person is now chargeable in
  Victoria in a way it is not elsewhere. — https://posh.austlii.edu.au/cgi-bin/viewdoc/au/legis/vic/num_act/jlaasca202511o2025645/ · http://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/vic/num_act/jlaasca202511o2025645/s9.html · https://www.humanrights.vic.gov.au/resources/fact-sheet-explaining-victorias-new-anti-hate-laws/ · https://engage.vic.gov.au/anti-vilification-reforms
- **Damning institutional response, but with reform attached:** a Victorian coronial inquest into
  the deaths of **five young transgender women** (Bridget Flack, Heather Pierard, Matt Byrne,
  Natalie Wilson and "AS") found Victoria Police failed the search for Flack; Coroner Ingrid Giles
  issued nine recommendations and called for an **overhaul of missing-persons risk assessment** for
  trans people; advocates called the police handling "a terrible miscarriage of justice". — https://www.abc.net.au/news/2024/08/29/melbourne-inquest-into-transgender-deaths/104284898 · https://www.theage.com.au/national/coroner-urges-missing-persons-overhaul-after-probe-into-deaths-of-young-trans-women-20240829-p5k68l.html · https://www.abc.net.au/news/2023/11/29/advocates-criticise-victoria-police-bridget-flack-investigation/103167696
- Victoria is also the case study in the academic literature for how thin LGBT prison protections
  are in practice (policies not legally enforceable). — https://doi.org/10.1177/1037969x241231007

### New South Wales (`82085004B21656044714531`)
Direction: better than national on law, worse on violent-crime pattern.
- **Criminal ban on conversion practices:** the Conversion Practices Ban Act 2024 (No. 19 of 2024,
  bipartisan) came into force **4 April 2025**, making practices directed at changing or suppressing
  sexual orientation or gender identity punishable by **up to five years' imprisonment**, including
  taking a person interstate/overseas for the purpose or engaging a provider outside NSW; a civil
  complaints scheme runs through Anti-Discrimination NSW. — https://www.nsw.gov.au/ministerial-releases/harmful-lgbtq-conversion-practices-now-banned-nsw · https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/whole/html/inforce/2024/04/03/act-2024-019 · https://antidiscrimination.nsw.gov.au/discrimination/conversion-practices/frequently-asked-questions.html
- **Killing pattern concentrated on trans women in sex work, with lenient sentencing:** Kimberley
  McRae was killed in her Coogee apartment in January 2020; the accused admitted killing her **after
  learning she was transgender** and had a manslaughter plea rejected (murder trial, NSW Supreme
  Court). Mhelody Bruno, a 25-year-old Filipina trans woman who had arrived in Australia ~6 weeks
  earlier, was strangled and her killer was jailed for only **22 months** on a consensual-
  strangulation account that ABC reporting showed the court never heard contested. Two killings of
  trans women in the same state within months, with sentencing outcomes advocates characterised as
  inadequate → recorded as a pattern plus a contested institutional response, not as one story. — https://qnews.com.au/man-admits-to-killing-sydney-trans-woman-kimberley-mcrae/ · https://www.abc.net.au/news/2021/07/02/background-briefing-mhelody-bruno-investigation/100256330
- NSW also holds an explicit self-identification prison-placement policy (see the grouped entry).

### Queensland (`82085004B13995298844162`)
Direction: better than national — first in Australia to outlaw conversion practices, with a full anti-discrimination rewrite drafted.
- **Public Health Act 2005 Chapter 5B (s.213F)**, in force 20 August 2020, prohibits health service
  providers from performing conversion therapy, defined to include attempts to change or suppress
  sexual orientation **or gender identity**, with named prohibited techniques. — https://www.health.qld.gov.au/system-governance/legislation/specific/public-health-act/prohibition-of-conversion-practices · http://www5.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/qld/consol_act/pha2005126/s213f.html
- A complete replacement statute, the **Anti-Discrimination Bill 2024**, was introduced with a
  modernised "gender identity" attribute aligned to the Yogyakarta Principles and separated from
  "variants of sex characteristics", plus vilification provisions; it was still in consultation at
  the time of the documents reviewed. — https://www.publications.qld.gov.au/ckan-publications-attachments-prod/resources/1e94a0ee-e203-47db-8431-5262a2727486/anti-discrimination-bill-2024-consultation-guide.pdf · https://database.ilga.org/api/downloader/download/1/AU-QLD%20-%20LEG%20-%20Anti-Discrimination%20Bill%20(2024)%20-%20OR-OFF(en).pdf

### Western Australia (`82085004B93533858076668`)
Direction: better than its older framework, weaker vocabulary than the eastern states.
- WA's Equal Opportunity Act 1984 protects the attribute of **"gender history"** (a person who
  "identifies as a member of the opposite sex by living, or seeking to live, as a member of the
  opposite sex") rather than "gender identity" — a narrower and medically-inflected formulation than
  Victoria/Queensland, with statutory exceptions and no equivalent vilification offence. — https://www.legislation.wa.gov.au/legislation/prod/filestore.nsf/FileURL/mrdoc_48241.pdf/$FILE/Equal%20Opportunity%20Act%201984%20-%20%5B07-i0-00%5D.pdf?OpenElement= · https://www.wa.gov.au/government/publications/gender-history-discrimination-information · https://justiceconnect.org.au/resources/how-to-deal-with-gender-discrimination-and-transphobia-wa/
- Reform in progress: the government removed medical/surgical prerequisites for registering a sex
  or gender change (April 2024 announcement implementing Law Reform Commission recommendations), and
  the **Births, Deaths and Marriages Registration Amendment (Sex or Gender Changes) Act 2024**
  received assent on **26 September 2024**, with substantive commencement by proclamation — which
  also feeds back into what "gender history" means for complaints. — https://www.wa.gov.au/government/media-statements/Cook-Labor-Government/Reforms-to-remove-barriers-for-LGBTQIA+-community--20240416 · https://www.wa.gov.au/government/publications/equal-opportunity-commission-fact-sheet-gender-reassigned-person-discrimination

### Prison placement — a four-way split across states
Direction: mixed (jurisdiction-dependent) — the most operationally different rule in Australia for anyone detained.
- Placement rules **differ from state to state**: NSW, Victoria, the ACT and Tasmania use an explicit
  **self-identification** basis, while in other jurisdictions placement turns on **whether someone
  has had surgical intervention** or on case-by-case assessment — so "some trans women can be placed
  in men's prisons", and which ones depends on which state border was crossed. — https://www.abc.net.au/news/2020/07/03/why-are-transgender-women-jailed-in-mens-prisons-in-australia/12416562
- Academic critical reviews of corrections policy across Australian jurisdictions confirm the
  variation is real and material: policies "vary in their availability, breadth and
  appropriateness", and trans prisoners face disproportionate harm. — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10756016/ · https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11044746/
- Cross-cutting but state-administered: strip searching is documented by the Human Rights Law
  Centre (March 2026) as a gendered, state-perpetrated harm with disproportionate impact on
  women, survivors of sexual violence and Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander prisoners — the
  practical risk a detained gender-nonconforming visitor faces differs by which state's custodial
  rules apply. — https://www.hrlc.org.au/app/uploads/2026/03/Strip-searching-report.pdf

### Australian Capital Territory (`82085004B40257551482390`)
Direction: better than national — rights-statute jurisdiction with self-identification custody rules.
- The ACT has its own Human Rights Act 2004 requiring public authorities to act compatibly with
  rights, and it is one of the four jurisdictions with an explicit self-identification prison
  placement policy. — https://www.abc.net.au/news/2020/07/03/why-are-transgender-women-jailed-in-mens-prisons-in-australia/12416562
- No ACT-specific incident series was found.

## Regions with no sub-national signal
South Australia, Tasmania (beyond its inclusion in the self-identification custody group), and
Other Territories (Norfolk Island, Christmas Island, Cocos (Keeling), Jervis Bay) — no
region-specific statute, incident series or enforcement pattern was found for these units.

## Urban / rural and general notes
- **Australia's sub-national variation is mostly about speech/coercion law and detention, not about
  access to public space.** No Australian state or territory has banned toilet or locker-room access
  by gender identity, banned drag, restricted pride events, or created expression offences — so
  unlike the USA, the "worse" end of the Australian map is *removal of protection* (NT), not
  imposition of a criminal ban.
- **The city/countryside split matters more than the state border for street-level risk.** Sydney's
  killing pattern centres on Kings Cross/Coogee-stype areas and sex-work exposure; Melbourne's
  coronial findings concern women whose disappearances were under-prioritised; the NT and remote
  Australia combine high general violent-crime rates (Alice Springs alone records ~2,600+ assaults a
  year) with thin community infrastructure and now weaker speech protections.
  — https://pfes.nt.gov.au/police/community-safety/nt-crime-statistics/alice-springs
- **Federal items apply uniformly and should not be regionalised:** marriage equality (2017), the
  federal Sex Discrimination Act 1984 covering sexual orientation, gender identity and intersex
  status in every state and territory, passport X markers, and the Administrative Appeals Tribunal
  tightening of gender-recognition review (2024). The state layer sits on top of that federal floor.
  — https://justiceconnect.org.au/resources/how-to-deal-with-gender-discrimination-and-transphobia-wa/
- **Australian advocacy bodies publish travel alerts for other countries, not for domestic
  regions** (e.g. Equality Australia's US Travel Alert of 17 March 2026), so there is no internal
  advisory geography to mine here. — https://stunmagazine.com.au/equality-australia-issues-us-travel-warning/
- Time-sensitivity: NT's rollback Part 3 commenced 27 July 2026 and Victoria's vilification offences
  are new, so 2026 is the year the Australian map actually diverged; older state-neutral assessments
  are now stale.
