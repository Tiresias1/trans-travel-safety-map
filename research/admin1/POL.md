# POL — Poland · ADM1 research
Researched: 2026-09-17 · Units in boundary data: 16 usable, 0 excluded

## Richness classification
RICH — Poland is the rare case where the sub-national legal/official distinction is fully documented
at unit level: **five voivodeships and roughly 100 municipalities formally adopted "LGBT-ideology-free"
resolutions covering about a third of the country's territory**, they were struck down by the courts
up to the Supreme Administrative Court, the EU leveraged membership funds against them, and the last
one standing was repealed only in April 2025. Plus a documented attack history on equality marches in
Podlaskie. Direction within units is real but the resolutions are now formally gone — the finding is
**coerced compliance**, not changed climate.

## Different-legal-system regions
**None.** Poland has one penal code and one administrative-court system; no religious or customary
criminal jurisdiction. The relevant legal fact runs the other way: the Roman Catholic Church has no
formal legal jurisdiction over morals here, but the **episcopate's public positions and the
church-adjacent civic committees were the institutional vehicle behind the "anti-LGBT ideology"
resolutions**, and one counter-demonstration at the Białystok march was a **prayer vigil at the
Cathedral Basilica** — i.e. a religious institution operating inside the public-order geography of a
specific voivodeship. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bia%C5%82ystok_equality_march

## Regions with findings

### Subcarpathian Voivodeship (`17162690B44985096553766`) — Podkarpackie
Direction: worse than national — the last holdout, and the epicentre of the resolutions.
- One of the **five voivodeships** that had adopted an "anti-LGBT ideology" resolution by mid-2020; it
  **repealed the designation on 20 September 2021** under direct pressure from the European
  Commission over regional development funds. — https://www.them.us/story/polish-province-rejects-lgbt-free-zone · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT-free_zone
- **Łańcut county (Podkarpackie) was the last local authority in Poland still holding an anti-LGBT
  "charter of the family" resolution**, and repealed it only on **24 April 2025**, at an extraordinary
  session with that single item on the agenda — six years after the phenomenon began. — https://notesfrompoland.com/2025/04/27/polands-last-anti-lgbt-resolution-repealed/ · https://tvpworld.com/86360798/poland-abolishes-last-lgbt-free-zone-
- **Rzeszów, the voivodeship capital, banned its Equality March outright**: mayor Tadeusz Ferenc
  prohibited the march in June 2019, explaining that "after consultations with the police we know it
  would not be possible to guarantee full safety for participants and residents" — the ban itself
  rather than protection. The regional SLD called the decision "undemocratic"; the district court
  subsequently **overturned the ban** on the organisers' appeal and the march took place. — https://rzeszow-news.pl/sad-okregowy-uchylil-zakaz-ii-marszu-rownosci-w-rzeszowie-jest-decyzja/ · https://www.gazetaprawna.pl/wiadomosci/artykuly/1416613,parada-rownosci-w-rzeszowie-tadeusz-ferenc.html

### Lublin Voivodeship (`17162690B69262761487853`) — Lubelskie
Direction: worse than national.
- A voivodeship-level "LGBT-free" resolution was in force; **repealed 20 September 2021** alongside
  Podkarpackie and Małopolska after EU demands. — https://www.them.us/story/polish-province-rejects-lgbt-free-zone · https://legalclarity.org/polands-lgbt-free-zones-from-declaration-to-repeal/
- **Świdnik, in Lublin county, was the first Polish municipality to adopt an anti-LGBT resolution**
  (March 2019 — "against homopropaganda, LGBT ideology and the early sexualisation of Polish
  children"), and its deputy council chair explained it explicitly as an act of opposition to the
  "unnecessary LGBT+ declaration" signed a month earlier by Warsaw's mayor. A county of ~40,000
  people became the template that ~100 authorities then copied. — https://www.zois-berlin.de/en/publications/polands-lgbt-free-zones-and-global-anti-gender-campaigns · https://www.polityka.pl/tygodnikpolityka/spoleczenstwo/1971724,1,gdzie-utknela-warszawska-karta-lgbt-pora-dzialac.read

### Lesser Poland Voivodeship (`17162690B67655395900928`) — Małopolska
Direction: worse than national.
- Voivodeship-level resolution **repealed 20 September 2021** under EU pressure. — https://www.them.us/story/polish-province-rejects-lgbt-free-zone
- **Kraków (the voivodeship's capital) moved the other way:** the newly elected mayor Aleksander
  Miszalski announced in May 2024 that he would become **the first Kraków mayor ever to attend the
  city's LGBT Equality March**, and would fly a rainbow flag at city hall — his predecessor Jacek
  Majchrowski (2002–2024) had only become an honorary patron of the march in 2021 and never attended.
  Within one unit: a repealed regional anti-LGBT resolution and a capital city whose mayor now
  marches. — https://notesfrompoland.com/2024/05/09/krakows-new-mayor-to-become-first-to-attend-citys-lgbt-parade/

### Łódź Voivodeship (`17162690B86810837764805`) and Świętokrzyskie Voivodeship (`17162690B20834640531728`)
Direction: worse than national — the other two voivodeships in the "five regions, a third of the
country" configuration that adopted anti-LGBT resolutions by June 2020. Both lie in the PiS heartland
belt running east of Łódź to the Carpathians. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT-free_zone · https://legalclarity.org/polands-lgbt-free-zones-from-declaration-to-repeal/

### Podlaskie Voivodeship (`17162690B23777949290372`) — Białystok
Direction: worse than national — the country's most violent assault on an equality march, and then a demonstrable improvement in police protection.
- **First Białystok Equality March, 20 July 2019:** roughly **1,000 marchers were attacked by thousands
  of far-right groups and football hooligans** who tried to block the route; police **detained 25
  people**, and **32 separate demonstrations had been registered that day, mostly against the march**,
  including a prayer vigil at the Cathedral Basilica and a picnic at the Branicki Palace. Solidarity
  marches followed across Poland. — https://www.cnn.com/2019/07/21/europe/bialystok-polish-lgbtq-pride-intl · https://www.reuters.com/article/world/polish-police-detain-25-after-attacks-on-equality-march-idUSKCN1UG0H0/ · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bia%C5%82ystok_equality_march
- **The religious dimension is documented at voivodeship scale:** on **7 July 2019 Archbishop Tadeusz
  Wojda issued a proclamation read in all churches in Białystok and the whole Podlaskie region** in the
  run-up to the march, while the **surrounding Białystok County (powiat) had been declared an LGBT-free
  zone in 2019** even though the city itself did not adopt one and its independently elected mayor
  Tadeusz Truskolaski was openly more tolerant — he nevertheless questioned whether the march was
  "needed" and the PiS-controlled city council filed a motion to stop it. The unit therefore contains a
  city-versus-county split mediated by the church, not a uniform position. — https://iiab.live/kiwix/content/wikipedia_en_all_maxi_2023-05/A/Bia%C5%82ystok_Equality_March · https://forsal.pl/artykuly/1423489,truskolaski-bialegostok-pis-probuje-dzis-przybrac-lagodna-maske-a-wczesniej-sam-podsycal-wrogie-nastroje-wobec-lgbt.html · https://wspolczesna.pl/sesja-rady-miasta-bialystok-spor-o-marsz-rownosci-wotum-zaufania-absolutorium-i-studium-zagospodarowania-przyjete-zdjecia/ar/c1-14221623
- **Fourth Equality March, 28 September 2024:** ~300 participants were escorted by **500 police
  officers**, counter-demonstrations formed at several points on the route, and the Podlaskie police
  reported **no incidents**. Same city, same march, five years apart — evidence that the
  state-protection variable can move even where the hostility does not. — https://isokolka.eu/podlaskie-2/59287-marsz-rownosci-w-bialymstoku-okolo-300-uczestnikow-ochranianych-przez-500-policjantow-foto · https://podlaskie24.pl/2024/09/28/ulicami-bialegostoku-przeszedl-iv-marsz-rownosci-film/

### Masovian Voivodeship (`17162690B84565288021286`) — Warsaw
Direction: better than national — the courts and the capital did the work of dismantling the zones.
- On **6 February 2024 Warsaw's Voivodeship Administrative Court repealed the last remaining
  "against LGBT ideology" document in Poland**. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT-free_zone · https://legalclarity.org/polands-lgbt-free-zones-from-declaration-to-repeal/
- **Warsaw is the origin point of the whole reaction.** In February 2019 Mayor Rafał Trzaskowski signed a
  12-point **LGBT+ Declaration/Charter** (security, education, culture, sport, administration,
  employment; drawn up with LGBT NGOs, including an intervention hostel and "lighthouse keeper"
  school projects), and the first anti-LGBT municipal resolution (Świdnik, March 2019) was explicitly
  framed as opposition to it. As of March 2020 activists' **Atlas of Hate** tool listed 80+ municipalities
  and four voivodeships with declarations. — https://www.blog.urbact.eu/2019/03/warsaw-lgbt-declaration/ · https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13642987.2022.2121708 · https://www.zois-berlin.de/en/publications/polands-lgbt-free-zones-and-global-anti-gender-campaigns
- Warsaw also carries the country's worst march-ban record: in November **2005 Mayor Lech Kaczyński banned
  the Parada Równości**, and the **ECtHR ruled in Bączkowski and Others v. Poland (2007)** that the ban
  violated Convention Articles 11, 13 and 14 — the foundational European judgment on Polish pride bans.
  — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equality_marches_in_Poland · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C4%85czkowski_and_Others_v._Poland
- **Silesian Voivodeship** also appears in the court history: the provincial administrative court
  sitting in **Gliwice** ruled on the Istebna gmina resolution, and the **Supreme Administrative Court's
  ruling of 28 June 2022 (III OSK 3746/21)** dismissed the appeals of four municipalities whose
  annulments had been ordered below — holding that the resolutions **"violate the dignity"** of LGBT
  people, and in an earlier May 2022 ruling that they "pose a risk of violence against LGBT people".
  Lower Silesian (`17162690B9130125042960`) and Opole (`17162690B67719337891950`) sit in the same
  judicial district history. — https://notesfrompoland.com/2022/06/29/polish-top-court-upholds-annulment-of-anti-lgbt-resolutions/ · https://www.jurist.org/news/2022/06/poland-court-orders-dissolution-of-lgbt-free-zones/ · https://notesfrompoland.com/2020/07/15/polish-courts-annul-lgbt-ideology-free-zones-finding-they-violate-constitution/ · https://notesfrompoland.com/2022/05/10/court-annuls-polish-anti-lgbt-resolutions-saying-they-discriminate-and-risk-violence/ · https://fra.europa.eu/en/caselaw-reference/poland-supreme-administrative-court-iii-osk-374621

### National frame that changes the meaning of every unit (`all 16`)
- The **penal code still has no hate-crime protection based on sexual orientation or gender
  identity**: Art. 119 and Arts. 256/257 cover national, ethnic, racial, political or religious
  grounds only. In April 2025 the **President referred to the Constitutional Tribunal** a parliamentary
  act of 6 March 2025 that would have added **disability, age, gender and/or sexual orientation** as
  protected grounds, asking whether it violates free speech; the Tribunal heard it on **8 July and 30
  September 2025**. So the country's single most important legal question for trans and queer safety is
  currently suspended at the constitutional court — and it applies everywhere equally. — https://www.jurist.org/news/2025/04/poland-president-asks-constitutional-tribunal-to-review-criminal-amendment-on-lgbtq-hate-crime/ · https://trybunal.gov.pl/en/news/press-releases/after-the-hearing/art/przeslanki-penalizacji-mowy-nienawisci-5
- A February 2025 government bill on **protecting minors from harmful online content** would impose
  verification duties on internet access providers with penalties; it is framed around pornography,
  not LGBT content, and is recorded here only as context for the "protection of minors" legislative
  genre that Polish anti-LGBT politics habitually uses. — https://www.gov.pl/web/premier/projekt-ustawy-o-ochronie-maloletnich-przed-dostepem-do-tresci-szkodliwych-w-internecie · https://dwfgroup.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2025/3/new-potential-obligations-regarding-the-protection-of-minors-from-harmful-online-content

## Urban / rural and general notes
- **The geography is east/south-east rural vs the big cities**, tracking the PiS vote share. At their
  peak the declarations covered ~1/3 of national territory across ~100 municipalities and 5
  voivodeships, concentrated in the eastern belt (Podkarpackie, Lubelskie, Świętokrzyskie, Małopolska,
  Łódź) plus scattered gminas elsewhere; by January 2020 four voivodeships were implicated, rising to
  five by June 2020. Every anti-LGBT resolution has now been repealed or annulled. The voivodeship
  boundary in this dataset is a *coarse but real* proxy for the divide. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT-free_zone · https://www.zois-berlin.de/en/publications/polands-lgbt-free-zones-and-global-anti-gender-campaigns · https://archive.ph/3S09E
- **Repeal was extrinsic, not conversion.** Courts annulled the resolutions as unconstitutional and
  dignity-violating; the Commission linked EU money to their removal; councils then complied. Two of
  the three September 2021 repeals happened days after the Commission's funding demand. Nothing in the
  sources indicates the underlying attitude moved, and Łańcut held out until April 2025. An assessor
  should treat the formal repeal as removing the *de jure* signal while leaving the *de facto* one. — https://notesfrompoland.com/2025/04/27/polands-last-anti-lgbt-resolution-repealed/ · https://www.them.us/story/polish-province-rejects-lgbt-free-zone
- **Equality marches are the reliable indicator of local risk**, and their history is city-specific:
  Warsaw banned in 2005 (overturned at the ECtHR), Rzeszów banned by its mayor in 2019, Białystok
  attacked by thousands in 2019, and marches spreading into conservative municipalities only from the
  late 2010s under police escort. Note that the *purpose* of the anti-LGBT resolutions was expressly to
  ban equality marches and LGBTQ events — so a unit with a resolution history had a declared intent to
  exclude a visible public LGBTQ presence. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equality_marches_in_Poland · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT-free_zone
  Where a march has happened without incident under heavy protection (Białystok 2024), the state is
  doing something right — record that as positive evidence.
- **No Polish region has a bathroom, expression or facility rule**, and no region criminalises
  anything trans-specific. Polish regional risk is (a) official declarations, (b) march violence, (c)
  the absence of any hate-crime category nationally, and (d) church-adjacent civic pressure. For a
  visitor the practical exposure is being visibly queer at a public event in an eastern city, and
  otherwise being in a country with no legal recognition of anti-trans motive in its penal code.
- **Data gap:** no voivodeship-level statistics on politically-motivated offences against trans people
  were retrievable, because the category does not exist in Polish crime classification. Absence of
  regional violence data here is a definitional artefact, not reassurance.
