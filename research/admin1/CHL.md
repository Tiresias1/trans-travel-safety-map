# CHL — Chile · ADM1 research
Researched: 2026-09-17 · Units in boundary data: 16 usable, 0 excluded

## Richness classification
RICH — Chile has a **complete region-by-region breakdown of anti-LGBTI discrimination cases** published
annually by Movilh (all 16 regions with shares of the national total and growth rates for 2024 and 2025),
**named judicial findings that turn on gender identity in two specific regions** (the Iquique Court of
Appeals annulling a trial because "femicide" covers only "a person biologically female"; the Magallanes
sentence for the attempted femicide of Kenia Chacón, which the national women's service is appealing), a
**hospital placing a trans woman in male beds in Biobío**, and **municipal divergence inside Región
Metropolitana** (Ñuñoa's first-in-country hate-speech ordinance versus Vitacura refusing to fly the flag).
Legal framework is national and protective; the variation is in enforcement, courts and municipalities.

## Different-legal-system regions
**None.** One code, one judiciary, no religious or customary criminal jurisdiction.
- The closest analogue is **indigenous customary authority in La Araucanía / Biobío** (Mapuche communities,
  *machitun* and *law mapu* dispute resolution) in a region under a prolonged security conflict with state
  of exception measures in force at various times. No trans-specific Mapuche-law jurisdiction was found, so
  this is a structural note only — relevant because La Araucanía is also the region with the sharpest
  recorded increase in homo/transphobic incidents (below).

### National frame (bounds all 16 units)
- **2025: 3,620 documented discrimination cases on the basis of sexual orientation, gender identity and
  expression — the highest figure on record, +27.1%, and 23% of all cases known in the last 24 years**;
  2024 was 2,847 (+78.7%, including **2 homicides, 44 stranger attacks, 2 assaults in police spaces, 89
  workplace and 65 educational abuses, 9 campaigns, 436 exclusions**), with **2024 being the first
  regression of LGBTIQ+ rights since the return of democracy**. — https://www.movilh.cl/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/XXIV.-Informe-Anual-de-DDHH-Diversidad-Sexual-y-de-Genero-2025-Movilh.pdf · https://www.movilh.cl/informe-ddhh-derechos-lgbtiq-retroceden-por-primera-vez-desde-la-recuperacion-de-la-democracia-y-los-casos-y-denuncias-por-homo-transfobia-aumentan-un-78-en-chile/
- **The 2025–26 national rollback is concrete:** Congress **removed the budget line for the PAIG gender-
  identity support programme** from the 2026 Budget Law, leaving trans children and their families in
  "state orphanhood", and the **Ministry of Health's circular N°8 restricted PAIG access for minors**,
  alongside a **legislative initiative (CEI-57) aimed at limiting trans children's rights**. — https://www.movilh.cl/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/XXIV.-Informe-Anual-de-DDHH-Diversidad-Sexual-y-de-Genero-2025-Movilh.pdf
- **Gender Identity Law 21.120 (in force 27 December 2019; seven years of application to 10 December 2025):

## Urban / rural and general notes
- **Chile's regional pattern is legible and large-scale: Valparaíso > Metropolitana >> Biobío > the rest** —
  but Movilh's own 2025 conclusion is that the meaningful change is *diffusion*, not concentration: the two
  big regions fell while all 14 others rose, which the organisation reads as hate moving to territories with
  less LGBTIQ+ visibility and less public debate, and as an attempt to obstruct equality work. A scorer
  should treat low-visibility regions as deteriorating, not calm.
- **Chile has the regionally strongest legal framework in South America** (Gender Identity Law 21.120,
  same-sex marriage 2022, anti-discrimination law 21.220 creating the National Disability and
  Anti-Discrimination Service) — which is precisely why the sharp findings here are about *courts and
  hospitals*, not legislatures: the Iquique annulment and the Concepción bed placement are institutions
  failing to apply existing law, and the Magallanes sentence is a court under-penalising.
- **For a visitor, the practical Chilean risk is administrative and medical**: document/ID mismatch at
  control points (compounded by the NSI issuance failures), and hospital or police custody placement — where
  Concepción's male-beds episode shows how an emergency room visit can go. Santiago, Valparaíso/Viña and
  Concepción are where a visitor will actually be.
- **Political direction:** the 2024 regression, the 2025–26 budget and circular attacks on the PAIG, and
  Republican councillors blocking municipal gestures (Vitacura) mark an official-climate turn under the
  current government that the region-level case data is tracking with a lag.
- Chile's national anchor is 0.723 — this dossier supports keeping Valparaíso, Biobío, Magallanes, Ñuble and
  La Araucanía below it and Santiago's progressive comunas above it, but the ADM1 layer is too coarse to
  express the latter.
