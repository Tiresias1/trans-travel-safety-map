# NGA — Nigeria · ADM1 research
Researched: 2026-09-17 · Units in boundary data: 37 usable, 0 excluded

## Richness classification
RICH on the axis that matters most for this map. **Twelve northern states operate a parallel system of
Sharia penal and criminal-procedure codes with their own courts** — a genuine different-legal-system
exception, with documented capital-punishment sentences for same-sex acts (Bauchi 2022) and statutory
death-by-stoning provisions in force in Kano, Sokoto and other states. Outside that block there is
also documented sub-national enforcement variation (Imo, Edo, Lagos, FCT/Abuja, Kano Hisbah). The
national SSMPA is uniform; the *sharia layer is the regional distinction*, and it is the single
highest-stakes one in the whole project.

## Different-legal-system regions
**Twelve states apply Sharia criminal law in parallel with the secular system** — these are "the only
states in Nigeria where Sharia courts have jurisdiction to try criminal cases":
**Bauchi, Borno, Gombe, Jigawa, Kaduna, Kano, Katsina, Kebbi, Niger, Sokoto, Yobe, Zamfara.**
- Reintroduced from 1999 (Zamfara first, then a wave under governors copying each other); **by 2002 all
  twelve had adopted some form of Sharia into their criminal legislation.** — https://www.hrw.org/reports/2004/nigeria0904/4.htm
- **Nigeria's legal system is expressly hybrid** — English common/statutory law, customary law and
  Islamic law — and since 1999 Sharia Penal Codes and Criminal Procedure Codes have operated in the 12
  northern states; USCIRF's study of implementation examined **Kano, Sokoto and Zamfara** using 173
  cases from 2017. — https://www.uscirf.gov/sites/default/files/USCIRF_ShariahLawinNigeria_report_120919%20v3R.pdf
- **What it does to trans/gender-nonconforming people specifically:** "Same-sex sex between men is
  criminalised and punishable by up to 14 years' imprisonment under secular laws. Islamic law (sharia),
  which applies to Muslims in the **12 northern states**, criminalises same-sex sexual acts **both
  between men and between women**" — i.e. the northern Sharia layer criminalises conduct the federal
  law does not, and applies it to women. The same UK CPIN records that the constitution has no
  protection for LGBT+ people and that there is "limited information on the number of arrests,
  prosecutions and convictions". — https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/685bd328c07c71e5a87097dd/NGA+CPIN+Sexual+orientation+and+gender+identity+or+expression.pdf
- **Statutory penalties differ state by state within the block** (see the Kano and Sokoto entries
  below): the same conduct can be 100 lashes plus one year, or stoning to death, depending on the state
  and on marital status. This is not rhetorical — it is written into each state's Sharia Penal Code Law.
- Caveat for the scorer: Sharia criminal jurisdiction applies to **Muslims**, and its practical reach
  over visitors is mediated by arrest practice (Hisbah religious police in Kano/Bauchi/Yobe/Sokoto/Zamfara)
  rather than by any formal "tourists excepted" rule. Some Muslims in central Nigeria also want Sharia;
  the twelve-state list is the formal legal boundary.

## Regions with findings

### Bauchi (`27671186B7584771598694`)
Direction: worse than national — the only Nigerian state with a **carried-out death-by-stoning sentence** for same-sex acts in the modern record.
- On **28 June 2022 a Sharia Court in Ningi LGA sentenced three men to death by stoning** — Malam Haruna,
  70, Abdullahi Abubakar Beti, 30, and Kamilu Ya'u, 20 — for homosexuality, convicted on their own
  admissions after arrest on 14 June 2022 by the **Hisbah religious police** (whose head, Adam Dan Kafi,
  announced the case). The judge, Munka'ilu Sabo-Ningi, cited **Section 134 of the Bauchi State Penal Law
  of 2001**. Widely reported as the first time the death penalty had actually been imposed for
  homosexuality in Nigeria. — https://www.reuters.com/world/africa/nigerian-islamic-court-orders-death-by-stoning-men-convicted-homosexuality-2022/07/02/ · https://guardian.ng/news/homosexuality-bauchi-shariah-court-sentences-three-to-death-by-stoning/ · https://www.thisdaylive.com/2022/07/03/in-bauchi-70-year-old-man-two-others-to-die-by-stoning-over-homosexuality/ · https://punchng.com/homosexuality-how-sharia-court-sentenced-septuagenarian-others-to-death-relatives/
- LGBTQ+ groups told Reuters the sentence "could trigger similar cases in the country's states that apply
  Islamic law and unleash a wave of homophobic violence" — the deterrent/incitement effect is itself a
  finding about the whole 12-state block. — https://www.reuters.com/article/world/us/feature-lgbtq-nigerians-fear-violence-after-sharia-court-death-sentence-idUSL8N2YP4IQ/
- **But the same state's Sharia appellate courts have repeatedly quashed such sentences:** Amnesty
  International recorded the **Upper Sharia Court of Bauchi State quashing Jubrin Babaji's death-by-stoning
  sentence for "sodomy"**, following the quashing of Yunusa Rafin Chiyawa's conviction on 14 November 2003
  — "This latest development follows a number of cases where convictions and sentences to death have been
  quashed on appeal." Record this as the mitigating institutional fact inside the harshest jurisdiction. — https://www.amnesty.org/ru/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/afr440092004en.pdf
- Ongoing lower-level practice: March 2014, an Upper Sharia Court in Unguwar Jaki, Bauchi, **flogged four
  men 15 lashes each and fined them ₦20,000** after a **trial held in secret** "for security reasons"; and
  the **Bauchi State Sharia Commission** charged a dozen men in January 2014 with belonging to a "gay
  club" said to be US-funded, seven of them released on bail secretly. — https://pmnewsnigeria.com/2014/03/06/4-men-flogged-for-homosexuality-in-bauchi/ · https://www.france24.com/en/20140307-nigeria-court-flogs-four-men-homosexuality · https://www.vanguardngr.com/2014/03/islamic-courts-release-7-gay-suspects-bail-secretly/

### Kano (`27671186B50148357657004`)
Direction: worse than national — the model state that started the Sharia wave's criminal code, with a graduated corporal-to-capital penalty.
- The **Sharia Penal Code Law 2000 took effect in Kano on 26 November 2000**: consensual same-sex
  activity punished with **100 lashes** if unmarried (plus up to one year's imprisonment), with
  **death** prescribed where the offender is or has been married. Kano is also the state where the
  Hisbah religious police are most active. — https://www.equaldex.com/log/14124 · https://www.uscirf.gov/sites/default/files/USCIRF_ShariahLawinNigeria_report_120919%20v3R.pdf

### Sokoto (`27671186B99510750195830`)
Direction: worse than national — the most capital-heavy text in the block.
- The **Sharia Penal Code Law 2000 took effect in Sokoto on 31 January 2001**; consensual same-sex
  activity is punished by **stoning to death**, with ta'azir punishment (up to 100 lashes and
  imprisonment) where the act involves a minor and an adult. — https://www.equaldex.com/log/14118 · https://www.uscirf.gov/sites/default/files/USCIRF_ShariahLawinNigeria_report_120919%20v3R.pdf

### Zamfara (`27671186B21163522287660`), and the remaining nine Sharia-criminal-law states
Direction: worse than national — shared statutory framework; Borno, Gombe, Jigawa, Kaduna, Katsina,
Kebbi, Niger, Yobe (`27671186B753479316714`, `27671186B90305463766462`,
`27671186B1226853346294`, `27671186B54051914323789`, `27671186B99661334382691`,
`27671186B53868438251790`, `27671186B79778155682183`, `27671186B69054806776702`).
- Zamfara's 2000 adoption is what triggered the cascade: governors elsewhere "capitalized on the mood"
  and introduced their own Sharia legislation, reaching twelve states by 2002. USCIRF's case-level
  implementation study covers **Kano, Sokoto and Zamfara**. Individual state-specific *trans*
  incidents for the other nine were not retrievable in English-language sources; the framework itself
  is the finding, exactly as the brief anticipates. — https://www.hrw.org/reports/2004/nigeria0904/4.htm · https://www.uscirf.gov/sites/default/files/USCIRF_ShariahLawinNigeria_report_120919%20v3R.pdf

### Abuja Federal Capital Territory (`27671186B76269877687363`)
Direction: worse than national — a killing of the most publicly visible trans person in the territory, followed by a refusal to investigate.
- **"Abuja Area Mama"**, a 33-year-old transgender woman / self-described "number one Abuja cross-dresser
  and queen of the street" with ~10,000 TikTok followers, was found **beaten and bruised beside the
  Katampe-Mabushi Expressway on 8 August 2024**, hours after posting a video captioned "getting ready to
  go and see my boyfriend"; she had previously expressed fear for her life. — https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c0rwg5krq98o · https://www.them.us/story/abuja-area-mama-tiktok-nigeria-found-dead · https://www.washingtonblade.com/2024/08/12/popular-gay-crossdresser-murdered-in-nigeria/
- **Damning response, and it is being litigated:** a human-rights group sued the Federal Government and
  the police **for failing to investigate**, seeking a declaration of rights violations and an order
  compelling investigation and prosecution after multiple petitions were ignored. The case qualifies the
  killing under the small-region/state-failure tests rather than leaving it as a lone incident. — https://www.legit.ng/nigeria/1668148-nigerian-government-police-dragged-court-death-tiktok-star-abuja-area-mama/
- Reporting on Abuja's queer scene documents the surrounding climate of **threats and raids** alongside
  informal community organising. — https://www.thedial.world/articles/news/issue-25/lgbtq-rights-nigeria-abuja-area-mama

### Imo (`27671186B8148656125930`) and Edo (`27671186B63712480376187`) — the non-Sharia southeast/west belt
Direction: worse than national — violent "community justice" and active SSMPA policing without Sharia courts.
- **Imo, April 2018:** two men were **stripped naked, beaten and paraded through the streets** by a crowd in
  Isiala Mbano after being accused of gay sex. **Imo, February 2025:** the State Police Command announced
  it had "foiled a same-sex party" at the Graziella Hotel, Orogwe, Owerri West, arresting multiple gay and
  lesbian suspects, explicitly describing the operation as enforcing the anti-gay law's 14-year penalty. — https://76crimes.com/2018/04/26/nigeria-2-men-stripped-paraded-for-alleged-sex/ · https://www.nigerianewspoint.org/2025/02/19/imo-police-command-foil-same-sex-party-in-owerri-arrest-many-gay-lesbian-suspects/
- **Edo, May 2018:** in Ekpoma, two men in their late twenties were discovered by their community; **one
  was lynched to death** and the other escaped, in coverage framed around the local concept of an
  "abominable act" defying "the traditional laws and heritage of the land" — customary authority operating
  as a moral-jurisdiction mechanism outside the Sharia belt. — https://nigerianobservernews.com/2018/05/abominable-act-in-ekpoma-community-2-male-gay-lovers-caught-making-love-one-lynched-to-death-other-escaped/
- Pattern across the two states (public parades in Imo 2018, arrests in Imo 2025, a lynching in Edo 2018)
  supports a regional finding rather than three isolated stories.

### Lagos (`27671186B19119389308001`)
Direction: worse than national on enforcement, better on community presence.
- The State Police Command launched an **intensified manhunt for two men, aged 43 and 54, accused of
  engaging in a same-sex relationship**, allegedly caught at a residence on 30 June 2025 — reported in
  May 2026 as a case that "underscores Nigeria's continued enforcement of laws criminalizing
  homosexuality". Lagos remains the centre of Nigeria's LGBTQ+ organising and of TIERs' casework. — https://independent.ng/lagos-police-intensify-search-for-two-men-accused-under-same-sex-prohibition-law/

### National enforcement data (bounds all 37 units)
- **TIERs (The Initiative for Equal Rights)**, the country's main SOGIESC monitor: its first report in 2014
  documented **124 cases** of human-rights violations in the immediate aftermath of the SSMPA; by the 2024
  reporting period (December 2023–August 2024) it had recorded **556 violations affecting 850 victims**,
  with the 2023 report compiling **996 verified cases** through 8 partner organisations — and stating
  plainly that these "represent only a fraction of the true scale", because fear of **outing** and
  reprisals silences victims. — https://theinitiativeforequalrights.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Human-Rights-Violation-Report-2024.pdf · https://theinitiativeforequalrights.org/wp-content/uploads/2024-09-2023-Human-Rights-Violation-Report.pdf
- **Police predation is documented as a national pattern with counts:** in 2024 alone TIERs tracked **89
  cases of extortion, 118 incidents of harassment and more than 20 unlawful detentions** of LGBTQ+ people
  by law-enforcement officials; people report being detained for "looking gay", beaten, and released only
  on payment of a bribe. — https://www.context.news/socioeconomic-inclusion/lgbtq-nigerians-recount-police-abuses-under-weaponised-law · https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/africa/2025-03-04-lgbtq-nigerians-recount-police-abuses-under-weaponised-law/
- The **SSMPA 2014** itself: up to 14 years for same-sex marriage *and* for public displays of same-sex
  affection, plus a ban on LGBTQ+ organisations — the legal hook used by every state command above. — https://pmnewsnigeria.com/2025/01/27/five-times-nigeria-crackdown-on-homosexuality/

## Regions with no sub-national signal
The remaining units produced **no state-specific trans-related source in this pass**: Abia, Adamawa,
Akwa Ibom, Anambra, Benue, Cross River, Delta, Ebonyi, Enugu, Kogi, Kwara, Nasarawa, Ogun, Ondo, Osun,
Oyo, Plateau, Rivers and Taraba. Note two things: (i) Borno, Gombe, Kaduna,
Katsina, Kebbi, Niger and Yobe are inside the Sharia block but had no incident documentation here — for
them "no signal" means no *incident*, not no *legal divergence*; (ii) Adamawa
(`27671186B49737451005073`) and other central-belt states are described in the literature as having
Muslim communities pressing for Sharia without formal adoption — a latent, undocumented distinction.

## Urban / rural and general notes
- **The north–south legal line is the map.** A trans visitor crossing from the Federal Capital Territory
  or Nasarawa north into Kano, Jigawa, Bauchi, Sokoto or Zamfara changes criminal-law systems, changes
  the arresting authority (from Police to Police **plus Hisbah**), and changes the maximum penalty for the
  same conduct from a 14-year secular term to lashes or death. No other ADM1 distinction in this project
  is that sharp.
- **Secular enforcement is geographically broad but institutionally thin:** the SSMPA is federal, and the
  documented arrests/parades are concentrated in the southeast and southwest (Imo, Edo, Lagos, Rivers),
  i.e. *outside* the Sharia block — so the correct reading is not "north bad, south safe" but "north has
  an additional, harsher jurisdiction; south has mob justice and police extortion".
- **Mob and "community" violence is a real mechanism and the state is implicated by omission:** public
  stripping and parading (Imo 2018), a lynching (Edo 2018), and the FCT's refusal to investigate a killing
  (2024, now in court). Under the brief's rules these count because the response is damning.
- **Reporting decay is severe.** TIERs explicitly says its numbers are a fraction of reality, and the UK
  CPIN says there is limited information on arrests/prosecutions/convictions. A state with no recorded
  case in this dossier has no *documentation*, not no risk — and the fear of being "outed" is itself the
  reason.
- **No trans-specific statistics exist for most states**: the SOGIESC monitoring lumps sexual orientation
  and gender identity/expression together, so a "trans" subtotal per state cannot be derived. Where the
  record is explicit about gender identity (Abuja Area Mama; CPIN's mention of arrest for "perceived
  gender identity"), it is noted above.
- Nigeria's national anchor is 0.12 → 0.084 (Do Not Travel). The Sharia block should score meaningfully
  below the national figure and the southeast/west states roughly at it; the difference between, say,
  Kano and Enugu is real but both are severe.
