# PHL — Philippines · ADM1 research
Researched: 2026-09-17 · Units in boundary data: 17 usable, 0 excluded

## Richness classification
RICH — the Philippines has **two real different-legal-system exceptions** (the expanding Shari'ah court
system, with the Bangsamoro parliament drafting *ta'zir* criminal offences, and the codified
customary-justice *bodong/pagta* system of the Cordillera), **a set of protective local ordinances**
concentrated in NCR, Central Visayas, Central Luzon, Ilocos and Calabarzon, and **a six-case series of
killings of transgender women across five named regions in 2024–2025** that the national Commission on
Human Rights itself condemned as marked by "cruelty and impunity". Evidence touches about 9 of 17 units.

## Different-legal-system regions
### ARMM (`36201628B49646111072560`) — Shari'ah courts (unit name in boundary data predates the 2019 BARMM reorganisation)
- The **Code of Muslim Personal Laws (P.D. 1083, 7 February 1977)** created **Shari'ah District Courts and
  Shari'ah Circuit Courts as courts of limited jurisdiction within the Philippine judicial system**, under
  Supreme Court supervision, applying **only to Muslims**; the original five Shari'ah judicial districts all
  have territorial jurisdiction in Mindanao. Codified coverage is personal law — "personal status, marriage
  and divorce, matrimonial and family relations, succession and inheritance, and property relations between
  spouses". — https://www.lawphil.net/statutes/presdecs/pd1977/pd_1083_1977.html · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_of_Muslim_Personal_Laws · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shari'ah_in_the_Philippines
- **ARMM/BARMM is an autonomous region whose justice system is expressly plural:** the **Bangsamoro
  Administrative Code (Bangsamoro Autonomy Act No. 13, begun 29 March 2019), Title V** provides that "the
  dispensation of justice in the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region shall be in consonance with the Constitution,
  **Shari'ah, traditional or tribal laws**, and other relevant laws", while guaranteeing religious freedom. — https://officialgazette.bangsamoro.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/BAA-13.pdf · https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/phi204825.pdf
- **Criminal jurisdiction is the live frontier:** **Parliament Bill No. 473**, published for a comprehensive
  **Bangsamoro Shari'ah Justice System Code**, would govern "Shari'ah civil actions, Islamic commercial
  transactions, supplementary Muslim family relations, **ta'zir offenses**, alternative dispute resolution,
  Agama Arbitration" — *ta'zir* being the category of Sharia **criminal** offences under which morality
  offences against same-sex conduct and gender expression are prosecuted in comparable systems. — https://parliament.bangsamoro.gov.ph/2026/07/14/proposed-bangsamoro-shariah-justice-system-code-seeks-broader-legal-framework-for-shariah-justice/
- **The system is expanding nationally, not just in BARMM:** in **August 2024 President Marcos Jr. signed a
  law creating three new Shari'ah judicial districts and twelve circuit courts**, meaning "the Islamic court
  system will spread beyond the Muslim-majority territory of BARMM"; and **Republic Act No. 12304 (18
  September 2025)** amended the National Commission on Muslim Filipinos Act to provide **equitable access to
  Shari'ah courts**. The Supreme Court's Court En Banc resolved on 21 January to operationalise the
  long-delayed **Shari'ah High Court** in BARMM. — https://www.christianitytoday.com/2024/11/philippines-sharia-court-expansion-christians-muslim-barmm/ · https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2025/ra_12304_2025.html · https://ninetydegreesph.com/sc-initiates-process-to-establish-shariah-high-court-in-barmm/
- **Assessor's caveat, stated honestly:** no source in this pass establishes that Philippine Shari'ah courts
  currently prosecute same-sex conduct or gender expression — P.D. 1083 codified only personal law, and a
  Supreme Court separate opinion stresses "only a portion of the otherwise comprehensive system of shari'ah
  was expressly codified". **The framework fact is the finding**: an autonomous region plus a nationally
  expanding religious court system, with an active bill to add *ta'zir* criminal offences, in a country whose
  national score otherwise reflects no criminalisation. — https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2023/jul2023/gr_211089_leonen.html

### CAR (`36201628B92932511992503`) — codified customary justice, with the same system reaching Ilocos Region (`36201628B64564033926366`) via Abra/Ilocos Sur
- The **Kalinga *bodong*/pagta** system — a bilateral inter-tribal peace pact with its own holders,
  sanctions and dispute-resolution authority over "crimes and tribal-related offences" — was **formally
  codified by the Kalinga provincial government in February 2026 as a 137-page written *Pagta***, moving
  centuries of oral customary law into an official text "to guide future leaders". Kalinga board members state
  the bodong "is no longer recognized solely as a traditional peace pact" but is **recognised by Philippine
  courts and even abroad**. — https://bworldonline.com/the-nation/2026/02/11/729991/kalinga-peace-pact-system-now-codified/ · https://www.gurupress-cordillera.com/post/bm-donaal-kalinga-s-bodong-proves-indigenous-justice-works-even-recognized-beyond-philippine-cou
- It has **spread beyond the Cordillera**: the bodong "has been adopted and modified through the efforts of
  Itneg elders in the provinces of **Ilocos Sur and Abra**", now operating as the Bodong Indigenous Allied
  Group (BIAG) as an ADR institution "designed to complement existing judicial bodies of the state" — Abra
  and Ilocos Sur sit in the **Ilocos Region** unit. — https://www.ukdr.uplb.edu.ph/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=6839&context=journal-articles
- Academic work frames this as **legal pluralism in which indigenous and formal criminal justice coexist**,
  with police handling customary-law cases — i.e. real criminal-dispute jurisdiction outside the state
  system. — https://journals.aloysianpublications.com/index.php/articles/article/view/671 · https://doi.org/10.22161/ijels.104.51
- **No trans-specific bodong ruling or customary exclusion was documented**; the framework fact plus the
  constitutional recognition of indigenous justice (also echoed in the Bangsamoro Basic Law's IP-rights
  section) is the finding.

## Regions with findings

### NCR (`36201628B69282955287113`)
Direction: **better than national on law, worse than it looks on violence** — the protective ordinances and two of the killings are all here.
- **Quezon City** enacted **SP-3417, S-2025**, a comprehensive anti-discrimination ordinance on **sexual
  orientation, gender identity and expression and sex characteristics (SOGIESC)**, revising its 2014
  Gender-Fair Ordinance (SP-2357, S-2014), and in July 2025 became **the first local government unit in the
  country to enact a Gender-Inclusive Health Ordinance**, creating a framework for gender-affirming primary
  care, mental-health support and specialised services in city facilities. — https://quezoncity.gov.ph/public-notice/sp-3417-s-2025-an-ordinance-providing-for-a-comprehensive-anti-discrimination-policy-on-the-basis-of-sexual-orientation-gender-identity-and-expression-and-sex-characteristics-sogiesc-revising-f/ · https://quezoncity.gov.ph/quezon-city-expands-healthcare-access-with-landmark-gender-inclusive-ordinance/ · https://www.philstar.com/nation/2025/07/13/2457694/quezon-city-passes-gender-inclusive-health-ordinance
- **Killings in NCR, 2024–2025:** **Shaina Bayabay, 29**, strangled in **Pasig City** on 1 March 2024 during
  a drinking session; and **"Christine", 44**, a college-educated therapist, **stabbed** on 22 September 2025
  and found in a rented apartment in **Valenzuela City** after neighbours heard an argument — **two men were
  arrested**. — https://www.tdor2.translivesmatter.info/reports/2024/03/01/shaina-bayabay_pasig-city-metro-maniila-philippines_2b574f1e · https://www.tdor2.translivesmatter.info/reports/2025/09/22/christine_valenzuela-city-metro-manila-philippines_0b8a5c49
- **Institutional hostility in a Manila state college:** **EARIST** reportedly **denied enrollment to
  trans women from 2023 unless they cut their hair short**; a March 2024 video of a student being given a
  haircut in a classroom went viral, drawing condemnation from student groups and an **official Commission on
  Human Rights statement** noting that complaints continue despite repeated joint discussions and demand
  letters. — https://qa.philstar.com/nation/2024/03/15/2340700/earist-hit-denying-transwomen-enrollees · https://www.rappler.com/philippines/metro-manila/haircut-video-transgender-student-earist-triggers-lgbtq-community/ · https://chr.gov.ph/statement-of-the-commission-on-human-rights-over-a-viral-video-allegedly-showing-a-transgender-woman-being-compelled-to-get-a-haircut-as-a-condition-for-enrollment-at-a-school-in-manila/

### Soccsksargen (`36201628B76469343449086`)
Direction: worse than national — the only **targeted assassination** of a trans activist in the record.
- **23 June 2025, General Santos City:** a hooded gunman **shot Ali Jejhon Macalintal**, a transgender
  rights activist and radio commentator, several times at the acupuncture clinic she owned and fled on a
  motorcycle; police said they were investigating whether her **history of activism** was connected to the
  killing. Human Rights Watch called it an "apparent targeted killing". — https://www.hrw.org/news/2025/06/26/philippines-trans-rights-activist-murdered
- The **Commission on Human Rights publicly condemned the killings of two transgender women as "marked by
  cruelty and impunity"**, naming Macalintal's shooting — an official body using the word impunity, which is
  the damning-response marker the brief asks for. — https://mb.com.ph/2025/06/27/chr-condemns-killings-marked-with-cruelty-impunity-of-2-transgender-women

### Central Luzon (`36201628B47630810559445`) — Angeles City / Pampanga
Direction: worse than national — the sex-tourism/trafficking economy that a trans visitor is most likely to be drawn into or swept up by.
- **1 June 2025:** the **National Bureau of Investigation's Bataan District Office dismantled a human-
  trafficking operation at Field Sensation's Bar in Angeles City, Pampanga, arresting four people and
  rescuing 45 victims including 13 minors**; in **March 2025** the same office arrested a **foreign national
  (Shin Ki Jung)** and accomplices in Angeles City under the Expanded Anti-Trafficking in Persons Act,
  rescuing 12 victims including 5 minors. — https://nbi.gov.ph/press_releases/2025/06172025/8118/ · https://mb.com.ph/2025/06/17/nbi-arrests-4-sex-traffickers-rescues-45-victims-in-angeles-city · https://nbi.gov.ph/press_releases/2025/03102025/7756/
- Peer-reviewed research describes how the **US military-basing economy around Clark/Subic sustains sexual
  markets that "disproportionately exploit poor transgender Filipina women (trans-Pinays)"** — a structural
  account of why this specific region is where trans Filipinas are concentrated in tourist-facing sex work. — https://doi.org/10.52214/cja.v4i1.14226
- The US TIP Report 2024 confirms traffickers exploit victims "in tourist destinations and urban areas around
  the country". — https://2021-2025.state.gov/reports/2024-trafficking-in-persons-report/philippines/

### Central Visayas (`36201628B88329767373181`)
Direction: better than national — the most active provincial-level ordinance cluster outside Manila.
- **Mandaue City** signed the **Implementation Rules and Regulations of its Anti-Discrimination Ordinance** in
  July 2024, formalised with the city mayor, the KAABAG LGBT Federation and UNDP — an operational, not merely
  symbolic, local protection in the Cebu metro. — https://www.undp.org/philippines/press-releases/mandaue-city-strengthens-protection-against-lgbtq-discrimination
- The municipality of **Carmen, northern Cebu** approved a **Comprehensive Anti-Discrimination Ordinance
  protecting people on SOGIE grounds by unanimous vote** in the 16th Sangguniang Bayan regular session, days
  before the end of Pride month. — https://mytv.ph/article/cebu_town_passes_anti-discrimination_ordinance

### Western Visayas (`36201628B91065962446027`)
Direction: worse than national — documented campus exclusion, later reversed by advocacy.
- On **10 October 2022 the Capiz State University Office of Student Affairs informed students perceived as
  trans women that they would be barred from university premises unless they conformed on hair and clothing**;
  the Alternative Law Groups' case study documents the campaign that followed and a later milestone
  ("Khaz-JP's historic achievement") — a provincial, not metropolitan, pattern of institutional
  gender-expression policing. — https://alternativelawgroups.ph/capturing-equality-in-capiz-state-university-fighting-for-trans-students-right-to-dream/

### Ilocos Region (`36201628B64564033926366`)
Direction: better than national on paper.
- The **Pangasinan provincial board certified and affirmed Municipal Ordinance No. 15, s-2023 of Bayambang**
  "protecting the rights of lesbians, gays, bisexuals, transgenders, queers and intersex (LGBTQI)"
  (Provincial Board Resolution No. 799-2024, session of 19 August 2024) — provincial-level endorsement of a
  municipal LGBTQ+ protection ordinance in a conservative province; the same region also hosts the Abra/Ilocos
  Sur branch of the bodong system (see exceptions above). — https://www.pangasinan.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/799-2024-RES.pdf · https://www.ukdr.uplb.edu.ph/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=6839&context=journal-articles

### Calabarzon (`36201628B13373758884154`)
Direction: worse than national.
- **August 2025, Lipa City, Batangas:** **Jericho Elliorante, 25, newly crowned winner of an LGBTQ pageant in
  Barangay Sambat, Tanauan City, was found with 13 stab wounds**; police worked a homicide case within days of
  the crowning. — https://mb.com.ph/2025/08/13/lgbtq-pageant-winner-murdered-in-batangas

### Cagayan Valley (`36201628B63748016552864`)
Direction: worse than national.
- **31 July 2024, Ilagan City, Isabela:** **Morine "G." Cabansag, 40, was found dead in a cemetery after an
  alleged kidnapping and rape** in Centro San Antonio; three suspects were identified. — https://www.tdor2.translivesmatter.info/reports/2024/07/31/g-cabansag_ilagan-city-isabela-philippines_e03b76f6

### The violence series as a national finding (bounds all 17 units)
Six documented killings of trans women in eighteen months, in **five different regions** (NCR ×2,
Soccsksargen, Calabarzon, Cagayan Valley), with **arrests made in at least three of them** (Valenzuela,
Ilagan, and the Pasig drinking-session case) and **none reported as prosecuted to conviction**; plus a
targeted shooting of an activist in General Santos. That satisfies the pattern test, and the CHR's own
"cruelty and impunity" language is the official assessment. — https://www.hrw.org/news/2025/06/26/philippines-trans-rights-activist-murdered · https://mb.com.ph/2025/06/27/chr-condemns-killings-marked-with-cruelty-impunity-of-2-transgender-women

## Regions with no sub-national signal
Bicol Region, Caraga, Davao Region, Eastern Visayas, Mimaropa, Northern Mindanao, Zamboanga Peninsula —
no region-specific trans-related law, ordinance, incident series or enforcement finding located. Note that
Zamboanga Peninsula and Northern Mindanao adjoin BARMM and contain significant Muslim populations where
Shari'ah court jurisdiction now operates, so "no signal" there means no documentation, not no divergence.

## Urban / rural and general notes
- **The Philippines is best modelled as three zones, not seventeen regions:** (1) the **ordinance belt** —
  Metro Manila, Quezon City, Cebu's cities and Pangasinan towns, which legislate protections that a visitor
  can actually benefit from (a city health office that will treat you respectfully, an anti-discrimination
  complaint route); (2) the **tourist sex-economy belt** — Angeles/Clark, Subic, Metro Manila, Cebu, where
  trafficking enforcement operations and the militarised-intimacy literature locate trans Filipinas in
  maximum exposure; and (3) the **plural-law belt** — BARMM and the Cordillera — where the applicable
  dispute-resolution institution may not be a Philippine court at all.
- **There is no national anti-discrimination statute covering gender identity** and no SOGIE equality act in
  force, so the local ordinances are not decoration — they are the *only* enforceable protection, which is
  why the NCR/Central Visayas/Ilocos findings carry more weight than their page-count suggests.
- **Institutional hostility is mostly educational and religious rather than penal** (EARIST, Capiz State
  University), which matters for a visitor only as a climate signal; the CHR's willingness to issue public
  statements against both a haircut-for-enrollment practice and "impunity" in killings is a genuine
  counterweight and should be scored as good response.
- **The country's Catholic and evangelical political reaction to Shari'ah expansion is itself a documentable
  climate signal** — Christianity Today reports evangelical leaders divided and largely learning of the
  August 2024 expansion after the fact — useful context for how salient religious-law jurisdiction is in
  national politics. — https://www.christianitytoday.com/2024/11/philippines-sharia-court-expansion-christians-muslim-barmm/
- **DepEd's 2017 gender-responsive order has no implementing guidance on gender expression**, producing
  school-by-school variation nationwide — the underlying reason "which school/city" beats "which region" in
  this country. — https://www.rappler.com/newsbreak/in-depth/how-can-deped-make-schools-safe-space-lgbtq-students/
