# RUS — Russia · ADM1 research
Researched: 2026-09-17 · Units in boundary data: 83 usable, 0 excluded

## Richness classification
MEDIUM overall, with one RICH-grade exception — concentrated exactly as the brief anticipated. The repressive framework is **federal and
uniform** (transition ban, "LGBT propaganda" art. 6.21, "extremist movement" designation), so most of
the 83 units have no distinct sub-national legal signal. But two genuinely regional findings exist:
**Chechnya and the North Caucasus operate outside the federal pattern entirely** — documented
unofficial detention, torture, coerced front-line conscription, and the UK's own country-of-origin
guidance singles the region out as the exception to its "no real risk from the state elsewhere"
conclusion; and the **"extremism" prosecutions have identifiable regional venues** (Orenburg Oblast
produced the first criminal conviction set, with 32 people across **14 regions** charged since 2024,
one dying in detention).

## Different-legal-system regions
### Chechnya (`28173009B98838729156012`) — the mandatory exception, confirmed, plus a wider Caucasus pattern
- **Chechnya is legally part of the Russian federation but is governed in practice by Kadyrov's security
  apparatus operating outside it.** Human Rights Watch's foundational finding: in February 2017
  **Chechnya's law-enforcement and security officials launched an anti-gay purge**, rounding up dozens of
  men on suspicion of being gay, holding them **in unofficial detention facilities for days**,
  humiliating, starving and torturing them, **forcibly disappearing some**, and returning others to their
  families "barely alive from beatings" after **captors exposed them to their families as gay and
  encouraged relatives to carry out honour killings**. — https://www.hrw.org/report/2017/05/26/they-have-long-arms-and-they-can-find-me/anti-gay-purge-local-authorities-russias · https://www.hrw.org/sites/default/files/report_pdf/chechnya0517_web.pdf
- **Secret prisons are documented by name and location:** Novaya Gazeta's investigation identified a
  secret facility in the town of **Argun** — several formally abandoned buildings, a former military base
  in the 2000s — described as appearing "literally in every account" from survivors, and reporting in
  January 2019 that **persecution had resumed and the prison "is full again"**, while the head of
  Kadyrov's press service, Alvi Karimov, publicly denied that any unofficial places of detention exist
  ("there are no prisons or places of detention outside the FSIN system"). — https://novayagazeta.ru/articles/2017/04/04/72027-raspravy-nad-chechenskimi-geyami-publikuem-svidetelstva · https://novayagazeta.ru/articles/2019/01/11/148260-chechnya
- **The pattern continues and has mutated:** North Caucasus SOS (NC SOS) documented that Chechen security
  forces **blackmail detained homosexual men into "volunteering" for the front lines in Ukraine — at
  least seven such cases, one of the men already killed** — a method running back before 21 September
  2022. Memorial separately found a **mass wave of unlawful detentions sweeping Chechnya** tied to forced
  conscription after Kadyrov's October calls for Chechens to "go to war". — https://theins.press/en/news/274368 · https://oc-media.org/memorial-investigation-reveals-chechens-are-forcibly-conscripted-to-fight-in-ukraine/
- **Returnees are pursued:** in 2021 Kadyrov's aide Akhmed Dudayev announced that two young men —
  Magomadov and Isayev — who had been evacuated and then abducted back into Chechnya (at the Gudermes
  police station, where a second group of officers was waiting to take them) had "confessed" to aiding a
  teenager; the episode is a documented instance of the region reaching evacuated people abroad-adjacent. — https://www.the-independent.com/independentpremium/world/chechnya-gay-lgbt-kidnap-kadyrov-b1799707.html
- **Not only Chechnya:** North Caucasus SOS's human-rights defender David Isteev states flatly that
  **"there is no safe life for LGBTIQ+ people in the North Caucasus, and even private existence can
  trigger violence, detention, or forced return to families"**, and that "every year it gets worse". — https://humanrightshouse.org/interviews/every-year-it-gets-worse-protecting-lgbtiq-people-in-the-north-caucasus/
- **Other units with a real claim on this exception clause** (no separate written law, but the same
  security-and-custom enforcement logic plus a special-access regime): **Dagestan, Ingushetia, Kabardino-
  Balkaria, Karachay-Cherkessia, North Ossetia and the south-eastern part of Stavropol Krai bordering
  Chechnya** — all named in Western travel advisories as a distinct "North Caucasus" don't-travel zone,
  with Canada noting that **"you must obtain special permission from the Ministry of the Interior to enter
  certain areas"** in the republics of Chechnya, Dagestan and Ingushetia and Stavropol Krai. — https://travel.gc.ca/destinations/russia · https://www.smartraveller.gov.au/destinations/europe/russia
- **The decisive official-state source:** the UK Home Office Country Policy and Information Note on SOGIE
  (June 2025) concludes that in Russia **including the autonomous republic of Chechnya** LGBT+ people form
  a particular social group, and that "in general, lesbian, gay, bisexual persons and those from other
  sexual minorities **do not face a real risk of persecution or serious harm from the state solely due to
  their sexual orientation. However, those in the North Caucasus, trans people and those who are publicly
  active in LGBT+ advocacy are likely to face treatment amounting to persecution.** The same note adds that
  under the 'gay propaganda' law, public expression 'promoting' non-heterosexual relationships or gender
  transition is punishable by administrative fines or **detention of up to 15 days**, and that **repeated
  administrative offences may result in criminal prosecution with potential prison sentences of up to four
  years**, while the 2023 'extremist' designation "allows the state to classify LGBT+ [people]" as such.
  This is the most important sentence in the whole Russian dossier: an immigration authority's own
  assessment that (a) the country's risk is regional, with the Caucasus the exception, and (b)
  **trans people as a category are likely to face treatment amounting to persecution** — not merely
  "publicly active" ones. — https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/russia-country-policy-and-information-notes/country-policy-and-information-note-sexual-orientation-and-gender-identity-and-expression-june-2025-accessible · https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/684ae112efd2a4de6296ff43/RUS+CPIN+SOGIE.pdf
  *(quote verified against the gov.uk accessible HTML, 2026-09-17)*

### Federal laws that are uniform across all 83 units (so they cannot create regional variance)
- **Transition banned:** the federal law signed in 2023 prohibits medical interventions, including
  medicines, aimed at changing a person's gender, with a narrow exception for congenital anomalies; it
  also made legal gender change impossible, so documents can no longer be corrected. — http://en.kremlin.ru/acts/news/71795 · https://www.hrw.org/news/2025/06/30/russia-rising-toll-of-lgbt-extremism-designation
- **"Extremist" designation:** the Supreme Court ruled in November 2023 that the "international public LGBT
  movement" is extremist; it has since produced **101 extremism convictions (~98 administrative, 3
  criminal)**, and in April 2025 the Justice Ministry for the first time designated an **individual**
  activist (Vadim Vaganov) an extremist for queer activism. — https://www.hrw.org/news/2025/06/30/russia-rising-toll-of-lgbt-extremism-designation · https://novayagazeta.eu/en/articles/2025/04/21/in-legal-first-russian-justice-ministry-names-individual-lgbt-activist-as-extremist-en-news

## Regions with findings

### Orenburg Oblast (`28173009B76603557910928`)
Direction: worse than national — the region where the "LGBT extremism" label first became prison time.
- The **first criminal case in Russia concerning the "international LGBT public movement"** was filed
  against staff of the **Pose club in Orenburg**, submitted to court by the regional Investigative
  Committee (case received 10 July 2025). — https://ovd.info/en/express-news/2025/07/10/orenburg-city-southern-urals-court-has-received-russias-first-criminal-case
- **Verdict (June 2026):** the owner of the Pose bar, **Vyacheslav Khasanov, was sentenced to seven years**,
  with **two employees also receiving prison terms** and **more than one million rubles of alleged
  "proceeds of criminal activity" forfeited** — the country's first criminal 'LGBT extremism' sentencing,
  and it took place in this oblast, in a venue case (a bar) rather than an advocacy case. — https://novayagazeta.eu/en/articles/2026/06/29/russian-bar-employees-sentenced-to-up-to-seven-years-in-country-s-first-lgbt-extremism-case-en-news
- Why this matters for a visitor: the precedent is that **a commercial leisure venue can be prosecuted as
  an extremist organisation**, which is the risk a foreign visitor is most likely to walk into.

### Moscow (`28173009B19676032168465`)
Direction: worse than national — the venue for raids and the first high-profile organiser prosecution.
- The Moscow Times' 2025 survey of persecution is titled around **"arrests, club raids and extremism
  charges"**, and the first person charged with **creating** an extremist organisation on LGBT grounds
  (Kotov) was **arrested in Moscow in late December 2024**. Moscow also concentrates the medical and
  bureaucratic gatekeeping that the transition ban bites on. — https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2025/06/23/youre-a-second-class-citizen-arrests-club-raids-and-extremism-charges-define-lgbtq-life-in-russia-in-2025-a89514 · https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2025/02/19/russia-issues-first-prison-sentence-for-lgbt-extremism-a88080

### Yaroslavl Oblast (`28173009B38848907206629`)
Direction: worse than national — a prosecution for posts, showing how little conduct is required.
- The regional Investigative Committee opened a criminal case against LGBTQA+ activist **Yaroslav
  Sirotkin** for participation in an extremist organisation, based on **14 social-media posts and videos**
  of "propaganda of non-traditional sexual relationships" between December 2023 and January 2024. — https://www.jurist.org/news/2025/06/legal-proceeding-initiated-against-russia-activist-amid-lgbtqa-crackdown/

### Leningrad Oblast (`28173009B90810482533567`) and St Petersburg
Direction: mixed — a brutal killing of a gender-nonconforming person, prosecuted and sentenced.
- In **Murino (Leningrad Oblast)** on 26 February 2025, **Vyacheslav, who presented as a woman/gender-
  nonconforming person ("Arina") and sold sex**, was **beaten to death by 26-year-old Daniil Kuznetsov after
  a conflict involving his wife**, then **dismembered; body parts were packed into two suitcases and dumped
  from a bridge into the Neva** in St Petersburg, where a winter swimmer found them. On **9 September 2025
  the Vsevolozhsk City Court sentenced Kuznetsov to 11 years in a strict-regime colony**. — https://www.fontanka.ru/2025/09/09/76021148/ · https://www.kommersant.ru/doc/7552007
- Recorded as **positive institutional response** (arrest within days, prosecution, substantial sentence),
  while noting the press coverage was mocking ("there's a penis, so it's Arkady") and misgendering, and
  that the victim's sex work and the wife's own transition history complicated the narrative. This is one
  case; it is included because the *response* is the evidence, not the violence.

### Siberian courts (region not specified in source — likely Novosibirsk/Sverdlovsk, `28173009B27844369294009` / `28173009B92397886288731`)
Direction: worse than national.
- The **first prison sentence anywhere in Russia under the "LGBT extremism" ban** was handed down by **a
  court in Siberia** (reported January 2025 via Mediazona), predating the Orenburg club case. Also
  documented: trans Russians **tricked into "conversion therapy" in Siberia**. Because the source does not
  name the oblast, this is recorded as a Siberian federal-district finding, not attributed to a specific
  unit. — https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2025/02/19/russia-issues-first-prison-sentence-for-lgbt-extremism-a88080 · https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cl7yk2l925xo

### Regional distribution of repression, as far as it can be measured
- OVD-Info's September 2026 accounting: **since 2024, at least 32 people across 14 regions have faced
  LGBT-related extremism charges; 4 have been sentenced to actual prison terms, 4 remain in custody
  awaiting sentencing, 2 are under house arrest and 1 died in detention**; and **at least 134
  administrative cases over "LGBT symbols"** — including prosecutions over **rainbow earrings** and over
  "collaged images of hands framing a face accompanied by hearts". This is the closest thing to a
  per-region enforcement map that exists, and it shows repression dispersed across 14+ regions rather
  than concentrated in Moscow. — https://ovd.info/en/2026-09-07/under-umbrella-extremism-how-russia-bans-ideas-people-and-imaginary-movements
- Annual totals for 2025 (HRWF): **117 cases under Art. 6.21 ("propaganda"), 41 under Art. 20.3 (display
  of extremist symbols, predominantly rainbow symbols), 41 documented threats of criminal prosecution
  including against minors, and 17 criminal cases under extremism provisions**. In 2024 courts issued
  **146 "gay propaganda" fines**. — https://hrwf.eu/russia-study-on-the-situation-of-the-lgbtq-people-in-russia-in-2025/ · https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2025/06/23/youre-a-second-class-citizen-arrests-club-raids-and-extremism-charges-define-lgbtq-life-in-russia-in-2025-a89514
- A structured dataset of these administrative cases exists by region and article (Zenodo/GitHub), which is
  the resource to use if finer regional scoring is later wanted. — https://zenodo.org/records/18187982 · https://github.com/iurshina/lgbt_russian_courts/
- **Foreigner-specific federal rule that dominates the visitor calculation:** the US State Department
  advisory states that **foreigners found guilty of violating the "propaganda" law may be arrested and
  detained for up to 15 days and then deported**, and that displaying certain symbols including the
  rainbow flag is a crime; the FCDO advises against **all** travel to Russia and notes UK government
  support is "very limited" in parts of the country. — https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/russia.html · https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/russia

## Regions with no sub-national signal
The remaining ~76 units — the oblasts, krais and republics outside the North Caucasus and outside the
five venues named above — have **no region-specific trans-related evidence**. This is a genuine finding
rather than a gap: Russia's hostility is legislated federally and administered by federal security
services, so almost every unit legitimately inherits the national score. The one honest qualification is
that the **republics of the North Caucasus** (see above) are where inheritance understates risk.

## Urban / rural and general notes
- **The correct mental model is two-layer:** a uniform federal repression layer covering 83 units, and a
  **regional impunity layer** covering Chechnya and neighbouring republics, where the state's own
  instruments (unofficial detention sites, family exposure, honour killing, coerced conscription) operate
  outside legal process. The UK CPIN says this in effect: general risk from the state is *not*
  established across Russia, but the North Caucasus is different. Scoring all 83 units at the same value
  is the alternative error; so is inventing differences among, say, Tambov and Tula.
- **For a foreign visitor the federal layer is what bites:** 15 days' detention plus deportation under the
  propaganda law, the impossibility of correcting a document marker (so any Russian-issued interaction
  with police, hotels or transport will read a mismatched passport), and 146+ fines a year being issued
  with no requirement of any conduct beyond a symbol. Moscow and St Petersburg are where a visitor most
  plausibly encounters police; the Caucasus is where a visitor should not go at all.
- **Healthcare hostility is measurable but not regional:** a Novaya Gazeta Europe investigation found
  **one in five LGBTQ+ people in Russia had faced discrimination from doctors**, with transgender people
  the most affected and refusals of treatment rising after the 2023 ban — relevant only if a visitor needs
  emergency care mid-transition. — https://novayagazeta.eu/en/articles/2024/05/29/a-poor-bill-of-health-en
- **Data caveat, stated by the state source itself:** the UK CPIN notes **"there is no reliable
  information on the size of the LGBT+ population or how it is distributed across the country"**, so any
  attempt to normalise per-region incident counts would be building on a category error. Independent
  Russian monitoring (OVD-Info, Memorial, Social/Citizen Facts, the Zenodo court dataset) is the only
  regional evidence base, and it is now largely produced in exile.
- Danish Refugee Council's February 2026 country-of-origin report is the most current synthesis: no legal
  prohibition on being LGBT+ as such, but prosecutions of LGBT+ persons have escalated since 2023. — https://asyl.drc.ngo/media/y2bpyqku/lgbtplusrusland_february2026.pdf
