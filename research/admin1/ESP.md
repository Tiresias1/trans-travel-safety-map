# ESP — Spain · ADM1 research
Researched: 2026-09-17 · Units in boundary data: 19 usable, 0 excluded

## Richness classification
RICH — Spain's 19 autonomous communities have their **own LGTBI and gender-identity statutes**, so
the divergence is legislative and documentable per region: Madrid has actively amended its own trans
and LGTBI laws to strip out mandatory content, one community (Castilla y León) still has **no LGTBI
law at all**, the Interior Ministry publishes **hate-offence counts disaggregated by autonomous
community**, and a FELGTBI survey gives regional victimisation rates. Evidence quality is high but
its interpretation needs care: the regions with the highest *recorded* rates are the ones with the
best reporting machinery.

## Different-legal-system regions
**None in the mandatory-clause sense** — criminal law is entirely state (Código Penal, Ley de
Enjuiciamiento Criminal) and no autonomous community has its own penal code or religious courts. But
two features come closer here than in most countries:
- **Foral/charter fiscal regimes (Navarra, País Vasco)** and the transfer of substantial legislative
  competence under the Statutes of Autonomy mean the *administrative and public-service* law a
  visitor actually encounters genuinely differs community to community — each CCAA has its own
  equality police, its own health and education administration, and in two cases its own regional
  police force (**Ertzaintza** in the Basque Country, **Mossos d'Esquadra** in Catalunya, **Policia
  Foral de Navarra**) responsible for public-order response to gender-motivated offences.
- **Ceuta** (`25490228B48808997991554`) and **Melilla** (`25490228B18225280299410`) are autonomous *cities* rather than communities, with a distinctive
  religious-demographic makeup (Muslim, Hindu minorities under their own autonomous arrangements) and
  no regional LGTBI law; the border fences create an entirely different document-and-crossing
  environment from the rest of Spain.

## Regions with findings

### Comunidad de Madrid (`25490228B43154843394848`)
Direction: worse than national — the one community that has used its own legislative power to narrow trans law.
- **Ley 17/2023, de 27 de diciembre**, amending Madrid's own **Ley 2/2016 de Identidad y Expresión de
  Género e Igualdad Social y no Discriminación**: the stated purpose is to "correct aspects" of the
  2016 law that "may end up violating rights such as freedom of expression and of the press, and
  academic and educational freedom" — i.e. a region-specific amendment removing the parts of trans
  law that obliged educational institutions. — https://www.bocm.es/boletin/CM_Orden_BOCM/2023/12/29/BOCM-20231229-3.PDF
- **Ley 18/2023, de 27 de diciembre**, amending Madrid's **Ley 3/2016 de Protección Integral contra la
  LGTBIfobia** on the same reasoning; the amendment text itself concedes the original law "contained
  elements" requiring correction. Madrid is one of only two communities (with Valencia) to have
  layered laws in *both* the trans-specific and the general-LGTBI category — and it is the one that
  walked them back. — https://www.boe.es/eli/es-md/l/2023/12/27/18 · https://felgtbi.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Radiografia-politicas-publicas-educativas-2025.pdf
- **Ley 1/2022, de 10 de febrero, "Maestra de Libertad de Elección Educativa"** — the Madrid education
  law that centres parents as "primary responsible parties" and structures opt-outs, the vehicle
  through which the "pin parental" mechanism operated in Madrid schools. — https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2022-6768 · https://www.bocm.es/eli/es-md/l/2022/02/10/1/dof/spa/pdf
- Context for scoring: Madrid is also the community whose citizens have the highest reported use of
  LGTBI hate-crime reporting services, so low *recorded* hostility there is not available as an
  alibi; the divergence here is statutory, not statistical.

### Castilla y León (`25490228B8471183517505`)
Direction: worse than national — the only Spanish region with no LGTBI law of its own.
- Repeatedly described in the 2024–25 coverage as **"la única comunidad autónoma" of the autonomous
  Spain without its own LGTBI regulation**, blocked across several legislatures. — https://www.europapress.es/castilla-y-leon/noticia-abstencion-pp-posibilita-tramitar-ley-lgtbi-cyl-impulsada-psoe-20250611134515.html · https://www.larazon.es/castilla-y-leon/ley-lgtbi-sigue-curso-cortes-esto-que-plantea-norma-sus-novedades_202506116849bf523686c164f3898ec8.html · https://www.elespanol.com/castilla-y-leon/region/20250611/pp-abre-puerta-abstencion-aprobacion-ley-lgtbi-psoe-castilla-leon-solo-rechaza-vox/1003743799652_0.html
- The PSOE-proposed **ley de igualdad real y efectiva de las personas LGTBI+** (registered December
  2024) is explicitly framed around stopping the **"sexilio"** — LGBT people, especially young ones,
  leaving **rural** areas — and around breaking "a wall" in the region's society. Its take-up for
  processing (11 June 2025) succeeded only because the governing PP abstained while **Vox voted
  alone against**; Vox is on record rejecting LGTBI subsidies in the region. — https://www.salamancahoy.es/castillayleon/psoe-registra-proposicion-ley-evitar-sexilio-personas-20241220135343-nt.html · https://www.diariodecastillayleon.es/castilla-y-leon/250611/99563/pleno-luz-verde-tramita-ley-lgtbi-oposicion-vox-abstencion-pp.html · https://www.elnortedecastilla.es/castillayleon/luz-verde-tramite-ley-lgtbi-vox-abstencion-20250611194431-nt.html
- This is the region-specific fact with the most direct visitor relevance: an official political
  class that still has not enacted protection law, and a documented pattern of LGBT residents
  leaving for the cities.

### Comunitat Valenciana (`25490228B14384488423733`)
Direction: better than national on the statute book.
- Has a dedicated integral law on the **right to identity and gender expression** (Llei de 7 d'abril,
  integral del reconeixement del dret a la identitat i expressió de gènere), and is one of only two
  communities (with Madrid) to have enacted legislation in **both** the trans-specific and the
  general LGTBI-equality category. — https://plataformatrans.es/index.php/territorios/ · https://felgtbi.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Radiografia-politicas-publicas-educativas-2025.pdf

### Andalucía (`25490228B88831207743232`)
Direction: better than national — the oldest and deepest regional framework.
- **Ley 2/2014, de 8 de julio**, integral for non-discrimination on grounds of gender identity — one
  of the first trans-specific regional laws in Europe — followed by **Ley 8/2017, de 28 de diciembre**
  guaranteeing LGTBI rights and equal treatment for LGTBI people *and their families* (in force
  4 February 2018), and a **2024–2028 equality strategy** approved by the regional government on
  26 December 2024. — https://www.boe.es/eli/es-an/l/2017/12/28/8/con · https://plataformatrans.es/index.php/territorios/ · https://www.juntadeandalucia.es/boja/2025/1/BOJA25-001-00005-56572-01_00313522.pdf
- Visitor-relevant geography: Andalucía carries much of Spain's LGBTQ mass tourism (Mijas,
  Torremolinos, Seville), which sits alongside the "pin parental" conflicts that historically
  concentrated in Andalusian municipalities — the two coexist.

### País Vasco/Euskadi (`25490228B56567998760255`) and Navarra (`25490228B33348776534112`)
Direction: **mixed — recorded as the worst regions in Spain for hate offences, but the data
structure says this is a reporting-and-institution effect, not a safety deficit.**
- Interior Ministry's annual hate-crime report (1,955 recorded offences/incidents in 2024, itself
  down 13.8% on 2023) gives the **highest per-capita rates of hate incidents to Navarra and the
  Basque Country**, with Extremadura the lowest. Navarra "leads the ranking". — https://www.elmundo.es/espana/2025/07/18/687a1a0ffdddffdb0d8b45ae.html
- Both also operate dedicated institutional apparatus: the Basque Government's **Euskal LGBTIAQ+
  Behatokia** observatory maintains its own comparative legislation database, and Pamplona's
  **Observatorio contra la LGTBIfobia** publishes an annual report (2025 edition issued April 2026)
  whose own framing is that hate *discourse* precedes and normalises aggression. High counts in
  these two units should be read as "people report, and someone counts" — the opposite of the
  inference a naive scorer would draw. — https://lgtbi-behatokia.eus/normativas-lgbtiaq/normativas-autonomicas/ · https://www.pamplona.es/sites/default/files/2026-04/informe2025digital_0.pdf
- Navarra has its own regional police (Policia Foral) and the Basque Country the Ertzaintza, so
  first-response quality really is regionally determined.

### Ciudad Autónoma de Ceuta (`25490228B48808997991554`) / Ciudad Autónoma de Melilla (`25490228B18225280299410`)
Direction: worse than national — no incident evidence found; statute-book gap only.
- These are the two units that are not "comunidades autónomas" at all but autonomous cities, and they
  appear in the regional-law inventories as the gap cases alongside Castilla y León — no regional
  LGTBI statute. No per-CCAA hate-offence figure separating them was retrievable from the summary
  material, so this is recorded as a **framework-only finding, explicitly not an incident finding**. — https://felgtbi.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Radiografia-politicas-publicas-educativas-2025.pdf · https://planlgtbi.es/legislacion-lgtbi

### National victimisation baseline (applies to all 19 units)
- FELGTBI's **Estado del Odio 2026**: **44% of respondents from the LGTBI collective had experienced
  a hate-motivated incident** — 22% assault, 36% harassment, 29% discrimination, 11% all three; 10%
  had been harassed more than ten times in five years, 11% suffered physical violence in the same
  period. The report also stresses that LGTBIfobia among **minors** is rising and that hate discourse
  creates the climate for aggression. — https://felgtbi.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Estado-del-Odio-2026-1.pdf
- The 2024 Interior report shows total hate offences **falling 13.8%**, with racism/ xenophobia the
  largest group (804) and antisemitism the fastest-rising (+60.9%) — i.e. LGTBI-motivated offences are
  not the growth story nationally. — https://poruntrabajodignougt.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/INFORME_Evolucion_delitos_de_odio_2024.pdf · https://www.elmundo.es/espana/2025/07/18/687a1a0ffdddffdb0d8b45ae.html

## Regions with no sub-national signal
Aragón, Cantabria, Canarias, Extremadura, Galicia, Illes Balears, La Rioja, Principado de Asturias,
Región de Murcia, and — beyond the general inventory — Cataluña. Each has regional LGTBI or equality
legislation recorded in the FELGTBI/Plataforma Trans/Behatokia inventories, but no distinct
region-specific incident, ordinance or enforcement finding was located in this pass. Note that
**Extremadura has the lowest recorded per-capita hate-offence rate in Spain**, which given its
reporting infrastructure should not be read as protection.

## Urban / rural and general notes
- **Spain's real fault line is rural vs. city, and Castile y León's own politicians said so:** the
  purpose of the pending law is explicitly to stop the **"sexilio"** of LGTBI people from the
  **mundo rural**. Madrid, Barcelona, Valencia, Seville, Las Palmas have dense, visible communities and
  dedicated regional police hate-crime units; the emptying interior does not. The national anchor
  (0.88) is a city score much more than a countryside score. — https://www.salamancahoy.es/castillayleon/psoe-registra-proposicion-ley-evitar-sexilio-personas-20241220135343-nt.html
- **The 2023 national Ley Trans (Ley 4/2023) is implemented by the regions**, so a region can make it
  operate (Andalucía, Valencia, Basque Country) or hollow out its education-facing parts (Madrid).
  That is the mechanism of divergence in Spain: not who is criminalised, but who is taught to
  accommodate you.
- **No Spanish region has a bathroom ban, expression ban or facility restriction**, and no regional
  government has banned a Pride event. Nothing in this pass suggests the Spanish worst-case resembles
  the US worst-case; the Spanish downside is administrative coldness and rural exposure.
- **Reporting-rate inversion is the main scoring trap in this file.** Navarra and the Basque Country
  top the per-capita hate-offence table precisely because they run observatories and police units
  that count; Extremadura and Castilla y León, where no LGTBI law exists and no observatory operates,
  record the fewest. If an assessor has only the Interior dataset, they should treat high counts in
  Navarra/Euskadi as neutral-to-positive and the absence of counts in Castilla y León/Ceuta/Melilla as
  uninformative rather than reassuring.
- Spain's national anchor (0.90 originally, 0.879 after blind re-rating) already notes "political
  backlash" — this file localises that backlash to **Madrid (statutory rollback), Castilla y León
  (legislative vacuum + rural exodus), Vox-governed municipal councils**, and the Ceuta/Melilla
  statutory gap.
