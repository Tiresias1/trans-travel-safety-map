# TUR — Türkiye · ADM1 research
Researched: 2026-09-17 · Units in boundary data: 81 usable, 0 excluded

## Richness classification
RICH for the units that matter, POOR in coverage terms for the rest. Real sub-national evidence exists
for about 8 of 81 provinces: **İstanbul** (a district-level campaign of sealed apartments, drone-backed
blockades and mass ID-roundups of trans women), **Ankara, İzmir, Antalya, Gaziantep and Mersin**
(provincial governors' standing bans on LGBTI public activity, with documented violent dispersals and
mass detentions), **Diyarbakır** (a trans woman murdered on the day of a national anti-LGBT "Great
Family Gathering", then two aggravated life sentences), and **Antalya** again for a life sentence for the
murder of a trans woman. As the brief anticipates, the variation is essentially "Istanbul/Ankara/İzmir
versus the rest", so findings are reported at province level and the remaining 73 units are left at the
national score.

## Different-legal-system regions
**None.** Türkiye has one civil and criminal legal system; the 2015 constitutional-court era ended any
parallel jurisdiction, and no province has religious or customary criminal law. The Kurdish-majority
south-east differs in *security practice* (state of emergency legacies, village-guard system, high police
presence) rather than in law, and no trans-specific south-eastern legal divergence was found.

## Regions with findings

### İstanbul (`25984515B18786507826220`) — especially the district of Beyoğlu
Direction: worse than national — the clearest documented case in this whole project of a local authority
treating a neighbourhood as a place trans people must be removed from.
- **March 2024:** the **Beyoğlu District Governors' Office ordered the eviction of trans women from their
  apartments on Bayram Street on the grounds that they had been "leaning out of their windows"**; police and
  municipal staff forced them out and **sealed the apartments**. Lawyers from the Human Rights Association
  LGBTI+ Commission applied to the Istanbul Administrative Court to annul the decision and suspend its
  execution. — https://bianet.org/haber/houses-of-transgender-individuals-on-bayram-street-in-istanbul-s-beyoglu-sealed-292574 · https://turkishminute.com/2024/03/05/authorities-evict-trans-women-istanbul/
- **18 July 2025:** a street in Beyoğlu where many trans women live was **placed under police blockade,
  all entrances sealed, drones deployed overhead, and homes raided in a nighttime operation**; three people
  were detained, and residents told bianet "we're too afraid to work". — https://bianet.org/haber/drone-backed-police-operation-targets-trans-women-in-beyoglu-were-too-afraid-to-work-309873 · https://www.egerin.com/drone-backed-police-operation-targets-trans-women-in-beyoglu-were-too-afraid-to-work/
- **31 July 2025:** **around 20 trans women were rounded up in Beyoğlu during ordinary activity — on the
  street, while shopping, sitting in cafés — taken to the Taksim Şehit Haşim Usta police station for "GBT"
  (identity checks), held about two hours in the station courtyard, their names recorded and their videos
  filmed.** One of them: "this is profiling". No official reason for detention was given. — https://bianet.org/haber/istanbul-police-detain-trans-women-for-id-checks-amid-profiling-concerns-310067 · https://kaosgl1.org/en/single-news/trans-women-in-beyoglu-taken-to-the-police-station-on-grounds-of-identity-checks-our-names-were-recorded-our-videos-were-taken-this-is-profiling
- **Pride:** Istanbul's governorate banned Pride and all LGBTI events for a **ninth consecutive year**
  (statement: no permission for events that "threaten the institution of the family"); on 25 June 2023
  police **detained 113 people** attempting to march. In 2024 the governorate **closed roads and metro
  stations across Beyoğlu** on both the Trans Pride (23 June) and the 22nd LGBTI Pride (30 June); 46 people
  were detained at the Acıbadem march and five later faced judicial controls and **travel bans**. — https://www.hrw.org/news/2023/06/27/turkey-mass-detentions-pride-marches · https://www.amnesty.org/en/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/EUR4487602024ENGLISH.pdf · https://www.ilga-europe.org/blog/the-ilga-europe-turkey-pride-monitor/

### Ankara (`25984515B47806653651907`)
Direction: worse than national — standing gubernatorial ban.
- Ankara's governor issued a **ban on public activities by LGBTI persons**, part of a set of provincial
  bans (with Istanbul, İzmir, Antalya, Gaziantep and Mersin) recorded in US government reporting. At the
  22 June 2024 Ankara Pride attempt, police blocked the march; activists read their statement and
  dispersed peacefully. — https://kaosgl.org/en/single-news/turkey-violates-lgbti-s-human-rights-according-to-us-report · https://www.ilga-europe.org/blog/the-ilga-europe-turkey-pride-monitor/

### İzmir (`25984515B59477790330217`)
Direction: worse than national on assembly; distinctive exposure for refugee trans women.
- The **13th İzmir LGBTI+ Pride Week and its events were banned**, and police broke up Pride-related public
  events in İzmir in May–June 2025 using **batons, tear gas, water cannons and rubber bullets**, with
  groups reporting police violence. — https://www.ilga-europe.org/blog/the-ilga-europe-turkey-pride-monitor/ · https://kaosgl.org/en/single-news/turkey-violates-lgbti-s-human-rights-according-to-us-report
- **Mülteci-Der's report on Syrian trans women living in İzmir** found they cannot find work, face
  **exploitation and harassment** when they do, experience intense violence, and have **"no faith in
  justice"**, with widespread frustration — a province-specific finding about the intersection of refugee
  status, trans identity and impunity. — https://kaosgl1.org/en/single-news/syrian-trans-women-refugees-struggle-with-both-systemic-barriers-and-prejudices

### Diyarbakır (`25984515B99670107521163`)
Direction: mixed — a trans murder on an anti-LGBT mobilisation day, followed by the strongest sentences in the record, but with the LGBTI bar commission shut out of the case.
- On **14 September 2024 — the day of the national "Büyük Aile Buluşması" (Great Family Gathering)** — two
  men, Ali Aküzüm (A.A.) and İlyas Uçak (İ.U.), attacked two trans women with sharp objects in the
  Kayapınar district; **Sudenaz Uran was killed at the scene and her friend G. was injured**. The
  defendants **recorded the killing on audio**, and the recording entered the case file. — https://tdor.translivesmatter.info/reports/2024/09/15/sudenaz-uran_kayapinar-diyarbaki-turkey_3aff8365 · https://kaosgl2.org/haber/sudenaz-in-katilleri-cinayeti-ses-kaydina-almis · https://medyascope.tv/2025/09/17/trans-kadin-sudenaz-cinayeti-saniklar-birbirini-sucladi-durusma-yine-ertelendi/
- **19 December 2025: both were sentenced to aggravated life imprisonment for "intentional killing of a
  woman"** — a strong judicial response, and unusually severe relative to typical outcomes. — https://www.bianet.org/haber/two-men-sentenced-to-life-in-prison-for-murder-of-trans-woman-in-diyarbakir-314707 · https://bianet.org/haber/trans-kadin-sudenaz-u-davasinda-karar-iki-saniga-agirlastirilmis-muebbet-314668
- Countervailing institutional detail: during the trial the **Family and Social Services Ministry's
  application to intervene as a party was accepted while the Diyarbakır Bar Association LGBTI+
  Commission's application was rejected** — the state appears as guardian of "the family", not of the
  victim's community. — https://medyascope.tv/2025/09/17/trans-kadin-sudenaz-cinayeti-saniklar-birbirini-sucladi-durusma-yine-ertelendi/

### Antalya (`25984515B97476213604692`)
Direction: mixed — murder of a trans woman in Türkiye's main beach-tourism province, with a full life sentence and no sentence reduction.
- **Nida Nazlıer, 45, was killed in her home in Antalya on 31 July 2025.** The **Antalya 4th Heavy Penal
  Court convicted Aref Elhussein of intentional murder, sentencing him to life imprisonment plus seven
  years for theft, and expressly declined to grant a reduction for good behaviour**. — https://bianet.org/haber/man-given-life-sentence-for-trans-woman-nida-nazlier-s-murder-321589
- Antalya's governor also issued a standing ban on LGBTI public activity, so the province pairs harsh
  homicide sentencing with the total suppression of visible community presence. — https://kaosgl.org/en/single-news/turkey-violates-lgbti-s-human-rights-according-to-us-report

### Eskişehir (`25984515B12971548112787`)
Direction: worse than national.
- Amnesty International's 2024 monitoring covered seven provinces and found that in **İstanbul (both
  Prides), Antalya and Eskişehir the authorities unlawfully prevented the marches from taking place** —
  Eskişehir being a university city that had previously hosted one of Türkiye's few functioning pride
  events. — https://www.amnesty.org/en/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/EUR4487602024ENGLISH.pdf

### Gaziantep (`25984515B63001774148772`) and Mersin (`25984515B33535290178113`)
Direction: worse than national — shared fact, one per province.
- Both provinces' governors are named alongside Ankara, Istanbul, İzmir and Antalya as having **issued bans
  on public activities by lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender and intersex persons during the reporting
  year**. Gaziantep additionally carries a large refugee population, so the ban interacts with the
  İzmir-documented pattern of refugee trans women being left outside protection. — https://kaosgl.org/en/single-news/turkey-violates-lgbti-s-human-rights-according-to-us-report

### National frame (bounds all 81 units)
- Amnesty's assessment of the 2023 pride season is that Turkish authorities imposed **blanket bans and
  discriminatory restrictions, using unnecessary and arbitrary force that in some cases amounted to
  torture**, and that **anti-LGBTI rhetoric from senior government figures increased prejudice and
  emboldened anti-LGBTI groups** — the national driver behind every provincial ban above. — https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2024/05/turkiye-discriminatory-restrictions-and-violence-against-pride-protesters-must-not-be-repeated/
- A 2026 compilation study of pride marches and the right to peaceful assembly in Türkiye documents the
  bans as a durable, multi-year rights pattern rather than seasonal policing. — https://hakikatadalethafiza.org/sites/default/files/2026-04/A%20STRUGGLE%20FOR%20EXISTENCE%3A%20PRIDE%20MARCHES%20AND%20THE%20RIGHT%20TO%20PEACEFUL%20ASSEMBLY%20IN%20T%C3%9CRK%C4%B0YE.pdf

## Regions with no sub-national signal
The other 73 provinces — including Adana, Bursa, Kayseri, Konya, Şanlıurfa, Batman, Van, Samsun, Sivas,
Denizli, Muğla, Hatay, Kahramanmaraş, Trabzon and the rest — produced no province-specific trans-related
source in this pass. Given the national blanket-ban practice, absence of a documented provincial ban here
is **not** evidence that a province permits LGBTI activity.

## Urban / rural and general notes
- **Türkiye's variation is city-scale and district-scale**, exactly as the brief anticipated: İstanbul (and
  within it specifically **Beyoğlu/Galata**), Ankara, İzmir, Antalya, Diyarbakır, Eskişehir. The Beyoğlu
  findings are the most operationally useful on this whole map: a named district where a trans person can
  be blocked from working, have their home sealed by governor's order, or be taken to a station for an ID
  check while drinking coffee.
- **Two opposite state behaviours coexist and must be scored separately:** (a) the *assembly and
  policing* axis, where the state is uniformly hostile and province governors implement it; (b) the
  *homicide-justice* axis, where courts in Diyarbakır and Antalya delivered aggravated life sentences
  without mitigation for killing trans women. Good homicide investigation is not evidence of safety for a
  visible trans person, and the ban is not evidence that murders go unpunished.
- **The "Büyük Aile Buluşması" (Great Family Gathering) calendar is a risk event, not just a political
  story:** Sudenaz Uran was killed on the day it was held. An assessor with date-of-travel data should
  treat large state-adjacent "family" mobilisation dates as elevated-risk days, particularly in Anatolian
  cities outside the tourist coast.
- **Refugee status compounds everything** in the provinces with large Syrian populations (İzmir,
  Gaziantep, Şanlıurfa, Hatay, İstanbul): the documented profile is no legal work, exploitation, violence,
  and no trust in justice — and a trans visitor with a refugee/asylum-adjacent appearance faces both the
  profiling of the ID-check system and this labour-precarity trap. — https://www.crisisgroup.org/europe-central-asia/western-europemediterranean/turkey/253-mitigating-risks-syrian-refugee-youth-turkeys-sanliurfa
- **Tourism geography matters:** Antalya and Muğla depend on foreign visitors and have the most visible
  police-ban regime on assembly while being comparatively tolerant of discreet gay tourism; the
  tourist-area experience (resort enclaves) is not the city-experience (Beyoğlu-style roundups).
- Türkiye's national anchor is 0.147 (Do Not Travel band after the blind re-rating), so these findings
  mainly tell an assessor *where inside that band* the enforcement is most concentrated.
