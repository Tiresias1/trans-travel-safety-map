# MEX — Mexico · ADM1 research
Researched: 2026-09-17 · Units in boundary data: 32 usable, 0 excluded

## Richness classification
RICH — 32 usable units and real sub-national evidence on three independent axes: (1) per-state
homicide/hate-crime counts published annually by Mexican NGO observatories (Letra S / Observatorio
Nacional de Crímenes de Odio), (2) genuinely divergent state legislation (transfeminicide
criminalisation in only a handful of states, six states still refusing any gender-identity
procedure, first-in-country comprehensive diversity statutes in Guanajuato and Guerrero), and
(3) documented regional differences in police behaviour toward trans people (Quintana Roo,
Veracruz, Michoacán). Mexico is not a country of legal bans on trans presence — the variation is
in violence, impunity and policing, plus a protective-law gradient.

## Different-legal-system regions
**None at ADM1 level.** No state has religious or customary criminal law; sodomy has been
decriminalised nationally since 1871 and no state legislature has criminalised same-sex conduct or
gender identity since. The closest analogue, which is **sub-ADM1 and not scoreable here**, is the
system of *usos y costumbres* community self-government (recognised by Art. 2 of the federal
constitution, concentrated in Oaxaca and Chiapas), where local assemblies and festival
brotherhoods set moral conduct rules — documented in 2024 excluding gender-nonconforming people
from a traditional festival in Juchitán (see Oaxaca below). This matters for an assessor as a
reminder that state-level scores overestimate uniformity in the Isthmus and highland Chiapas, but
it is not a state legal system.

## Regions with findings

### Distrito Federal (`31927357B79016588373767`)
Direction: mixed — **worst homicide count in the country, best institutional response.** (the Distrito Federal unit is Mexico City / CDMX)
- For the first time in the record, CDMX was the state with the **most killings of LGBT+ people in 2024: 17 homicides, of which 11 were trans women** — the highest transfeminicide toll of any state, ahead of Nuevo León (5). — https://letraese.org.mx/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Informe-crimenes-2024.pdf
- 2025: still among the highest-recorded states, "despite its image as an inclusive city" — 115 recorded violent incidents nationally of which 59 were killings in the year. — https://www.fundacionarcoiris.org.mx/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Violencias-que-persisten.-Informe-2025-Observatorio___Ok-1.pdf
- **Strong positive institutional response (record it as positive evidence):** transfeminicide made a distinct crime in July 2024 ("Ley Paola Buenrostro", 2nd state to do so); a dedicated Transfeminicide Investigation Unit created Dec 2024 covering all violent deaths of trans women; Protocolo Acuerdo FGJCDMX/04/2025 requiring feminist-context investigation with immediate action (72h/48h deadlines); new Gender Police launched Aug 2025 with 30 patrol units. — https://cnnespanol.cnn.com/2024/07/19/ciudad-de-mexico-delito-transfeminicidio-cambios-ley-orix · https://www.proceso.com.mx/nacional/cdmx/2024/12/20/crean-unidad-de-investigacion-del-delito-de-transfeminicidio-en-la-cdmx-340934.html · https://vlex.com.mx/vid/acuerdo-fgjcdmx-04-2025-1073720670 · https://www.excelsior.com.mx/comunidad/fiscalia-cdmx-protocolo-investigar-feminicidios-transfeminicidios/1703859 · https://www.proceso.com.mx/nacional/cdmx/2025-8-25/brugada-pone-en-marcha-nueva-policia-de-genero-atendera-casos-de-violencia-contra-las-mujeres-355669.html
- Long-standing protective framework: LGR since 2014, extended to 12-year-olds by decree in 2021. — https://www.hrw.org/feature/2025/03/11/gender-recognition-mexico-state

### Veracruz de Ignacio de la Llave (`31927357B5337219705256`)
Direction: worse than national — the longest-running fatality concentration, in a state of near-total impunity.
- Led the national count for **four consecutive years** (2021: 10 recorded violent deaths of LGBT+ people), followed by Guanajuato (9), Estado de México (7) and Chihuahua (6); 19 of 32 states recorded victims that year. — https://letraese.org.mx/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/Informe-Crimenes-2021.pdf
- 2025: at least **15 hate crimes** against LGBT+ people; activists report cases remain unclarified at year's end, and the state prosecutor's office has **lost investigative files**; a running total of **460 unresolved hate-crime cases** was reported in Jan 2025. Activists cite "rezago y archivamiento de expedientes"; some emblematic cases have arrests but **no final sentences**. — https://oem.com.mx/diariodexalapa/local/veracruz-cerro-2025-con-15-crimenes-de-odio-contra-la-comunidad-lgbtq-28188192 · https://jornadaveracruz.com.mx/crimenes-de-odio-contra-la-comunidad-lgbttqi-en-veracruz-sin-resolver/ · https://imagendelgolfo.mx/estado/veracruz-concluyo-2025-con-al-menos-15-crimenes-de-odio-va-saldo-blanco-a-inicios-de-este-ano-20260204-0043.html
- Example case: "Luis M", killed 19 Nov 2025 in a motel in Oluta; the state observatory counted 9 hate crimes earlier the same year (3 of them trans women). — https://microadmin.jornada.com.mx/letraese/2026/05/07/victimas-de-la-impunidad-1578.html · https://www.eldictamen.mx/van-9-crimenes-de-odio-contra-personas-lgbti-en-veracruz/
- Institutional counterweight only recently: Veracruz only passed its gender-identity civil-code reform in 2025 (with no age limit). — https://www.hrw.org/feature/2025/03/11/gender-recognition-mexico-state

### Nuevo León (`31927357B30741484985331`)
Direction: worse than national — a concentrated killing cluster the state refuses to legally name.
- **Three trans women murdered in less than a week in July 2024** (Arantza Castillo, 23, disappeared 7 July and found dead; Jaqueline, aka "Española"; a third woman unidentified), and four transfeminicides recorded in July 2024 alone; 5 nationally in the 2024 yearbook, second only to CDMX. — https://agenciapresentes.org/2024/07/31/mexico-tres-mujeres-trans-asesinadas-en-nuevo-leon-en-menos-de-una-semana/ · https://lasillarota.com/estados/2024/8/1/transfeminicidios-en-nuevo-leon-activistas-denuncian-victimas-en-julio-407144.html · https://letraese.org.mx/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Informe-crimenes-2024.pdf
- Activists reported **six** trans women killed in a short period and said three levels of government had omitted their rights; **Nuevo León's penal code contains no transfeminicide offence** and the state's 2019 hate-crime provision covers sexual orientation, not gender identity. — https://www.milenio.com/politica/comunidad/mujeres-trans-buscan-tipificar-delito-transfeminicidio · https://verificado.com.mx/transfeminicidio-ignorado-por-gob-nuevo-leon/
- **Damning response:** Colectivo Casa Trans ran a protest encampment outside the state congress for 50+ days, still ongoing in January 2026, because an earlier initiative they filed was "ignored and finally lapsed"; a PT deputy was reduced in 2026 to asking the congress to "unfreeze" the bill. — https://abcnoticias.mx/local/2026/1/12/mantendran-planton-en-congreso-hasta-que-el-transfeminicidio-sea-ley-270692.html · https://avimexnews.com/solicita-pt-descongelar-iniciativa-de-reforma-para-tipificar-el-delito-de-transfeminicidio/

### Michoacán de Ocampo (`31927357B49073316106075`)
Direction: worse than national — an internally concentrated danger zone plus armed-group control.
- The state's own LGBT+ welfare sub-director (Guillermo Maldonado Silva, June 2026) states that **most attacks on the LGBT community in Michoacán are concentrated in Tierra Caliente**, while Michoacán has **not typified hate crime** at all. — https://oem.com.mx/elsoldemorelia/local/en-tierra-caliente-donde-se-concentra-la-mayoria-de-agresiones-a-comunidad-lgbt-18876082.app.json

### Quintana Roo (`31927357B30293642818187`)
Direction: worse than national for what a **visitor** actually meets — the main tourist corridor, with a documented policing problem.
- Municipal and state police in Quintana Roo have been repeatedly documented committing discriminatory acts against LGBT+ people, with **multiple recorded assaults during detention concentrated in Tulum and Playa del Carmen**; the community says officers are untrained in human-rights standards. — https://www.poresto.com/quintana-roo/2021/7/25/comunidad-lgbt-acusa-policias-de-discriminacion-en-quintana-roo.html
- Transgender Law Center country report on Mexico: local trans women reported a **dramatic increase in police harassment**, officers dragging women out by the hair, and **arrests used to extract extortion payments and sexual favours** from trans women — the classic state-implicated pattern. — https://transgenderlawcenter.org/resources/resources-immigration-legal-policy-work/human-rights-mexico/
- Cancún-specific: the municipal Directorate for Sexual Diversity recorded **three formal discrimination complaints in a single year, including violence against a transgender woman**; and two members of the **Tourist Police were fired after their own body cameras showed them running an extortion ring against visitors with portable card terminals** — i.e. the tourist-facing police layer itself is a documented risk. — https://www.rivieramayanews.mx/transgender-women-face-workplace-discrimination-in-cancun-authorities-step-up-support/ · https://mexicodailypost.news/2026-07-03/tourist-police-officers-arrested-for-allegedly-extorting-tourists-using-a-bank-terminal-in-cancun/
- Cancún's INM immigration detention facility is documented as a place of systematic rights violations with no accountability (relevant to a visitor whose documents are questioned). — https://www.connectas.org/especiales/infierno-en-la-estancia-migratoria-de-cancun/index-en.html
- Countervailing: Quintana Roo enacted LGR in 2020 and banned conversion practices in Dec 2023; Cancún's hotel zone is commercially welcoming to LGBTQ+ tourists. — https://www.hrw.org/feature/2025/03/11/gender-recognition-mexico-state · https://www.equaldex.com/log/5130 · https://wanderingwithpride.org/wandersafe/cancun-mexico

### Oaxaca (`31927357B6525686063804`)
Direction: mixed — world-famous third-gender visibility, with documented customary exclusion and rising danger.
- In May 2024 the organising brotherhood of the Vela San Vicente Ferrer "Goola" (Lado Sur) in **Juchitán circulated an invitation stating "men dressed as women will not be admitted"**; muxe activists, including the poet-lawyer Elvis Guerra, filed a discrimination complaint with CONAPRED saying "this is not the muxe paradise" — the exclusion comes from traditional festival authorities, and these festivals are explicitly marketed to foreign tourists. — https://latintaoaxaca.com/2024/05/25/discriminan-a-las-muxes-en-la-vela-grande-de-juchitan-oaxaca/ · https://www.istmopress.com.mx/municipales/muxes-denuncian-ante-conapred-la-discriminacion-y-homofobia-que-viven-en-sus-velas-tradicionales-no-es-el-paraiso-muxe/
- The state human-rights ombudsperson opened **34 discrimination investigations** involving the LGBTIQ+ community between 2015 and 2022, with attacks on trans people prompting public demands for justice. — https://www.eluniversal.com.mx/estados/persiste-violencia-contra-muxes-y-comunidad-lgbtiq/
- Travel-facing: reporting in April 2025 characterised Oaxaca as Mexico's fastest-growing tourist paradise but **the most dangerous state for the LGBT+ community**, with several violent incidents under investigation **for possible municipal-police participation**, in regions where armed groups operate; authorities responded by stepping up visitor security. — https://www.trendencias.com/mexico/oaxaca-paraiso-mexico-que-ha-crecido-para-turismo-tambien-peligroso-para-comunidad-lgbt
- Positive: Oaxaca has had LGR since 2019, extended to 12+ in 2021 and fully opened by a 2023 Supreme Court ruling; and traditional muxe recognition is real (49th annual "Auténticas Intrépidas Buscadoras del Peligro" vela). — https://www.hrw.org/feature/2025/03/11/gender-recognition-mexico-state · https://liberaradio.com/muxes-de-oaxaca-celebran-49-anos-de-diversidad-y-resistencia-en-su-vela-anual-de-las-autenticas-intrepidas-buscadoras-del-peligro/

### Puebla (`31927357B1137737933275`)
Direction: worse than national — the top state for killings in the most recent year of the national observatory.
- **Puebla led the national statistics for 2025 with 7 recorded killings** of LGBT+ people; trans women were 58.3% of all such killings nationwide that year, across at least 17 states reporting victims. — https://www.quadratin.com.mx/justicia/concentran-mujeres-trans-58-de-los-asesinatos-a-personas-lgbt-en-2025/
- Its LGR law (2021) survives with an 18-year-old requirement the Supreme Court voided in March 2022, which **congress has still not fixed** — legislative non-compliance is itself a hostility signal. — https://www.hrw.org/feature/2025/03/11/gender-recognition-mexico-state

### Guanajuato (`31927357B33104012885361`)
Direction: mixed — one of the most advanced statutes, and one of the most lethal states.
- Second-highest state count in the 2021 national report (9 violent deaths), and among the top states in subsequent years. — https://letraese.org.mx/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/Informe-Crimenes-2021.pdf
- **First state in Mexico to pass a comprehensive sexual-and-gender-diversity law** (Ley para las Personas de la Diversidad Sexual y de Género, approved unanimously 15 Feb 2024 by all 32 deputies across PAN, PRI, Morena, PVEM and MC; published 7 June 2024), and it amended its penal code in 2024 to add sexual orientation and gender identity as protected characteristics. — https://www.am.com.mx/guanajuato/2024/02/15/guanajuato-pone-el-ejemplo-aprueban-ley-de-la-diversidad-sexual-de-genero-la-primera-de-su-tipo-en-el-pais-695035.html · https://vlex.com.mx/vid/ley-personas-diversidad-sexual-1042093811 · https://www.equaldex.com/region/guanajuato
- But HRW devoted a March 2024 documentary to discrimination against trans people in Guanajuato over identity documents, and a federal court had to **order** the state congress in June 2024 to create a rights-respecting recognition procedure. — https://www.hrw.org/news/2024/04/01/mexico-guanajuato-should-legally-recognize-trans-identities · https://www.hrw.org/news/2024/06/10/court-orders-guanajuato-mexico-recognize-trans-identities

### The six states whose legislatures still refuse any gender-recognition procedure
Direction: worse than national — a legislative refusal that keeps trans people's documents permanently mismatched, the single most visitor-relevant legal fact in Mexico (document mismatch is what gets someone questioned).
- **Aguascalientes** (`31927357B17004778023462`), **Chiapas** (`31927357B65177449444732`), **Durango** (`31927357B5285092776894`), **Querétaro** (`31927357B98648037880103`), **Tabasco** (`31927357B81008000569871`) and **Tamaulipas** (`31927357B88239390016047`) are listed by Human Rights Watch (March 2025) as having **not reformed their legislation at all** to create a legal gender-recognition procedure. — https://www.hrw.org/feature/2025/03/11/gender-recognition-mexico-state

## Urban / rural and general notes
- **Mexico's variation is not law-versus-law, it is violence-and-impunity-versus-institutional-response.** No state criminalises being trans or presenting in public. The meaningful regional axes are: (a) which state legislatures have legislated at all (HRW's 22-of-32 map); (b) which have created real investigative machinery (CDMX has a unit, a protocol and a gender police; Nuevo León and Michoacán have no transfeminicide/hate-crime offence); (c) where lethal violence actually concentrates (CDMX, Veracruz, Puebla, Nuevo León, Guanajuato, Estado de México, Chihuahua, Michoacán/Tierra Caliente).
- **Nationwide scale for context:** at least 60 LGBT+ people killed in 2025 (59–60 killings within 115 recorded violent incidents), 206 over 2023–2025; trans women were 58.3% of victims; June (Pride month) was the most violent month of 2025 with ≥12 killings, at least 6 of them LGBT+ human-rights defenders. Mexico is the **second-highest country in the world for crimes against LGBT+ people, behind Brazil** — while the national anchor sits mid-scale, so the sub-national spread is inside an already-high baseline. — https://microadmin.jornada.com.mx/letraese/2026/05/07/victimas-de-la-impunidad-1578.html · https://letraese.org.mx/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Informe-crimenes-2025.pdf · https://mx.headtopics.com/news/mexico-segundo-pa-s-con-m-s-cr-menes-contra-personas-85231388
- **City-vs-countryside dominates inside states.** Danger is concentrated in specific metros and corridors (Tijuana, Ciudad Juárez, Monterrey, Guadalajara/Zapopan, Puebla city, Coatzacoalcos–Oluta in Veracruz, Morelia plus Tierra Caliente) rather than uniformly. Michoacán's own official statement locates the risk in one *comarca*, not in the state as a whole.
- **Armed-group control is an amplifier in specific states** (Michoacán/Tierra Caliente, border Tamaulipas, parts of Veracruz and Guanajuato): a visitor's risk from strangers is high everywhere in Mexico, but what is trans-specific here is that armed groups and local police, not the penal code, are the actors who decide how a gender-nonconforming person is treated — and both have documented patterns of extortion and sexual exploitation of trans women in detention.
- **Two travel-relevant cautions** that are not regional: (1) document mismatch (passport marker vs presentation) is the practical trigger for police questioning, and in the six non-legislating states there is no legal route even for residents; (2) Mexican police extortion of foreigners is documented independently of gender (Cancún tourist-police body-cam case), which raises the cost of any stop.
- **UNVERIFIED CLAIM — deliberately excluded:** a widely circulated report that Tabasco added an "anti-eccentricity" (*ley contra la extravagancia*) provision to its state constitution in January 2023 punishing dressing or wearing makeup "notably different from one's sex at birth" with short detention. **Three separate searches and a claim-verification pass failed to locate a primary or reputable secondary source for it** (only Tabasco's unrelated narcomanta/ponchallantas penal reforms of that session surfaced). It is therefore **not** treated as a finding here; if an assessor meets it elsewhere, it should be sourced to the Periódico Oficial del Estado de Tabasco before use.
