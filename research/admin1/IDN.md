# IDN — Indonesia · ADM1 research
Researched: 2026-09-17 · Units in boundary data: 34 usable, 0 excluded

## Richness classification
RICH — **Aceh is a fully documented different-legal-system exception** (its own Sharia criminal code,
religious police, public canings actually carried out for same-sex intimacy as recently as August 2025),
and there is a second, very strong sub-national signal: a **wave of ten recorded mob attacks on
transgender women in Bogor (West Java) between 30 March and 15 July 2026 with 45 victim-survivors and no
arrests**, plus discriminatory local bylaws (perda) identified in **eight named provinces** and
mass police raids in **Jakarta** (56 men, February 2025) and **West Java/Bogor** (75 people, June 2025).
Every other province either has a perda of this type or has none — a real, nameable gradient.

## Different-legal-system regions
### Aceh (`65028918B93184959873838`) — the mandatory exception, confirmed
- **Aceh is authorised to implement Sharia law** and has its own Islamic criminal code. Under **Qanun No.
  6/2014**, sodomy is classed as *liwath* and female same-sex relations as *musahaqah*; **although
  same-sex activity is not illegal at the national level, it is criminalised in Aceh with a penalty of up
  to 100 lashes or up to eight years' imprisonment.** The Qanun does not criminalise *waria*
  (transfeminine people) by name, but enforcement reaches them. — https://doi.org/10.59698/quru.v3i1.405
- Enforcement is carried out by **Wilayatul Hisbah (WH), the Sharia public-order police, together with
  Satpol PP**, and adjudicated by the **Islamic Shariah District Courts**, sometimes sitting behind closed
  doors. — https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/indonesian-sharia-court-sentences-2-men-to-public-caning-for-kissing-and-hugging · https://portalaceh.inews.id/read/558401/satpol-pp-amankan-waria-dan-mahasiswa-gay-lagi-begituan-di-kamar-kos-terancam-dicambuk/2
- **Nothing comparable exists in any other Indonesian province.** A visitor who commits no crime outside
  Aceh can be caned in Aceh for the same conduct.

### Other regional legal divergence (not a separate system, but locally enacted criminal-adjacent law)
- OutRight International's study *Creeping Criminalisation* analysed **discriminatory regional
  regulations at province, district and city level in eight provinces — Aceh, DKI Jakarta, East
  Kalimantan, Lampung, North Sumatra, South Sulawesi, South Sumatra and Yogyakarta** — finding "much
  overlap" but also "significant regional variations" in how gender and sexual orientation are
  criminalised or penalised locally. — https://outrightinternational.org/sites/default/files/2022-10/CreepingCriminalisation-eng.pdf
- **National floor that changed in 2026:** the new **KUHP criminal code came into force on 1 January 2026**,
  replacing the Dutch-era code, criminalising **sex outside marriage and unmarried cohabitation (up to one
  year)**, and penalising insults to the president and state. Prosecution is complaint-based (spouse,
  parent or child), explicitly to allay tourist concerns — but the "indecency" provisions remain broadly
  written. This applies to all 34 units and interacts with Aceh's separate code. — https://apnews.com/article/indonesia-new-penal-code-takes-effect-4bcb6a0bdf1a63d17c3ff77f085b6382 · https://thediplomat.com/2026/01/indonesian-criminal-code-comes-into-effect-amid-civil-society-criticisms/ · https://eturbonews.com/indonesia-criminal-code-2026-sex-outside-marriage-and-what-tourists-need-to-know/ · https://www.thejakartapost.com/indonesia/2026/01/01/new-criminal-code-requires-public-oversight-minister-says · https://legalindonesia.id/indonesia-adultery-law-rules/
- **National policy shift with regional consequences:** **Presidential Regulation No. 111 of 2025**, in
  force **October 2025**, lists the "spread of LGBTQ culture" among **non-military threats to national
  defence**, alongside terrorism, radicalism, trafficking, online gambling and drugs. Officials say it is
  not aimed at individuals; a human-rights law professor describes it as "a shift from social stigma and
  political rhetoric into the official doctrine of national defence policy". Local governments have since
  cited it when drafting anti-LGBT rules (Palembang, below). — https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026/08/15/men-attack-transwomen-in-indonesia-after-rule-deems-them-threat/107023468 · https://rri.co.id/palembang/regional/2580056/pemkot-palembang-siapkan-regulasi-penolakan-lgbt

## Regions with findings

### Aceh (`65028918B93184959873838`)
Direction: worse than national — corporal punishment is being carried out, and the net has widened from sex to kissing.
- **27 February 2025, Banda Aceh:** two men caught in a boarding house by local residents were **publicly
  caned 159 strokes in total** at Sari Park for consensual sex; one collapsed at the end and was carried
  off by Sharia police to a changing room. They were first given a sermon. — https://www.cnn.com/2025/02/27/asia/indonesia-caning-gay-sex-intl-latam · https://www.asia-pacific-solidarity.net/index.php/news/2025-02-27/gay-couple-caned-159-times-aceh-one-collapses-following-punishment.html
- **26 August 2025, Banda Aceh:** two men were **caned 80 strokes each after an Islamic court convicted
  them of hugging and kissing**, which the court ruled "can lead to banned sexual relations"; about 100
  people watched from a stage in Bustanussalatin city park, and the trial itself had been held behind
  closed doors. Amnesty International called it "a disturbing act of state-sanctioned discrimination and
  cruelty". — https://www.cbsnews.com/news/indonesia-caning-men-kissing-lgbtq-anti-homosexuality-laws-banda-aceh/ · https://www.ksat.com/news/world/2025/08/11/islamic-court-in-indonesia-sentences-2-men-to-public-caning-over-sexual-acts/ · https://www.independent.co.uk/news/banda-aceh-indonesia-islamic-men-indonesian-b2814177.html · https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2025/08/indonesia-caning-of-gay-men-an-act-of-cruelty/
- **18 February 2025, Banda Aceh:** Satpol PP and Wilayatul Hisbah again detained a pair — **a waria
  (transfeminine person) and a gay university student** — in a boarding room, "facing caning"; the
  enforcement unit's own head of Sharia-law enforcement (Kasi Penegakan Syariat Islam, Roslina) publicised
  the roundup. Boarding-house raids on this basis are recurring. — https://portalaceh.inews.id/read/558401/satpol-pp-amankan-waria-dan-mahasiswa-gay-lagi-begituan-di-kamar-kos-terancam-dicambuk/2
- **Earlier anti-trans practice:** in January 2018 **Aceh police detained 12 transwomen and shaved or cut
  their hair**, saying they were teaching them "how to act like real men" — part of what a Harvard/UNSW
  review describes as transwomen being "the most frequent victims of police violence in Indonesia" in 2018. — https://www.humanrights.unsw.edu.au/news/epidemic-violence-against-transgender-women-indonesia-when-government-fails-protect-its-vulnerable-citizens

### West Java (`65028918B28366581005022`) — Bogor
Direction: worse than national — the country's most concentrated wave of street violence against trans women, with total impunity.
- **Ten attacks on transgender women in Bogor between 30 March and 15 July 2026, with 45 victim-survivors**
  (some attacks hit several women at once), documented by Pelangi Nusantara: men punched and kicked trans
  women, threw stones, glass bottles and firecrackers, and **stripped some of them naked**. Amnesty
  International Indonesia recorded **groups of 10 to 20 men** carrying out the attacks and **filming them
  for social media**, with some victim-survivors too afraid to report. — https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026/08/15/men-attack-transwomen-in-indonesia-after-rule-deems-them-threat/107023468
- **Damning response:** **no alleged perpetrator from the Bogor attacks has been publicly identified,
  arrested or prosecuted**; a trans-rights activist who accompanied women making police reports said
  several received no follow-up and that in the Bogor case the police "accepted the case but worked on it
  half-heartedly". The attacks were publicly branded by an Instagram account named **@bogorbersihlgbt**
  ("Bogor clean of LGBT"). This satisfies both the pattern test and the damning-response test. — https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026/08/15/men-attack-transwomen-in-indonesia-after-rule-deems-them-threat/107023468
- **22 June 2025:** police detained **75 people at a villa in the Puncak area of Bogor Regency** at a
  gathering labelled a "gay party"; Amnesty demanded their release, noting "this gathering violated no law
  and posed no threat". — https://en.tempo.co/read/2021791/indonesian-police-arrest-75-members-of-lgbt-community-in-puncak-villa · https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2025/06/indonesia-police-must-release-75-people-arrested-in-discriminatory-raid-on-gay-party/
- Activists attribute the wave of violence to the politicisation of LGBT issues since the 2016 moral panic,
  culminating in Perpres 111/2025: "Because of the presidential regulation and discriminatory regional
  regulations, people feel able to commit violence arbitrarily because they believe the state has
  legitimised that violence." — https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026/08/15/men-attack-transwomen-in-indonesia-after-rule-deems-them-threat/107023468

### Jakarta Special Capital Region (`65028918B14696209246677`)
Direction: mixed — the largest community and the biggest raids, with police acting without legal basis.
- **1 February 2025:** police **arrested 56 men at a hotel in Jakarta** at what was reported as a "gay sex
  party"; the spokesperson said the event was "merely based on pleasure that they wanted to seek", and
  organisers risked prosecution under the pornography law with penalties up to **15 years**. Amnesty
  International Indonesia: "The police's discriminatory and senseless harassment of LGBTI people,
  targeting individuals because of their real or perceived sexual orientation or gender identity, **has no
  basis in law**. Those detained must be released immediately." — https://www.metroweekly.com/2025/02/police-arrest-56-men-in-gay-sex-party-hotel-raid/ · https://www.amnesty.id/kabar-terbaru/siaran-pers/lgbti-people-must-be-released-after-discriminatory-police-raid/02/2025/
- Jakarta is also one of the eight provinces where OutRight found discriminatory regional regulations, and
  simultaneously the city with the country's densest LGBTQ+ infrastructure, media presence and the offices
  of the monitoring groups (Arus Pelangi, TIERs-equivalent local partners, Sekang) — an assessor should
  expect both to be true at once. — https://outrightinternational.org/sites/default/files/2022-10/CreepingCriminalisation-eng.pdf

### West Sumatra (`65028918B4119458748300`)
Direction: worse than national — the clearest *enacted* modern local anti-LGBT bylaw outside Aceh.
- **Bukittinggi City Regional Regulation (Perda) No. 2 of 2024 on Public Order, articles 43–44, provides
  sanctions targeting LGBT people**: oral and written warnings, cessation of activities, **social
  rehabilitation programmes**, and **administrative fines of Rp1,000,000** — with academic analysis noting
  implementation remains weak precisely because rehabilitation facilities do not exist. — https://ejournal.yasin-alsys.org/ahkam/article/view/10641 · https://ejournal.yasin-alsys.org/aldyas/article/download/7712/5736
- **Limapuluh Kota regency** finalised a draft (ranperda) on public tranquility and order containing an
  **explicit LGBT prohibition, administrative fines and "rehabilitation" for people of same-sex
  attraction**. — https://mimbarsumbar.id/dprd-limapuluh-kota-finalisasi-ranperda-anti-lgbt-memuat-denda-administratif-dan-rehabilitasi/

### South Sulawesi (`65028918B17554737605304`) — Makassar
Direction: worse than national — an active local-legislative push.
- Makassar's mayor **Munafri 'Appi' Arifuddin** reaffirmed his commitment to pushing through an
  **anti-LGBT Perda** after a same-sex act at a night entertainment venue went viral; the deputy chair of
  the DPRD's legislation body backed it as necessary "to preserve moral order and religious norms",
  saying the regulation's creation is urgently needed. Makassar is the gateway to eastern Indonesia and a
  major domestic-tourism hub. — https://harian.news/dorong-perda-anti-lgbt-dprd-makassar-siap-kawal
- South Sulawesi is also one of OutRight's eight analysed provinces. — https://outrightinternational.org/sites/default/files/2022-10/CreepingCriminalisation-eng.pdf

### South Sumatra (`65028918B9551391119865`) — Palembang
Direction: worse than national — an anti-LGBT measure framed as national defence.
- On **19 July 2026 the Palembang city government announced it was preparing a regulation rejecting
  LGBT**, presented as follow-through on **Perpres No. 111 of 2025** on the general defence policy
  2025–2029, announced by Mayor Ratu Dewa in the courtyard of the Grand Mosque. This is the local
  implementation layer of the national "non-military threat" classification. — https://rri.co.id/palembang/regional/2580056/pemkot-palembang-siapkan-regulasi-penolakan-lgbt
- South Sumatra also appears in OutRight's eight-province analysis, and in November 2018 **Satpol PP raided
  three transwomen in Lampung** (neighbouring province) — the Sumatran belt is where local enforcement is
  consolidating. — https://outrightinternational.org/sites/default/files/2022-10/CreepingCriminalisation-eng.pdf · https://www.humanrights.unsw.edu.au/news/epidemic-violence-against-transgender-women-indonesia-when-government-fails-protect-its-vulnerable-citizens

### Lampung (`65028918B88589358118692`)
Direction: worse than national.
- In **November 2018 three transwomen were subjected to a raid by Satpol PP**, the regional moral-and-order
  force, in Lampung — one of the incidents the comparative review of violence against Indonesian
  transwomen records as police-perpetrated. Lampung is also inside OutRight's eight-province set. — https://www.humanrights.unsw.edu.au/news/epidemic-violence-against-transgender-women-indonesia-when-government-fails-protect-its-vulnerable-citizens · https://outrightinternational.org/sites/default/files/2022-10/CreepingCriminalisation-eng.pdf

### Bali (`65028918B6690755070061`)
Direction: **better than national — but explicitly not a refuge, and the legal floor has fallen.**
- Indonesian LGBTIQ+ people **migrate to Bali to "remake the meaning of home"**: "The island is no utopia,
  but it can offer pockets of refuge, places where they feel valued, accepted and able to be themselves,
  even if those spaces are hidden and limited." — https://projectmultatuli.org/en/building-a-house-above-the-waves/
- A 2025 ethnographic study of **transpuan** communities in Bali deliberately challenges the
  "romanticised refuge" framing, documenting "resistance and misrecognition" and systemic discrimination
  even there. — https://doi.org/10.3167/ame.2025.200104
- The **2026 KUHP now reaches Bali visitors**: guides note Art. 412 on cohabitation outside marriage and
  broadly written "indecency" provisions as the new complexity for LGBTQ+ travellers, with public
  displays of affection discouraged under conservative norms. — https://gayborhood.com/guide/indonesia/bali/ · https://yourhappinesstours.com/blog/is-bali-safe-lgbtq-2026/ · https://www.equaldex.com/region/bali

### Papua (`65028918B16658067687714`) and West Papua (`65028918B84835514626455`)
Direction: worse than national as an **amplifier**, on non-trans-specific evidence that is clearly documented.
- **138 recorded acts of torture or other ill-treatment in detention** were committed by police and
  military in Maybrat and the Central Highlands between September 2021 and Q1 2025, "with almost no
  consequences"; and there are named arbitrary-detention cases **at airports** — three Papuans detained by
  officers at Nop Goliat Airport, Dekai (Yahukimo) on 26 July 2025 while one of them was boarding to
  study in Jayapura. Q3 2025 saw expanded military deployments and forced displacement. — https://www.asia-pacific-solidarity.net/index.php/reports/2025-07-22/torture-detention-west-papua-impunity-secrecy-and-silence.html · https://humanrightsmonitor.org/case/police-officers-arbitrarily-detain-three-papuans-at-dekai-airport-yahukimo/ · https://humanrightsmonitor.org/reports/papua-quarterly-report-q3-2025-justice-denied-fatal-shootings-arbitrary-detentions-and-the-failure-of-accountability-mechanisms/
- **No trans-specific Papuan incident series was found.** These units are recorded as worse because the
  probability that any police contact escalates into unlawful detention and ill-treatment is documented
  and far above the national average — that is an amplifier, not an independent finding about trans people.

### East Java (`65028918B43243108525571`)
Direction: mixed — custody-placement note, not a protection finding.
- **11 February 2025:** content creator **Isa Zega became the first transgender detainee held at the
  women's prison (Lapas Perempuan Kelas IIA Sukun, Malang)**, after her file was declared complete on
  suspected defamation/extortion — a documented instance of an authority placing a trans woman in a
  facility by identity rather than legal sex, which cuts against the pattern in most countries here. — https://surabaya.kompas.com/read/2025/02/12/083347278/isa-zega-transgender-pertama-di-lapas-perempuan-malang

### National violence series (bounds all 34 units)
- **Arus Pelangi recorded 28 attacks on the LGBTQIA+ community in 2024 rising to 66 in 2025** — the
  clearest national trend line, published in *Catatan Kelam 4 (A Bleak Chronicle 4.0)* — while the ABC
  analysis cautions that it "is unclear if the recent wave in Bogor represents an overall increase, because
  there is limited data on such attacks". — https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026/08/15/men-attack-transwomen-in-indonesia-after-rule-deems-them-threat/107023468 · https://aseansogiecaucus.org/images/resources/publications/Kekerasan-Terhadap-LGBTI-di-Indonesia-Tahun-2024-2025_Final.pdf · https://aseansogiecaucus.org/latest/asc-news/342-report-launch-catatan-kelam-4-kekerasan-terhadap-lgbti-di-indonesia-tahun-2024-2025
- TGEU's Trans Murder Monitoring counted **281 reported murders worldwide 1 Oct 2024–30 Sep 2025**, with
  **sex workers the most targeted known occupation (34%)** — the relevant frame for street-based Indonesian
  trans women, whose attackers are motivated by visible public-work. — https://transmurdermonitoring.tgeu.org/en/

## Urban / rural and general notes
- **Indonesia's map is three-tiered, not city-vs-countryside:** (1) **Aceh**, with a separate criminal code
  and corporal punishment actually applied; (2) provinces/municipalities that have enacted or are drafting
  **anti-LGBT bylaws** (West Sumatra's Bukittinggi and Limapuluh Kota, Makassar, Palembang, plus
  OutRight's eight provinces); (3) everywhere else, where being LGBTIQ+ "is not generally a criminal
  offence" but the **new national KUHP** and the **2025 presidential regulation** create fresh exposure.
  Bali sits in tier 3 socially but is legally exposed via KUHP Art. 412 and the tourism economy.
- **The main national-level mechanism for a visitor is extortion and arbitrary detention by police, not
  prosecution.** Rights groups describe raids on private gatherings with no offence committed, charges
  brought under the pornography law, and detentions used to extract payment. The Bogor wave adds
  **vigilante mob violence with zero prosecutions**, which the brief treats as damning-response evidence.
- **Street-work visibility is the exposure variable:** the groups being attacked (waria in boarding houses,
  transpuan on Bogor's streets) are working-class trans women in public space; a tourist in a Bali villa
  and a visibly trans person in Bogor face completely different risk profiles inside the same country.
- **Data scarcity is itself a finding:** Arus Pelangi's 28→66 series is the only time series available, and
  ABC reports there is "limited data on such attacks". Do not treat provinces absent from this dossier as
  documented-safe.
- Indonesia's national anchor sits in the criminalisation-free but high-hostility band (0.155). Aceh should
  be scored materially below it; Bali materially above it, mainly because local enforcement there is
  visibly absent rather than because the law differs.
