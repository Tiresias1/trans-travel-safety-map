# FRA — France · ADM1 research
Researched: 2026-09-17 · Units in boundary data: 13 usable, 0 excluded

## Richness classification
MEDIUM — France has no regional legal divergence whatsoever on trans matters (criminal, civil and
public-order law are unitary and the Constitution applies identically in all 13 regions), but there is
**real, official, sub-national quantification of anti-LGBT crime** — the interior ministry's SSMSI
publishes rates by zone type and department — plus two concrete, dated **regional incidents of state
restriction on public LGBTQ+ assembly**: the Côte-d'Or prefecture's ban of the Dijon Pride from the
city centre in 2024 and 2025 (upheld by the administrative court), and the cancellation of the Béziers
Pride in June 2026 for lack of security volunteers in Robert Ménard's city. RN-governed municipalities
supply the political-climate variation. So: evidence exists for ~5 units, of a quantitative and
event-based kind rather than a legal one.

## Different-legal-system regions
**None.** No region has its own criminal or family law. The one configuration worth recording is
**outside the scoreable ADM1 set entirely**: France's overseas departments (Guadeloupe, Martinique,
Guyane, La Réunion, Mayotte) fall under Article 73 "legislative identity" — the Code civil applies "word
for word", so a trans minor in Saint-Denis de La Réunion has the same legal avenues as one in Lille —
yet **SSMSI data show the highest per-capita rates of anti-LGBT offences nationally are in Guadeloupe,
followed by Martinique, Guyane, La Réunion and Mayotte**, and parliamentary and Le Refuge research
reports that homophobia is "more marked" in these territories because of the valorisation of virility,
the weight of family, the impossibility of anonymity and the weight of religion, with "accrued" violence
against women and LGBT+ people and fewer resources to respond. **These territories are not ADM1 units in
this boundary layer**, so France's most acute sub-national contrast cannot be scored here; the national
French score is a metropolitan score. — https://rci.fm/deuxiles/infos/Societe/Une-hausse-de-5-des-infractions-anti-LGBT-en-2024 · https://www.humanite.fr/societe/violences-faites-aux-femmes/en-outre-mer-des-violences-accrues-contre-les-femmes-et-la-communaute-lgbt-802798 *[404 on 2026-09-17: filename is double-encoded; the same 2018 parliamentary finding is carried by the rci.fm and l'Humanité pieces cited with it]* · https://le-refuge.org/blog/precarite-sanitaire-et-psychologique-des-minorites-sexuelles-et-de-genre-dans-les-outre-mer/ · https://grandirtrans.fr/mineurs-trans-outre-mer-specificites-recours/ · https://la1ere.franceinfo.fr/mariage-pour-tous-outre-mer-13-ans-d-egalite-juridique-mais-une-acceptation-encore-fragile-1700857.html

## Regions with findings

### Bourgogne-Franche-Comté (`19338628B30439183828151`) — Dijon / Côte-d'Or
Direction: worse than national — the only French prefecture found that has twice banned a Pride march from its city centre, and had the ban upheld.
- **12 June 2025:** the **Côte-d'Or prefecture prohibited the "Pride 2025" from marching in central Dijon**,
  invoking a "risk of trouble to public order" — **the second consecutive year** the same restriction was
  applied. Solidaires 21, which initiated the march, withdrew citing the visibility/exposure of LGBT people,
  and the union's withdrawal was itself called "significant" by the city. — https://www.franceinfo.fr/societe/lgbt/la-prefecture-interdit-la-pride-2025-dans-le-centre-ville-de-dijon_7307532.html · https://france3-regions.franceinfo.fr/bourgogne-franche-comte/cote-d-or/dijon/pride-2025-interdite-dans-le-centre-de-dijon-la-polemique-continue-d-enfler-a-la-veille-de-la-marche-des-fiertes-3170109.html
- **14 June 2025:** the **Dijon administrative court rejected the legal challenge** brought by Solidaires
  21 and the Ligue des droits de l'Homme, and the march (about **1,500 people** per police) proceeded on a
  diverted route around the historic centre. — https://www.ici.fr/infos/societe/dijon-le-tribunal-administratif-rejette-la-requete-des-organisateurs-de-la-pride-2025-pour-defiler-en-centre-ville-6631391
- **Mitigating, and worth recording as a good response:** the march itself went off peacefully — "this year,
  1,500 people came to parade for the Pride 2025, according to the police. A joyful and festive event which
  went off without a hitch (*sans encombre*)". The stated grounds for the ban were therefore disproportionate
  to what materialised: the prefecture justified it by a number of demonstrators "probably underestimated by
  the organisers (200), given previous demonstrations (2,500, 1,500)" and by a "high probability that
  individuals with a will to commit violent acts would attach themselves to the procession". The harm here is
  the *restriction and the chilling effect*, not violence. — https://www.ici.fr/infos/societe/dijon-le-tribunal-administratif-rejette-la-requete-des-organisateurs-de-la-pride-2025-pour-defiler-en-centre-ville-6631391
  *(verified against the ICI Bourgogne article, 2026-09-17; a citation to dijon-actualites.fr was removed
  because that URL now soft-redirects to the site homepage)*

### Occitanie (`19338628B57683459369229`) — Béziers and Pyrénées-Orientales
Direction: worse than national — one cancelled Pride, and the one department with a measured above-average rate.
- **13 June 2026: the Béziers Pride was cancelled because no volunteers could be found to staff its
  security**, a blow to the local LGBTQIA+ community described by organisers as "we're talking about
  freedom, about love, about a space where you can be whoever you want" — in the city governed by
  **Robert Ménard**, whose record of bending laïcité and public-space rules for conservative ends is
  separately documented (the LDH asking the new préfète to enforce the 1905 law after municipal Hanouka
  ceremonies). — https://www.midilibre.fr/2026/06/05/on-parle-de-liberte-damour-dun-espace-qui-permet-detre-qui-on-veut-a-beziers-lannulation-de-la-marche-des-fiertes-alourdi-les-coeurs-13405053.php · https://www.lamarseillaise.fr/societe/la-ldh-en-appelle-a-la-prefete-pour-faire-respecter-la-loi-de-1905-DK19310461
- **Pyrénées-Orientales recorded 40 anti-LGBT offences in 2024, a rate of 8.14 per 100,000** against a
  national average of about 5 — 6th of the region's departments — noted in local analysis of the Interior
  Ministry statistics, in a region where "attractive metropoles" coexist with conservative rural areas. — https://madeinperpignan.com/breve/dans-les-pyrenees-orientales-un-taux-dinfractions-anti-lgbt-superieur-a-la-moyenne-nationale/

### Île-de-France (`19338628B22645672383103`) — Paris
Direction: mixed — the highest measured rate in the country and the strongest protective apparatus, with pride now policed as a sensitive event.
- The **Paris agglomeration and other urban units over 200,000 inhabitants concentrate 55% of anti-LGBT
  offences for 43% of the population — a rate of 9 per 100,000, more than double the rural rate of 4**
  (2025 update: 54% for 43%, still 9 per 100,000). Paris is "markedly over-represented". — https://www.dilcrah.gouv.fr/files/2025-08/Info%20rapide%20n53%20-%20Les%20atteintes%20anti-LGBT%2B-1.pdf · https://www.cnafal.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/IR65-Les-infractions-anti-LGBT-enregistrees-par-les-services-de-securite-en-tres-legere-augmentation-en-2025.pdf
- **28 June 2025 Marche des Fiertés, Paris:** police reinforcements were mobilised, the self-declared
  "gay patriot" collective **Eros sought to march with prefectural backing and Inter-LGBT threatened to
  cancel the march if it participated**, and participants noted "the Marche des fiertés is becoming a
  sensitive event" — a conflict between organisers and authorities over who is allowed to represent the
  march, staged inside a heavy police frame. — https://www.franceinfo.fr/societe/lgbt/la-marche-des-fiertes-devient-un-evenement-sensible-une-edition-2025-sous-tension-avec-la-presence-d-homo-patriotes-et-de-policiers-dans-le-cortege-parisien_7338684.html · https://www.fdesouche.com/2025/06/26/paris-75-soutenu-par-la-prefecture-le-groupe-gay-patriote-eros-veut-defiler-a-la-pride-linter-lgbt-menace-dannuler-la-marche-sils-y-participent/
- Île-de-France also has the country's densest trans support infrastructure, dedicated police liaison and
  the largest community — the same reason its recorded rate is high.

### Hauts-de-France (`19338628B36302232233914`) and Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur (`19338628B95725112529620`)
Direction: worse than national — RN municipal practice.
- **Hauts-de-France:** around thirty associations of the Nord and Pas-de-Calais accused newly won **RN town
  halls in the mining basin of "vainly trying" to cancel or hollow out local pride events while claiming to
  defend LGBT rights — "we are not fooled by this pinkwashing"**; the same analysis notes the party's MEPs
  voting against conversion-therapy resolutions. This is a documented pattern of municipal behaviour
  across several communes, not one mayor's statement. — https://basta.media/extreme-droite-RN-pinkwashing-instrumentalisation-lgbt-bassin-minier-nord
- **PACA (Var):** RN mayor **David Rachline of Fréjus issued an arrêté on 1 August prohibiting swimming on
  the commune's beaches for anyone wearing "covering and loose clothing"** — a municipal dress-code order of
  exactly the type that reaches gender-nonconforming appearance; the **Toulon administrative tribunal
  suspended it** and condemned the commune to €3,000. — https://france3-regions.franceinfo.fr/provence-alpes-cote-d-azur/var/frejus-et-saint-raphael/a-frejus-l-arrete-du-maire-rn-pour-interdire-le-burkini-suspendu-par-le-tribunal-administratif-de-toulon-2821895.html · https://web.lexisnexis.fr/LexisActu/Tribunal.administratif,Toulon,5Ao%C3%BBt2023%E2%80%93n%C2%B0%202302537.pdf
- Both regions' relevance is that **French mayors and prefects retain regulatory power over public space
  and dress**, and the boundary of that power is being tested in RN-governed communes — with courts so far
  pushing back.

### National frame (bounds all 13 units)
- **SSMSI (Interior Ministry statistics service): 4,900 anti-LGBT offences recorded by police and
  gendarmerie in 2025, up 2% year on year** — of which **3,200 crimes/délits and 1,800 contraventions** —
  continuing the general upward trend observed since 2016; **only about 3% of victims file a complaint**.
  Nationally: an average of 7 offences per 100,000 inhabitants; **9 in urban units over 200,000, 4 in rural
  communes, 6–7 in between.** — https://www.interieur.gouv.fr/actualites/communiques-de-presse/infractions-anti-lgbt-enregistrees-par-services-de-securite-en-tres-legere-augmentation-en-2025 · https://www.leprogres.fr/faits-divers-justice/2026/06/27/injures-menaces-agressions-ou-les-actes-anti-lgbt-sont-ils-les-plus-nombreux · https://www.dilcrah.gouv.fr/files/2025-08/Info%20rapide%20n53%20-%20Les%20atteintes%20anti-LGBT%2B-1.pdf
- RN parliamentary record on trans rights, PMA and conversion therapy is documented as inconsistent with its
  claims to defend LGBT+ people — relevant because RN is the largest party in several regional councils. — https://similiqueer.com/rn-lgbt-votes-positions-droits/

## Urban / rural and general notes
- **France's variation is almost entirely urban-density-driven, and the official data say so directly:
  9 per 100,000 in big urban units versus 4 in rural communes.** A region score is therefore mostly a proxy
  for how much of its population lives in a large city. This is a genuine finding, and it argues for
  scoring Île-de-France, PACA and Occitanie on their metro exposure rather than on any regional legal
  difference.
- **But high recorded rates in Paris should not be read as Paris being dangerous for a trans visitor** —
  reporting propensity, community visibility, dedicated police desks and the presence of associations all
  raise counts. The Dijon and Béziers cases point the other way: in smaller cities the *event* is the risk
  site, and the state's own authorities (prefecture, and the absence of police capacity) created it.
- **No French region has any facility, expression or bathroom rule**, no regional registration divergence,
  and no regional religious jurisdiction. French sub-national risk is: prefectural/municipal assembly
  restrictions, RN commune practice, departmental offence rates, and — outside this ADM1 layer entirely —
  the overseas departments.
- **Explicit disclosure for the scorer:** the single largest French sub-national contrast (overseas
  departments at the top of the per-capita offence table, Guadeloupe highest) is **not representable in the
  13 metropolitan regions in this boundary file**. Do not let a metropolitan average imply uniformity across
  the French Republic.
- France's national anchor is 0.672 (Reduced Risk), consistent with: strong law, real street-level
  hostility, an active and capable state response, and a growing anti-LGBT political front in specific
  communes.
