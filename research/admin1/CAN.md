# CAN — Canada · ADM1 research
Researched: 2026-09-17 · Units in boundary data: 13 usable, 0 excluded

## Richness classification
RICH — real sub-national legal evidence in 3 provinces (Alberta, Saskatchewan, New Brunswick,
including two uses of the notwithstanding clause against trans rights), documented event-level
disruption patterns in 2 provinces (Alberta/Calgary, British Columbia), and an official
Statistics Canada provincial rate dataset for gender-related homicide. Canada has no provincial
bathroom or expression ban; the variation is in which governments legislate *against* trans
people and which reverse course — plus a prairie/north rate gradient on gendered lethal violence.

## Different-legal-system regions
**None in the sense of the mandatory clause** — no province or territory operates religious or
customary criminal law, and criminal law is federal (Criminal Code uniform across all 13 units).
Two structurally relevant qualifications:
- **Quebec** (`82249235B52639524229375`) runs a distinct civil-law system (Civil Code of Québec)
  and its Charter of Human Rights and Freedoms expressly enumerates "gender expression or
  identity" — a legal difference, though in the protective direction and not a morals jurisdiction.
- **The territories** (Northwest Territories, Nunavut, Yukon) have no separate legal system, but
  gender-related homicide rates there are roughly triple the national provincial figure, and
  2SLGBTQ+ people are subsumed into that pattern (see general notes).

## Regions with findings

### Alberta (`82249235B5553315981608`)
Direction: worse than national — the only jurisdiction in Canada to pass laws targeting trans people as such, and to shield them from judicial review.
- Three bills announced Jan 2024 and enacted December 2024: the **Education Amendment Act, 2024 (Bill 27)** — schools must obtain parental notification/consent before a staff member uses a student's gender-related preferred name or pronouns, in full force since **1 September 2025**; the **Health Statutes Amendment Act, 2024 (No. 2) (Bill 26)** — prohibition on puberty blockers/hormone therapy for minors; and a vital-statistics change. — https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/calgary/alberta-legislation-on-transgender-youth-student-pronouns-and-sex-education-set-to-become-law-1.7400669 · https://teachers.ab.ca/news/education-act-amendments-now-force-across-alberta-schools
- **Bill 9 (2025) invoked the notwithstanding clause (Charter s. 33) over all three laws**, and after it passed a Court of King's Bench judge lifted the injunction that had been pausing the care ban — "The system failed us today." A government appeal succeeded: the injunction was removed. — https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/edmonton/alberta-egale-canada-skipping-stones-bill-9-notwithstanding-clause-transgender-laws-9.7013063 · https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/calgary/gender-affirming-care-injunction-court-decision-9.7021936 · https://edmontonjournal.com/news/politics/judge-lifts-injuction-alberta-gender-affirming-care-ban/wcm/332542da-0bfc-497c-bc72-4c400e2c1540
- Egale Canada + Skipping Stone + five gender-diverse youth continue a constitutional challenge despite the s.33 shield ("the fight isn't over"). — https://egale.ca/egale-in-action/fight-isnt-over/ · https://ablawg.ca/2025/11/19/the-nuclear-option-an-update-on-albertas-legislation-targeting-trans-and-gender-diverse-youth/
- Street-level pattern (Calgary): a pastor, Derek Reimer, led repeated disruptions of library drag-storytime events from 2023 (children left crying, homophobic slurs shouted at parents and kids); he was charged, and litigation has run for years — a conviction for harassing a librarian was upheld on appeal ("your call for action turned on you"), while acquittals on disturbance/mischief charges were **overturned with a new trial ordered**; a further breach conviction came in 2026. Xtra reported in March 2023 that "many drag events have been cancelled or postponed in Calgary over the last few months, as threats and protests escalate". — https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/calgary/derek-reimer-calgary-drag-storytime-protest-harassment-appeal-denied-1.7540173 · https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/calgary/derek-reimer-protest-acquittal-appeal-overturned-library-drag-reading-1.7581018 · https://calgaryherald.com/news/local-news/calgary-pastor-hate-motivated-incident-at-seton-library-drag-event · https://xtramagazine.com/power/calgary-drag-storytime-protest-247232
- Alberta also had the second-highest provincial gender-related homicide rate of women and girls in 2021 (0.68 per 100,000) — a proxy for gendered-violence base rates, not a trans-specific measure. — https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/en/pub/85-002-x/2023001/article/00003-eng.pdf?st=Mbs5KwB-

### Saskatchewan (`82249235B71336988791422`)
Direction: worse than national — a provincial government that overrode its own court to keep a name/pronoun consent rule.
- 22 Aug 2023: ministerial "Use of Preferred First Name and Pronouns by Students" policy requiring parental consent for students under 16, plus mandatory parent notification about sexual-health curriculum and a pause on third-party school programs. — https://www.saskatchewan.ca/government/news-and-media/2023/august/22/education-minister-announces-new-parental-inclusion-and-consent-policies
- **Struck down by the Court of King's Bench** in UR Pride Centre for Sexuality and Gender Diversity v Saskatchewan (Education), 2023 SKKB 204 — after which the government legislated the rule into the Education Act (The Education (Parents' Bill of Rights) Amendment Act, 2023, s. 197.4) **and used s. 33 of the Charter** to immunise it; the matter returned to the Court of Appeal as Saskatchewan (Minister of Education) v UR Pride Centre, 2025 SKCA 74 (11 Aug 2025). — https://www.canlii.org/en/sk/skkb/doc/2023/2023skkb204/2023skkb204.html · https://jfcy.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/SK-v-URPride-CA-20250811.pdf · https://www.schoolofpublicpolicy.sk.ca/documents/research/policy-briefs/jsgs-policybriefs-bill-137.pdf
- Highest provincial gender-related homicide rate of women and girls in 2021 (1.03 per 100,000). — https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/en/pub/85-002-x/2023001/article/00003-eng.pdf?st=Mbs5KwB-

### New Brunswick (`82249235B4167839012994`)
Direction: **mixed** — the clearest case in Canada of a government reversing course, in both directions within 18 months.
- Policy 713 (2020) protected 2SLGBTQIA+ students' privacy and inclusion; the Higgs government amended it in July 2023 to require parental consent for under-16 name/pronoun use, triggering protests outside the legislature. — https://ablawg.ca/2024/12/13/albertas-bills-targeting-gender-diverse-youth-comparisons-constitutional-issues-and-challenges/
- The Holt government **reversed it on 19 December 2024, effective 1 January 2025**: parental consent is "encouraged but no longer required" for students of any age, with a new "presumed capacity" concept based on the child and youth advocate's recommendations. — https://www.gnb.ca/en/news/n-b.2024.12.revisions-to-policy-713-effective-jan-1.html · https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/new-brunswick/holt-government-new-policy-713-1.7415289
- Note for assessors: the policy text expressly extends to people visiting schools (student teachers, visiting professionals), so school-environment rules in this province reach non-residents working or presenting in classrooms. — https://www.campaignlifecoalition.com/shared/media/editor/file/Policy%20713%20Holt%20Revision%20202501.pdf

### British Columbia (`82249235B24619009834807`)
Direction: mixed — no hostile legislation, but a documented 2023 wave of disruptions and cancellations of public drag events with thin police protection.
- Coquitlam Public Library postponed/cancelled drag storytime events repeatedly during 2023 citing "public safety risk" and Canada-wide coordinated protests (Jan 14 2023 protest at the Town Centre branch; Oct 13 2023 postponement). A supporting counter-gathering became "the biggest gathering of queer people the Tri-Cities has ever seen". — https://vancouver.citynews.ca/2023/10/13/coquitlam-library-drag-story-time-cancelled/ · https://www.ctvnews.ca/vancouver/article/coquitlam-library-postpones-drag-queen-story-time-due-to-public-safety-risk/ · https://tricitiesdispatch.com/drag-library-protest/
- At a Richmond library event, with **no police on scene**, a man pushed through parents and children to photograph the performer; the Vancouver Sun documented the broader pattern of "outrage" campaigns against B.C. library drag story hours. A Vancouver Granville Island children's drag camp was threatened, requiring a public rally of support. — https://www.thetyee.ca/Culture/2023/01/31/Amidst-Protest-Pride-and-Joy/ · https://vancouversun.com/news/local-news/why-drag-queen-story-hours-at-public-libraries-are-drawing-outrage-and-bubbles-of-love · https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/kids-drag-camp-vancouver-granville-island-carousel-theatre-1.6896901

### Northwest Territories (`82249235B82395866892480`)

Not trans-specific, but the only official provincial/territorial rate data available. — https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/en/pub/85-002-x/2023001/article/00003-eng.pdf?st=Mbs5KwB-

## Regions with no sub-national signal
Manitoba (beyond the 0.72 provincial gender-related homicide rate), Ontario, Nova Scotia, Prince Edward Island, Newfoundland and Labrador, Quebec (beyond the constitutional-law note above) — no province-specific trans incident pattern, ordinance, or official statement with a source was found for these units.

## Urban / rural and general notes
- **Federal law is uniform and protective; the divergence is provincial and mostly about schools and youth care**, which a visitor encounters only as a climate signal (posters, board meetings, protest activity, provincial statements) rather than as an arrestable act. Canada has no provincial bathroom restriction, no expression ban, and no provincial registration bar; the Criminal Code and the Canadian Human Rights Act apply everywhere.
- **Two provinces have used the notwithstanding clause specifically to defeat trans rights** (Alberta Bill 9, Saskatchewan Bill 137/s.197.4). This is the sharpest sub-national distinction on the Canadian map: in those two jurisdictions, courts have already said the policies are unconstitutional/unlawful and been legislatively overruled. An assessor should treat "courts lost and the legislature overrode them" as an escalation, not a status quo.
- **City-vs-region dominates the safety picture, not province-vs-province.** Toronto, Vancouver, Montreal, Ottawa and Halifax host large, organised communities and institutional support (Egale Canada is national); the documented hostility clusters in Calgary's library/board-meeting politics and the Coquitlam/Richmond fringes. Trans PULSE Canada found markedly worse outcomes for **rural and small-town** trans and non-binary people — so within any province, expect the metropolitan core to behave very differently from the small towns. — https://transpulsecanada.ca/results/report-health-and-well-being-among-rural-and-small-town-trans-and-non-binary-people/
- **Data gap worth stating: Canada has no systematic count of trans deaths.** There is no coroner/police reporting line that isolates homicides of trans people by province, so per-province trans violence cannot be measured; the 2021 StatCan gender-related-homicide table is the closest proxy and should not be read as trans-specific. Researchers have explicitly called this "the urgent need for coroners' data on trans deaths in Canada". — https://perspectivesjournal.ca/counting-the-dead/
- **Immigration/document dimension is federal** (port of entry, passport markers, detention), so it does not vary by ADM1; it does not drive regional differences here.
- Note the direction of travel: New Brunswick shows a provincial reversal is realistic within an election cycle; Alberta shows the reverse. Assignments for these two units should be treated as time-sensitive.
