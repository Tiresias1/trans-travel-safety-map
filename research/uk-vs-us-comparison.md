## Why US stays clearly below UK
1. **Axis 1 (border)** is the single biggest traveler-specific differentiator. It is
   high-PROBABILITY (every foreign trans visitor transits the US border) and well-
   documented (7+ foreign-government advisories, ESTA "sex at birth" requirement, device
   searches). The UK has no equivalent trans-specific entry friction. This alone anchors
   the US lower.
2. **Axis 3 criminal exposure** in 4 US states (FL/ID/KS/TX — felony in Idaho, bounty
   suits in Kansas, detainment in Texas) is CRIMINAL; the UK's nationwide facilities code
   is civil/harassment-level (no criminal penalty). Criminal exposure > civil friction.
3. **Axis K**: US has federal executive demonization (2025 EO); UK's rollback is courts/
   EHRC-led (deteriorating but not executive-led).

## Why UK came down 0.74 → 0.71
The original UK note weighted only the axis-3 facilities code. New axis-4/5 findings:
- **Axis 4:** post-April-2025 Supreme Court ruling, UK police forces adopted biological-
  sex search policies — British Transport Police strip-searches trans women with male
  officers; Police Scotland + Surrey/Sussex "Searching Biological Sex Policy"; challenged
  by Sex Matters (2026). A degrading, nationwide, severe-when-triggered risk for arrested
  trans women.
- **Axis 5:** *For Women Scotland v Scottish Ministers* [2026] CSOH 59 declared SPS
  guidance unlawful — trans women moved out of women's prisons to men's (by biological
  sex); HMPPS presumption against women's estate; BBC documents suicide-risk litigation.
  Comparable to France/Germany's men's-prison issue, now nationally directioned post-SC.
These are severe-when-triggered (require arrest, lower probability than axis-3 daily code)
so the UK comes down modestly, not sharply. The UK remains above the US because the US's
worst risks are higher-PROBABILITY (border hits everyone) and CRIMINAL (4 states).
