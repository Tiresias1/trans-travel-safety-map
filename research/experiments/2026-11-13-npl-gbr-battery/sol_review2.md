## 1. Score the US around 0.33—but distinguish calibration from aggregation

Given the settled findings, **0.33 is a defensible provisional US national score**, with **0.30–0.35 as the calibration range**, not a statistical confidence interval. Do not retain 0.264 merely because instrument A produced it, or adopt B merely because 0.344 happens to match the consensus.

The evidence establishes a **calibration mismatch**, not its precise computational cause. Plausible mechanisms are:

- **Anchor/comparator effects:** different reference countries evoke different standards of severity.
- **Scope inflation:** consequences applying in particular jurisdictions or conditional encounters get treated as uniformly national.
- **Double counting:** the same federal exposure enters the national assessment and then reappears as a separate state-level penalty.
- **Inconsistent severity translation:** comparable tier-1 consequences receive different numerical penalties across assessments.

These require an audit, not an assumption. **Tier-1 dominance does not, by itself, imply 0.264:** it determines priority among harms, not a unique numerical distance on this deliberately underdefined scale.

B’s agreement with consensus may therefore be partly accidental. Its UK–Nepal distortion demonstrates that matching one country’s level does not validate the instrument.

## 2. Make divisions comparable before making them foundational

Each division should receive a **complete destination assessment under one common rubric**:

> Applicable national baseline + applicable local additions/protections + local enforcement conditions.

That is a conceptual decomposition, not necessarily additive arithmetic.

National rules must carry into every affected division. Local protections deserve credit only where they actually alter the visitor’s consequences; they cannot erase an unaffected national custody rule. Conversely, local hostility must not be charged once in the national baseline and again as a local increment.

Then calibrate divisions through:

1. **One fixed anchor ladder**, shared across countries and national/division assessments.
2. **Matched comparisons:** similar exposure profiles across US states, UK nations, Nepalese provinces, and other countries.
3. **Blinded re-rating and repeated judgments**, to identify systematic offsets and uncertainty.
4. **Consistency checks:** equivalent consequences should receive comparable scores; added hostility should not improve a score.

Do not prevent divisions from “lowering” the national score by imposing a floor. A genuine destination risk should lower it. Remove **spurious penalties, duplication, and comparator noise**, not unfavorable results.

The hostile-layer rule also needs care: a division adding hostility cannot outperform its **otherwise identical parent-baseline counterfactual**. It need not invariably fall below the eventual national average, which includes other divisions’ conditions.

## 3. Recommended instrument: calibrated destination aggregation, independently audited

A visitor-weighted mean is a reasonable operational instrument **after division calibration**, with an important qualification: an arithmetic mean assumes the scores are sufficiently interval-like. Undefined intermediate anchors do not establish that assumption.

Use:

\[
S_c=\sum_i w_{ci}s_{ci}
\]

where every \(s_{ci}\) already incorporates applicable national exposure.

**Weights:** preferably international leisure-visitor nights by division, over a stable multiyear period. Use the same definition across countries; disclose proxies and run sensitivity checks using visits and equal weights. Include border/entry exposure in the assessment, not merely in overnight geography.

These weights describe **where visits occur**, not a recommendation to pass, hide, or avoid discovery. Score consequences after discovery in every destination. A traveler-specific itinerary score can be offered separately.

Retain national-framework assessment as an **independent diagnostic**, not a competing score silently averaged into the result. Large disagreements trigger review.

**Crucially, existing data do not prove this formula will yield US 0.33 or preserve a substantial Nepal–UK gap.** That requires corrected division scores and actual weights. For now:

- Publish US **0.33 provisionally**, grounded in the settled calibration.
- Preserve the independently supported **Nepal-above-UK ordering**.
- Do not manufacture precise revised UK/Nepal numbers or tune weights to obtain desired outcomes.

## 4. First fix

**Run a matched national-to-division propagation and calibration audit before collecting elaborate weights.**

Start with US federal/state profiles and UK/Nepal division profiles: verify identical national exposures are inherited consistently, not omitted or counted twice, then rate them against the same anchors.

That directly tests the central failure mode. Better weights cannot repair incomparable inputs.

**Supported:** A under-scores the US under the settled calibration; B currently distorts relative ratings.  
**Uncertain:** the exact source of A’s error, corrected division values, and the weighted national outputs.
