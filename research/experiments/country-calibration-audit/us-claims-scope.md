### Exact quoted sentences

- **Wikipedia (EO 14201 article), reference [19] = the Guardian article** [direct]:
  > "On February 25, 2025, the State Department announced a ban on transgender athletes from entering
  > the United States if they attempt to compete in women's sports, and that visa applicants suspected
  > of such would have their file marked with the letters 'SWS25' for the purposes of tracking. Visa
  > applicants for any purpose who list a gender other than their assigned sex on their visa
  > application will be permanently banned from entering the United States on grounds of 'fraud'."

  Identical wording appears in `Persecution of transgender people under the second Trump
  administration` (section "Executive Order 14201"), where its citation [93] is **the same Guardian

  rest the "any purpose / will be permanently banned" sentence on one Guardian article. [direct]

- **The Guardian, 25 Feb 2025 (lead)** [direct]:
  > "The US state department has ordered officials worldwide to deny visas to transgender athletes
  > attempting to come to the US for sports competitions and to issue permanent visa bans against
  > those who are deemed to misrepresent their birth sex on visa applications."

- **The Guardian (on the cable and the statute)** [direct]:
  > "The 24 February state department cable obtained by the Guardian instructs visa officers to apply

- **The Guardian, quoting the cable (materiality language)** [direct]:
  > "'In cases where applicants are suspected of misrepresenting their purpose of travel or sex, you
  > should consider whether this misrepresentation is material such that it supports an ineligibility
  > finding,' reads the directive from the US secretary of state, Marco Rubio."

- **The Guardian, ACLU (Sarah Mehta, senior policy counsel)** [direct]:
  > "It's normal to say that people accused of fraud or misrepresentation are often considered to be
  > ineligible. It's usually a case-by-case determination."

- **The Guardian, State Department statement on enforcement** [direct]:
  > "When asked how the US government will be enforcing this new visa rule with other international
  > bodies who don't have a transgender ban in place, the state department said it would be operating
  > on a 'case-by-case basis'. … 'Visa applications are adjudicated on a case-by-case basis, and we
  > cannot speculate on whether someone may or may not be eligible for a visa.'"

- **The Guardian, cable tracking mechanism** [direct]:
  > "The state department cable instructs consular officers to examine birth certificates when
  > documentation conflicts and mark all cases with 'SWS25' to track enforcement across consular posts
  > worldwide."

- **Immigration Policy Tracking Project (copy of cable text, sections 6 and 7 quoted)** [direct]:
  > Section 6: "'all visas must reflect an applicant's sex at birth' … officials [may] 'refuse the case
  > under 221(g)' and request additional evidence of birth sex if there is a discrepancy or 'reasonable
  > doubt' as to the applicant's sex."
  > Section 7: officers should, for applicants "'suspected of misrepresenting their purpose of travel or
  > sex'", "'consider whether the misrepresentation is material such that it supports an ineligibility
  > finding under INA section 212(a)(6)(C)(i)'."

- **Meyner and Landis LLP client alert (law-firm reading of the same cable)** [direct]:
  > "While the Cable appears to be focused on preventing transgender athletes from traveling to the U.S.
  > to compete in women's sports, the Cable includes sections that are broadly phrased to effectively
  > apply to all transgender visa applicants - not just transgender athletes."
  > "…an applicant's intentional and 'material' misrepresentation of their purpose of travel or their
  > sex may be basis for denial of a visa and/or admission…"

- **EO 14201 § 4(c) (primary text, via IPTP)** [direct]:
  > directs DOS and DHS to "review and adjust, as needed, policies permitting admission to the United
  > States of males seeking to participate in women's sports" and to issue guidance "with an objective
  > of preventing such entry to the extent permitted by law, including pursuant to section
  > 212(a)(6)(C)(i) of the Immigration and Nationality Act".

### Who is covered
- Guardian framing: **transgender athletes** (and applications relating to competing in women's sports),
  with the SWS25 tracking code attached to that push. [direct]
- Cable text as quoted by two independent professional sources (IPTP; Meyner and Landis): the operative
  provisions ("all visas must reflect an applicant's sex at birth"; materiality of sex
  misrepresentation) are **phrased for all immigrant and nonimmigrant visa applicants**, i.e. on their
  face broader than athletes. The law firm states the discrepancy resolution route (221(g) refusal)
  applies generally. [direct]
- Wikipedia's "for any purpose … will be permanently banned" sentence is therefore **broader than the
  single source it cites** (the Guardian article frames athletes and reports case-by-case
  adjudication); Wikipedia's broader reading matches the law-firm/secondary reading of the cable text,
  but that is not what the cited reference establishes. [interpretation]

### Automatic vs discretionary
- **Not automatic on the cited evidence.** The consequence requires (i) a suspicion of misrepresentation
  of purpose of travel *or* sex, (ii) a judgment that the misrepresentation is **material**, and
  (iii) an ineligibility finding under INA §212(a)(6)(C)(i) — expressly "case-by-case" per both the
  ACLU and the State Department. A mere discrepancy triggers a **221(g) refusal plus request for
  evidence**, which is not the permanent bar. Waivers exist ("limited waiver possibilities" —
  Guardian; INA §212(i)/§212(d)(3) route noted in practitioner sources). [direct + interpretation]
- Wikipedia's "will be permanently banned" states an automatic, certain outcome; **no fetched source
  supports automaticity**. [interpretation]

### Enforcement evidence
- **None located.** No fetched source reports an actual §212(a)(6)(C)(i) permanent bar imposed on an
  applicant for a gender-marker mismatch, no SWS25 outcome data, no published case counts. The
  Guardian reports only the cable and the "case-by-case" posture. Later State Department activity found
  in follow-up searches: a 11 March 2026 final rule "Visas: Enhancing Vetting and Combatting Fraud in
  the Diversity Immigrant Visa Program" (changes "gender" to "sex" on the Diversity Visa form), plus
  passport sex-marker policy — **[snippet]** only, not fetched, and not evidence of fraud-bar
  enforcement. [direct for the absence in fetched sources; snippet for 2026 items]

### Verdict (a): **partly supported, scope unclear**
Exact reason: the mechanism exists and is documented — a 24 Feb 2025 Rubio cable invokes the permanent
fraud bar INA §212(a)(6)(C)(i) for alleged misrepresentation of **sex or travel purpose**, and its
quoted text is phrased for all visa applicants, not only athletes [direct]. But (1) the consequence is
**discretionary and case-by-case with an explicit materiality test**, not an automatic consequence of
"listing a gender other than assigned sex"; a plain discrepancy produces a 221(g) refusal and evidence
request instead; (2) the two Wikipedia pages assert "for any purpose … **will be** permanently banned"
while their **only cited reference** (Guardian, 25 Feb 2025) describes athletes, materiality and
case-by-case adjudication — so the breadth of covered applicants is asserted, not demonstrated, by the
citation chain; and (3) **no enforcement evidence** (actual permanent bans, SWS25 results) was found in
the bounded search. Neither the breadth nor the automaticity in the claim as stated is verified; the
documented risk (potential, discretionary fraud bar reachable in any visa case where sex is alleged to
be materially misrepresented) is real and is not being downplayed here.

### (b1) FBI cash bounties / "domestic terrorist groups" (December 2025)

**Exact quoted sentences** [direct]:
- Wikipedia: "In December 2025, Attorney General Pam Bondi instructed the FBI to begin offering cash
  bounties for information leading to the identification and arrest of transgender activists promoting
  what she referred to as 'radical gender ideology', and described such activists as 'domestic
  terrorist groups'."
- Pink News (17 Dec 2025): "US attorney general Pam Bondi has instructed the FBI to place bounties on
  trans activists promoting 'radical gender ideology,' **a leaked memo suggests**." / "…create a 'cash
  reward system' incentivising members of the public to share information on what the attorney's
  office described as 'domestic terrorist groups.'" / "These groups, according to Bondi, include
  groups or individuals promoting so-called 'radical gender ideology' and 'hostility towards traditional
  values on family, religion, and morality.'"
- LGBTQ Nation (16 Dec 2025), quoting the memo: "The FBI shall establish a cash reward system for
  information that leads to the successful identification and arrest of **individuals in the leadership
  of domestic terrorist organizations**," the memo reads. The memo, dated December 5, was leaked." /
  "…it also makes it clear that the targets … will be 'Antifa-aligned extremists,' including those
  promoting 'radical gender ideology'." / memo: perpetrators are "certain Antifa-aligned extremists"
  whose "animating principle is adherence to the types of extreme viewpoints on immigration, radical
  gender ideology, and anti-American sentiment."

**Who is covered** — the memo language quoted by LGBTQ Nation is about **leaders of domestic terrorist
organizations** and "certain Antifa-aligned extremists" whose ideology includes "radical gender
ideology"; Pink News says "groups or individuals promoting … 'radical gender ideology'". Neither
quoted passage establishes bounties aimed at "transgender activists" **as such** (i.e. because they
are trans or trans-aligned advocates generally). Wikipedia's "described such activists as 'domestic
terrorist groups'" is also grammatically/scopally off: the quoted sources describe **organizations /
"Antfa-aligned extremists"**, not individual activists as "groups". [interpretation]

**Automatic vs discretionary** — the quoted directive is an instruction to *establish a system*
("shall establish a cash reward system") tied to domestic-terrorist-organization leadership; it is not
an automatic bounty on any identified person, and designation/membership predicates apply. [direct +
interpretation]

**Enforcement evidence** — **none verified.** No fetched source shows a reward actually posted, paid,
or used; Pink News explicitly hedges ("a leaked memo suggests"). **[snippet, not fetched]:** LA Times,
11 Dec 2025, "Justice Department drafting a list of 'domestic terrorists'"; GovExec (July 2026) that
NSPM-7 is "the legal engine behind a wave of terrorism prosecutions against left-wing protesters";
Arnold & Porter (Dec 2025) on the DOJ directive singling out "Antfa-aligned extremists". These are
discovery aids only in this run.

**Verdict (b1): narrower than stated.**
Exact reason: a leaked, dated Attorney General memorandum directing the FBI to create a cash-reward
system for information leading to identification/arrest of **leaders of domestic terrorist
organizations**, with "Antfa-aligned extremists" whose ideology includes "radical gender ideology"
named as the priority, is supported by two consistent secondary reports quoting the memo [direct].
But the claim as worded ("bounties for identification/arrest of **transgender activists** … described
as domestic terrorist groups") overstates scope on both ends — the covered population in the quoted
text is organization leaders / Antfa-aligned extremists, not trans activists generally, and "domestic
terrorist groups" in the source refers to those organizations, not to trans activists — and **no
enforcement evidence** (posted or paid bounties) was found. Primary memo PDF text could not be read
directly (image-only; see limitations), so wording rests on outlet quotations.

### (b2) May 2026 counterterrorism strategy language

**Exact quoted sentences [direct, primary source] — whitehouse.gov PDF, May 2026, p. 7:**
> "…Americans have witnessed the politically motivated killings of Christians and conservatives
> committed by violent left-wing extremists, including the assassination of Charlie Kirk by a radical
> who espoused extreme transgender ideologies. **In addition to cartels and Islamist terror groups, our
> national CT activities will also prioritize the rapid identification and neutralization of violent
> secular political groups whose ideology is anti-American, radically pro-transgender, and anarchist.**
> We will use all the tools constitutionally available to us to map them at home, identify their
> membership, map their ties to international organizations like Antifa, and use law enforcement tools
> to cripple them operationally before they can maim or kill the innocent."

Also [direct, same PDF]: threat taxonomy lists "**Violent Left-Wing Extremists, including Anarchists
and Anti-Fascists**" as one of three major terror-group types (p. 5); and, p. 4: "Our counterterrorism
powers will not be used to target our fellow Americans who simply disagree with us."

The Wikipedia quotation matches the primary document **verbatim**. [direct]

**Who is covered:** "**violent secular political groups** whose ideology is anti-American, radically
pro-transgender, and anarchist" — a group-level, violence-linked category, not transgender people,
visitors, or non-violent trans advocates. The document simultaneously disclaims targeting
disagreement. [direct + interpretation]

**Verdict (b2): broadly supported as stated** (as a quotation claim: the language exists verbatim in
the primary May 2026 White House document). Scope caution: if the dossier renders this as "the
counterterrorism strategy treats transgender activists as terrorists", that would be **narrower than
the source** — the text targets *violent* secular political groups with *radically pro-transgender*
ideology, in a list with anarchists and Antifa ties. [interpretation]

**Overall verdict (b): partly supported, scope unclear** — b1 is narrower than stated with no
enforcement evidence; b2 is verbatim-verified primary-source language but narrower in coverage than
any "transgender activists = terrorists" paraphrase would imply.
