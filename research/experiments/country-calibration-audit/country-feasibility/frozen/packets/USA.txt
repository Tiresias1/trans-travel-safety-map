### ASSESSMENT
Travel documents are the sharpest immediate exposure for a trans visitor to the country. An executive order signed 20 January 2025 redefined sex for national purposes as a male-female binary determined at conception or birth, barred transgender people from government-funded single-sex facilities congruent with their gender identity, prohibited self-selection of gender on passports and visas, and directed the national justice department to "correct the misapplication" of a 2020 Supreme Court employment-discrimination ruling. The same order required national agencies to use "sex" rather than "gender," ordered removal of materials it calls gender ideology, rescinded policies requiring use of names and pronouns consistent with a person's gender identity in national workplaces, prohibited national funding and promotion of gender-identity policies, and rescinded an earlier executive order addressing LGBTQ rights in national prisons. The foreign ministry stopped processing "X"-marker passports and markers not matching sex assigned at birth in February 2025; existing "X"-marker passports remain valid until expiration, and new applications are processed only as M or F per supporting documents. In November 2025 the Supreme Court allowed the passport sex-marker restrictions to take effect, superseding a foreign ministry policy in place since 1992. Trans and nonbinary travelers have reported passports confiscated or blocked at processing, leaving some stranded abroad; a national civil-liberties union sued the foreign ministry on behalf of seven plaintiffs, and the policy remains challenged in national courts. Entry screening carries its own exposure. In February 2025 the foreign ministry announced a ban on trans athletes entering the country to compete in women's sports, and its 24 February 2025 cable instructs posts to add the case-note tag — the same text in all cases refused or pending an advisory opinion under the February 2025 executive order. Where there is a discrepancy in an applicant's documents or records, or other evidence casting reasonable doubt on the applicant's sex, the cable instructs officers to refuse the case and request additional evidence; where an applicant is suspected of wilfully misrepresenting sex or purpose of travel, the adjudicating officer should consider whether the misrepresentation is material such that it supports a permanent, lifetime ineligibility finding under the immigration code's fraud ground — a finding that requires all four elements, for which limited waiver possibilities exist, and which is never automatic. Multiple foreign governments responded: by March 2025 at least eight governments in one region, including two named neighbouring jurisdictions, had adjusted travel guidelines for trans and nonbinary citizens entering the country, following detentions of citizens from a neighbouring jurisdiction over visa issues; by April 2025 several governments were issuing advisories about trans people travelling here over identity-document and data rules. Facilities access is restricted in a growing number of state/provinces. The equality map (data as of 7 November 2026) splits these bans into disjoint groups: 9 state/provinces and 1 state/province prohibit facilities consistent with a person's gender identity in all government-owned buildings, including K-12 schools and colleges; 9 other state/provinces cover K-12 schools and at least some government-owned buildings; and 3 state/provinces cover K-12 schools only. In 6 state/provinces these bans apply to at least some private settings, while all other bans currently on the books apply only to government-owned settings. A further 29 state/provinces, 4 state/provinces and the capital district have no such ban in these mapped categories, though the map notes that other state/province laws or policies defining sex may still affect access beyond the laws shown on the map. It flags 4 state/provinces where state law makes such use a criminal offence in certain circumstances; in one of them the statutory trigger is trespass, not automatic arrest: a person, subject to the exemptions in the subsection and excluding the building's own government employees, who willfully enters a restroom or changing facility designated for the opposite sex at a public building and refuses to depart when asked by an employee of the governmental entity with jurisdiction over that building commits the offence of trespass. On 19 March 2025 a transgender student was arrested in a women's restroom at a state/province capitol and charged with trespass; the arrest was reported in April 2025, noting she was handcuffed, and civil-rights attorneys described it as the first known arrest under that state/province's bathroom-restriction law. State/province document rules are tightening. In February 2026 one state/province enacted a law invalidating birth certificates and driver's licences that do not match sex assigned at birth and barring trans people from sex-segregated facilities; rights groups called it a template, and further targeting bills are pending in state/province legislatures. A national equality map sets out which state/provinces still allow gender-marker changes by licence or birth certificate and under what conditions. A national risk assessment dated 13 July 2026 flagged one additional state/province as the latest "Do Not Travel" state/province for trans people, bringing the total to 4 so flagged, and recorded that the 2026 legislative season continued to degrade trans rights while national policies drove nationwide trends. Other national measures in force or in litigation include a military ban executive order, an executive order of 28 January 2025 describing gender-affirming care for minors as "chemical and surgical mutilation of children," an executive order of 29 January 2025 directing law enforcement to investigate schools promoting "gender ideology" and to criminally prosecute teachers who facilitate a trans minor's social transition, including using preferred names or pronouns, a women's sports ban executive order of 5 February 2025, a January 2025 national funding freeze, and an October 2025 rule blocking student loan forgiveness for public workers providing gender-affirming care to transgender youth. Several of these orders have been blocked or enjoined in court. Custody and detention carry documented harm. From 2026 the national corrections bureau adopted one state/province's policies mandating what the source describes as conversion therapy for transgender inmates, including cutting hair short, removing hormone medication, and substituting psychiatric therapy and drugs; imprisoned transgender individuals reported denial of care, and the corrections bureau stopped reporting the number of transgender prisoners held from 2025. In immigration custody, transgender men were reportedly subjected to forced-labour programmes with guards justifying it with statements such as "If you wanna be a man, I'll treat you like a man"; those who refused faced beatings and solitary confinement, and many also reported severe sexual abuse. Official hostility extends to advocacy and speech. In December 2025 the national justice department instructed a national law-enforcement agency to establish a cash reward system for information leading to the identification and arrest of individuals in the leadership of domestic terrorist organizations that conspire with others to commit violations of the provisions of law listed in section 2 of the guidance or other offenses against the country; the same memorandum uses the phrase "radical gender ideology" twice — once among the extreme viewpoints it says animate actors "with a willingness to use violence against law-abiding citizenry," and once in a list of political and social agendas attributed to violent extremist groups more generally — and never as a criterion for the reward. In May 2026 the government's counterterrorism strategy stated that national counterterrorism activities would "prioritize the rapid identification and neutralization of violent secular political groups whose ideology is hostile to the country, radically pro-transgender, and anarchist." Advocates have compared the administration's actions to the early stages of genocide. Marriage recognition stands on a different footing from the document and facilities regimes. Same-sex marriage is recognized nationwide under a 2015 Supreme Court ruling, and a December 2022 statute repealed the earlier national marriage-defence act and required all state/provinces to recognize existing same-sex marriages; recognition still varies, with same-sex marriages not licensed or recognized in one outlying state/province and status differing across sovereign tribal nations. Employment discrimination on the basis of gender identity is prohibited nationally under a 2020 Supreme Court ruling, and gender-identity hate crimes are punishable under a national hate-crimes statute, though only 23 state/provinces cover gender identity in state/province bias-motivated violence statutes and 37 lacked anti-bias statutes for gender-identity crimes as of 2019. Twenty-two state/provinces plus the capital district outlaw discrimination based on gender identity or expression. Public opinion data from a 2025 poll cited in the sources found 66% of residents supported laws requiring assigned sex at birth to be listed on government documents such as driver's licences and passports (89% of one major party's supporters, 66% of independents, 38% of the other major party's), and 69% supported laws requiring transgender athletes to compete aligned with assigned sex at birth. Gender-affirming care for children is already banned in 27 state/provinces, and in October 2025 proposed national health rules would bar public insurance reimbursement for care to transgender patients under 18.

### SECONDARY / SITUATIONAL FACTORS
The national posture is contested in court. The civil-liberties union's suit against the foreign ministry over passport markers, the challenges to the January 2025 executive orders, and the October 2025 proposed health rules are all unresolved; several orders have been blocked or enjoined. A traveller's practical exposure therefore depends on which measures are in effect at the moment of travel, and a legal-aid organisation maintains a running FAQ on passport and identity-document rules for transgender and gender-nonconforming people under the national policy change, covering what documentation to consider and how name and gender changes interact with national records. The state/province-level picture is uneven and moving. National equality maps and identity-document maps are the standard trackers for bathroom bans and marker-change rules; state/province scorecards (2024 snapshot) show three state/provinces supporting gender-marker updates, transgender healthcare, public-accommodations protections and hate-crimes protections, while one supported none of those categories and two only partially supported marker updates. A travel guide (27 April 2026) notes there is no national sodomy law, that safety varies by state/province, and advises checking recent violence reports before travel. Comparatively, the country is now the subject of travel advisories from allied governments rather than an issuer of them: at least eight governments in one region adjusted guidance for trans and nonbinary citizens by March 2025, and further advisories followed by April 2025. The 2023 legislative count recorded more than 190 of over 470 introduced anti-LGBTQ bills as specifically anti-trans, most passed bills focused on banning gender-affirming health care, with bathroom access, athletics and transgender healthcare for minors the top contested areas.

### AFFECTS RESIDENTS MORE THAN VISITORS
Residents face the document regime directly: from the January 2025 executive order, new and renewed national passports show sex assigned at birth regardless of gender identity or name change, and the "X" marker, available since 2022, has been eliminated for new issues. Residents of one state/province can no longer change markers on birth certificates or driver's licences under the February 2026 law, which rights groups describe as a template other state/provinces may copy. National employees lost the policy requiring use of names and pronouns consistent with a person's gender identity in the workplace, and a January 2025 list of "forbidden terms" including "gender," "transgender" and "LGBT" was distributed within national health-related agencies, with such words removed from national agency websites including a national monument site and over 350 pages about the LGBTQ community removed entirely; scientific grants mentioning LGBT topics were revoked. Transgender inmates in national custody are subject to the corrections bureau policies adopted from 2026 on one state/province's model, and the bureau stopped reporting the number of transgender prisoners held from 2025. In immigration custody, transgender men have reported forced labour, beatings, solitary confinement and severe sexual abuse. In schools, an executive order directs law enforcement to investigate schools promoting "gender ideology" and to criminally prosecute teachers who facilitate a trans minor's social transition, including using preferred names or pronouns, potentially as sexual exploitation of a minor or practicing medicine without a license, with national funding revoked for violating schools. Service access has narrowed: in July 2025 the national government shut down specialized LGBTQ youth services on the national crisis lifeline; in August 2025 it threatened to pull national funding from sex education programs mentioning transgender people, and 11 state/provinces and 2 state/provinces complied by November 2025. In summer 2025 the national justice department subpoenaed "over 20" hospitals for full records of patients under 19 including names, social security numbers and doctors' notes. In December 2025 the national justice department instructed a national law-enforcement agency to establish a cash reward system for information leading to the identification and arrest of individuals in the leadership of domestic terrorist organizations that conspire to commit the violations listed in section 2 of the guidance or other offenses against the country, and the May 2026 counterterrorism strategy stated that national counterterrorism activities would prioritize the rapid identification and neutralization of violent secular political groups whose ideology is hostile to the country, radically pro-transgender, and anarchist — a category the same document lists alongside violent left-wing extremists, including anarchists and anti-fascists — while stating that counterterrorism powers will not be used to target people who simply disagree with it. Protection machinery remains partial: gender-identity hate crimes are punishable nationally under a hate-crimes statute, but only 23 state/provinces cover gender identity in state/province bias-motivated violence statutes, and 37 lacked anti-bias statutes for gender-identity crimes as of 2019. Twenty-two state/provinces plus the capital district outlaw discrimination based on gender identity or expression. National crime statistics report bias against sexual orientation has consistently accounted for between one-seventh and one-fifth of single-bias reported hate incidents this century.

### DELIBERATELY NOT COUNTED
Several claims in the source inventory remain unresolved or unverified in this record. Whether the passport marker policy will survive the pending litigation is not settled; the sources describe the policy as challenged in national courts without a final outcome. The October 2025 proposed health rules barring public insurance reimbursement for care to transgender patients under 18 are described as proposed, not final. The corrections-bureau conversion-therapy-style policies and the immigration-custody forced labour and abuse claims are drawn from a single encyclopedic source in this inventory and are not independently corroborated here. The December 2025 cash-bounty instruction and the May 2026 counterterrorism strategy language are documented in primary documents. As of November 2026, no documented instance of the permanent fraud ground under the immigration code being imposed for a gender-marker discrepancy and no posted reward were found in available sources; whether and how these directives are implemented in practice is not established by the documents. The 4-state/province "Do Not Travel" designation is the assessment of one advocacy publication (13 July 2026), not a government advisory, and the criteria behind it are not stated in the inventory. The claim that the bathroom arrest was the first of its kind rests on civil-rights attorneys' characterisation reported in April 2025, and no later arrest under any state/province bathroom law is documented in this inventory. No trans-specific killing or violence rate for the country appears in this inventory, so no comparison to the general homicide rate can be made from these sources. The inventory contains no data on airport screening treatment specific to trans or gender-nonconforming travellers beyond the visa-flagging measure of February 2025.
