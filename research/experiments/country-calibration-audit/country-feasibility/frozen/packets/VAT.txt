### ASSESSMENT
No criminal provision in the country's law reaches consensual same-sex sexual activity between adults in private and non-commercial. The legal code derives from the 1889 code of a neighbouring jurisdiction and has carried no such offence since 1890, including through the founding of the sovereign state in 1929. Nothing in the criminal law addresses being transgender. The age of consent is 18 for all persons regardless of gender. A lower age of 14 applies to sexual relations within a marriage recognised as valid by the dominant religious institution and by the country's law. The country has no legal mechanism for changing a gender marker on identity documents, and no anti-discrimination law covering sexual orientation or gender identity applies in the country. For a trans visitor, it is unknown whether a trans-specific rule governs access to single-sex facilities, and no document-amendment route exists for a resident or a visitor. The country operates no prison system. It maintains a few cells for pre-trial detention, and a person sentenced to imprisonment by the country serves the term in prisons in a neighbouring jurisdiction at the country's expense. There is no trans-specific prisoner-placement policy and no documented case of a trans prisoner held under the country's authority. A religious education office's 2019 document frames 'gender theory' as denying the difference and reciprocity of man and woman and as eliminating the anthropological basis of the family. It is an educational instruction addressed to religious institutions rather than a rule binding visitors, but it states the governing institution's position on gender identity. A 2023 declaration by the doctrinal office permitted spontaneous blessings for same-sex couples while forbidding any rite resembling marriage. A January 2024 press release reiterated that blessings never extend to the unions themselves. The country has consistently rejected civil recognition of same-sex unions, same-sex marriage, and the granting of adoption rights to same-sex couples. The head of the dominant religious institution in 2023 expressed support for same-sex civil unions outside that institution while maintaining that marriage is between a man and a woman as instituted by God. In 2021 the foreign minister wrote to the ambassador of a neighbouring jurisdiction objecting to a parliamentary bill there meant to protect LGBT people against violence and discrimination. The objection targeted the provision prohibiting incitement of hatred on the basis of sexual orientation and gender identity, which the letter described as a violation of freedom of speech and of religion, and asked for the draft law's text to be reformulated. On 13 January 1998 an activist set himself on fire in a central square, under the country's jurisdiction, in protest at the dominant religion's refusal of homosexuality. He died days later in hospital from severe burns.

### SOURCE EVIDENCE
- The nation operates no prison system — only a few cells used for pre-trial detention — and persons sentenced to imprisonment by the nation serve their time in another nation's prisons with costs covered by the nation; there is no trans-specific prisoner-placement policy, trans custody facility, or documented trans-prisoner placement case. The sole prison-axis source is a general reference article on crime in the nation, used as a last resort.
- The 2024 declaration states that 'any sex-change intervention, as a rule, risks threatening the unique dignity the person has received from the moment of conception', while not excluding medical assistance for genital abnormalities evident at birth or developing later, which in that case would not constitute a sex change in its sense; it condemns 'gender theory' as 'extremely dangerous since it cancels differences' and separately denounces the imprisonment, torture and killing of people solely because of their sexual orientation. The sources report no documented mechanism for changing a gender marker on identity documents and no anti-discrimination law covering sexual orientation or gender identity; an educational document (2019) opposes 'gender theory' as denying natural difference. The 2024 declaration and the 2019 educational document express institutional doctrine, not rules binding visitors.
- A 2023 declaration permits spontaneous blessings for same-sex couples while forbidding any rite resembling marriage, with a January 2024 clarification reiterating that blessings never extend to the unions themselves.
- Nothing in the criminal law addresses being transgender. The sources cited record no trans-specific practice at entry, at screening, or in access to facilities for a trans visitor, and do not establish what document checks or screening visitors face; neither trans-specific danger nor trans-specific safety can be inferred from them.

### SECONDARY / SITUATIONAL FACTORS
Accreditation rules for foreign diplomats posted to the government bar anyone who is part of a same-sex family and anyone who is divorced. A neighbouring jurisdiction's openly gay nominee, in a civil pact, was rejected in 2008, and an openly gay practising single nominee was rejected in 2015. That jurisdiction left the post vacant from March 2015 until May 2016 before nominating another diplomat. In October 2008 officials of the dominant religious institution rejected that jurisdiction's first-choice candidate as ambassador because he is gay, forcing it to name a different envoy, a documented instance of the government refusing an LGBT person in diplomatic relations. The governing institution's posture against homosexuality and gender theory drives policy in diplomatic and internal matters, while the country's own criminal law has not criminalised consensual same-sex conduct in modern times. The legal baseline of the country and the doctrinal position of the institution that governs it are separate layers.

### AFFECTS RESIDENTS MORE THAN VISITORS
The government reserves the right to remove, suspend or dismiss immediately any official or employee who publicly admits to being gay or who questions the general policy of the government towards homosexuals. The provision applies to people working for the governing institutions rather than to visitors. No anti-discrimination protection on grounds of sexual orientation or gender identity applies to residents of the country, and no legal gender recognition procedure exists for them.

### DELIBERATELY NOT COUNTED
No human rights report chapter issued by a national government covers the country for 2023 or 2024, so the assessment rests on encyclopedic and institutional sources rather than a government human rights report. The absence of a documented trans-prisoner placement case reflects the absence of a domestic prison system and of published records, not a finding that no trans person has ever been held in pre-trial detention in the country. Whether the government's stated right to dismiss officials and employees who publicly acknowledge being gay has been exercised in specific cases is not documented in the sources reviewed. Enforcement practice at entry, at screening, or in access to facilities for a trans visitor is not documented. No source records a trans visitor being stopped, questioned or refused entry on grounds of gender identity.
