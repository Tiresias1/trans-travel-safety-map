# Scotland–England gap investigation (+0.008)

**Question.** Final scores are Scotland 0.463569 vs England 0.455856 = **+0.0077**
(parent GBR 0.437957; Scotland +0.0256, England +0.0179). Is that near-tie
correct given Scotland's documented divergence efforts and lower conservatism,
or is it a dossier/rater failure?

**Verdict (summary). (c) Mixed — direction correct, magnitude compressed by
the iterative system, and the challenge's expected large gap is overstated on
visitor-operative grounds.**

- The only surviving Scotland-over-England divergence that is *operative for a
  trans visitor conditional on discovery* is victim-side criminal-law recourse
  (standalone hate-crime offences naming gender identity vs England's
  sentencing-uplift-only regime). That genuinely supports Scotland > England,
  and raters price it consistently at **+0.03 to +0.05 head-to-head** (see §3).
- Almost everything else Scotland "tried" is either **failed without operative
  effect** (GRR Reform / s35), **residents-only** (blocker pauses, Tavistock/Cass
  lineage on both sides), or **genuinely GB-wide** (Equality Act 2010 as read in
  *For Women Scotland*, EHRC code, birth-sex search/custody rules) — and
  Scotland's repaired dossier now documents the GB-wide layer in full, which is
  why its pilot premium (+0.068) collapsed.
- The final +0.008 is **smaller than the +0.04 head-to-head margin raters
  actually award** because finals are alpha-blended stocks seeded 75% toward the
  parent, not head-to-head margins (§3.3). The gap is therefore best read as a
  *conservative system output*, not as "raters think Scotland and England are
  identical."
- **Quantified "true" gap estimate on current dossiers: +0.025 to +0.035**
  (Scotland above England), i.e. roughly 3–4× the synced +0.008 but well below
  the pilot +0.068. After the remaining repairs in §5 it could move ±0.01
  either way (Scotland's undocumented hospital-ward position is the swing item).
- **Social climate (less-conservative Scotland) is entirely absent from both
  dossiers** — zero Scotland-specific trans-attitude or per-capita incident-rate
  sources — so its contribution to the gap is currently **0 by construction**,
  not 0 by evidence. Whether it *should* count is a rubric-scope decision
  (operative-conditional-on-discovery vs street-level climate), flagged for the
  supervisor in §5, not decided here.

No provider calls were made; no data files were written. All rater evidence is
extracted from the archived OK (and rejected-raw) response contents of the two
runs; all dossier evidence is quoted from the live `data/admin1.json` /
`data/countries.json` records and the two reports (pilot
`uk-nations-fix-report.md`, repair re-rate `scotland-repair-rerate-report.md`,
parity audit `scotland-parity-audit.md`).

## 1. Legal-divergence inventory (visitor-operative, conditional on discovery)

Devolution baseline (from the dossiers themselves): criminal law, policing,
prisons and family law are devolved to Scotland; legal gender recognition
(Gender Recognition Act 2004) and equality law (Equality Act 2010) are set at UK
level. England has no devolved legislature — Westminster legislates for England
directly — so "England-specific" policy means UK-government/NHS-England action
that applies to England (usually health), while "UK-wide" means it applies in
Scotland too. Wales shares England's criminal law (England-and-Wales) but has
devolved health/education; Northern Ireland has separate equality law and a
separate equality commission, and police/prisons devolved but documented as a
gap in its dossier.

### 1.2 Does attempt-without-effect (GRR Reform / s35) count? Both ways.

- **For counting (attempt as signal):** the 86–39 vote shows the operative
  legislature's settled preference for recognition reform; the s35 veto was
  unprecedented (first use) and is itself evidence about the *ceiling* on
  devolved protection — a visitor (or a future resident path) faces a UK
  government willing to override devolved trans policy. One pilot rater (mimo,
  iter 19) partially credits it this way ("division's own institutions affirm
  trans protection"), and Scotland's dossier gives it prominent, repeated
  placement (summary + tangential + locals-only).
- **Against counting (operative-only):** the reformed procedure "is not in force,"
  so nothing a visitor can do, carry, or be subjected to differs because of it.
  The rubric is explicitly conditional-on-discovery/operative; haiku (pilot
  iter 6) states the correct discount: "resident-facing document issue." The
  strongest evidence for the against view is that Scotland's dossier *itself*
  frames the episode as futility ("establishing that the parent national
  government can review and stop gender-recognition reform"), and no rater in
  either run prices the vote as a protection — the one rater who mentions it
  (mimo iter 19) *misattributes* the 86–39 vote to the hate-crime law
  ("passed gender-identity hate-crime law 86-39"), conflating a failed reform
  with an operative statute. **Conclusion for this investigation: attempt-without-
  effect counts at most as context for the s35 ceiling, not as a Scotland
  advantage, and the dossiers' current treatment (documented, repeated, explicitly
  "not in force") is the right one.** The challenge's premise that "failed but
  attempted" should widen the gap is not supported under the rubric's own logic.

## 2. Dossier coverage check (symmetry after repair)

### 2.1 What raters cited for Scotland (paired reasoning, both runs)

Extraction method: every OK call in which Scotland appears (pilot n=8, re-rate
n=6 — the "22+20" OK totals minus other pairings), Scotland-side explanation /
synthesis / factor-matrix text searched for cited factors.

- **Hate-crime statute + 104 charges: cited in 8/8 (pilot) and 6/6 (re-rate).**
  Universally phrased as a *recourse signal / enforcement channel*, never as
  proof of visitor safety: "Those are evidence of an available enforcement
  channel, not proof that visitors receive effective protection" (luna, pilot
  iter 2); "recourse for victims of violence, not a rule governing a visitor's
  exposure" (haiku, pilot iter 6); "meaningful evidence of an operating remedy,
  but raw charge counts do not establish prevalence" (luna, re-rate iter 6).
- **Facility/search/custody/ward exposures: cited in 8/8 and 6/6.** In the pilot
  these came from the parent framework (Scotland's own division dossier was
  silent on single-sex services); in the re-rate they come from Scotland's new
  HP paragraph (Holyrood, EHRC interim guidance) *plus* still from the parent
  framework (ward bans, Joelle — see §2.3). The re-rate explanations are
  dominated by the new material: "already applied at the divisional parliament"
  appears in glm iter 4, deepseek iter 9, haiku iter 19, mimo iter 22.
- **GRR Reform / s35:** 4/8 pilot mentions (whole-response search; only 2 in
  Scotland-side explanation text), **0 in re-rate Scotland-side text.** Where
  mentioned, discounted as resident-facing or (mimo) misattributed to hate crime.
  No rater awards Scotland points for the attempt.
- **Cass / Tavistock / Bell:** 1/8 pilot, 0/6 re-rate. Never priced for either nation.
- **Social climate / conservatism:** no Scotland-specific tolerance evidence
  cited by any rater in either run. The only climate language is UK-wide
  ("hostile climate/ranking fall nationally" — muse factor matrix, pilot iter 12;
  "hostile national media rhetoric … rainbow-index fall to 22nd" — mimo factor
  matrix, re-rate iter 22), applied equally to both nations. **Raters weigh
  operative law only, because operative law is all the dossiers contain** (§4).

### 2.2 Does Scotland's dossier document both effort and futility? Yes — unusually well.

Scotland documents the GRR attempt with vote (86–39), mechanism (s35, first
use), and futility ("reformed procedure … is not in force") in summary,
tangential ("can review and stop … before it takes effect"), *and* locals-only
("obtained no new legal-gender pathway"). It documents its restrictions with
equal specificity (search guidance with operative threshold, prison ruling with
date/court/citation, blocker pause in its own record). Its P:R word ratio is
roughly 1:1.4 — it hides nothing. The audit's finding stands: Scotland's pilot
advantage came from *which* protections it documented (operative statute with
in-force date) vs England's single judicial aside, not from hiding restrictions.
Post-repair, Scotland additionally documents the full GB-wide restrictive layer
(Equality Act form of law, Supreme Court holding, EHRC code + interim guidance,
Holyrood application) — the repair worked as intended, and raters now cite it.

### 2.3 Does England's dossier document England-specific restrictiveness as strongly? No — but the shortfall is structural, not one-sided.

- England's division evidence covers the GB-wide layer (definition ruling,
  EHRC code, custody, hate-crime statistics + now s66) but **Cass / NHS England
  blockers / Tavistock / ward bans live only in the GBR parent framework**
  ("Trans hospital patients in England are to be banned…", "NHS England said in
  March 2024…", "The Cass Review (April 2024) also led to the closure of the
  Tavistock GIDS"). Because the parent framework is rendered into *every*
  nation's dossier, England-specific restrictiveness is simultaneously
  (a) visible to raters scoring England, and (b) **wrongly charged to Scotland,
  Wales, and NI** as well. Re-rate evidence: mimo (iter 22) scores *Scotland*
  on "announced hospital-ward bans and a documented fatal care-denial case"
  (Joelle, Wolverhampton — England); deepseek (iter 9) scores Scotland on
  "ward-exclusion policy linked to Joelle's documented death." The parent
  framework's own caveat ("NHS England ward rules apply in a neighbouring
  division only" — Scotland out-of-scope) is buried in the discounted section
  and ignored by raters. **This is the largest remaining asymmetry, and it runs
  against Scotland** — it suppresses the Scotland–England gap.
- Symmetrically, England's division dossier omits s35/GRR context entirely
  (Scotland leads with it; Wales/NI record it in tangential), and omits
  GRC medical-criteria content (NI's dysphoria ruling is documented only in
  NI). So a rater comparing "how hard is legal gender recognition here" gets a
  complete answer from NI, a partial answer from Scotland (reform failed →
  2004 Act continues → criteria unstated), and no answer from England.
- **Size asymmetry persists in words, though not sentences:** post-repair
  Scotland blindSummary = 792 words / 16 sentences vs England 484 / 15
  (1.64× by words). Sentence parity (1.07×) was the repair target and was met,
  but Scotland's sentences are ~50 words vs England's ~32 — the hate-crime
  statistics, vote counts, and search/prison detail are unabridged while
  England's corresponding material is terser. Rendered dossier totals are closer
  (Scotland ~23.3k vs England ~22.2k chars) because England's source block
  (6 items) compensates. This is a mild pro-Scotland salience asymmetry, much
  smaller than the pilot's 1.7–2.7× sentence gap.

### 2.4 Net coverage verdict

Post-repair dossiers are *sufficiently* symmetric to price the one genuine
operative divergence (hate-crime recourse) with both sides on the table — the
re-rate report's claim holds. Three residual asymmetries remain, two of which
*compress* the gap: (1) parent-framework contamination charging England's
ward/Joelle facts to Scotland (anti-Scotland); (2) Scotland's undocumented ward
position (unknown sign); (3) Scotland's 1.64× word density (mildly pro-Scotland).
None justifies discarding the +0.008, but (1)+(2) mean it is likely understated.

### 3.3 Is +0.008 real signal or compression toward the parent anchor? Both — and the arithmetic is exact.

The final is not a margin; it is an alpha-0.2 stock update from a reseeded start
(seed = 0.75·parent + 0.25·old-final):

- Re-rate seeds: Scotland 0.4527 (carries 25% of the inflated pilot final 0.497),
  England 0.4408 — seed gap **+0.0119 in Scotland's favour**.
- Scotland received 6 updates, England 9. Scotland's trajectory: 0.4527 → 0.4742
  (0.56) → 0.4613 (0.41) → 0.4431 (0.37) → 0.4625 (0.54) → 0.4620 (0.46) →
  **0.4636** (0.47). England's swings are larger (down to 0.4261, up to 0.4836)
  because it drew a 0.62 (glm vs GBR) that Scotland never drew.
- Final gap (+0.0077) ≈ decayed seed gap + (1−decay)·draw gap ≈ +0.008, given
  draw gap +0.006. **The system is doing exactly what it was designed to do:
  move slowly toward the draw mean from a parent-anchored seed.** The parent-anchor
  arm (nation-vs-GBR calls: mean nation−GBR +0.003, i.e. no format bias) confirms
  nations are being priced *against the parent*, not against each other — Scotland
  vs GBR drew only 0.37 once, dragging its stock below its head-to-head level.

Two corollaries: (a) the +0.008 **understates the head-to-head signal** (+0.04)
by construction, and any future run with more Scotland updates would be expected
to drift the final toward the draw mean (~0.47), i.e. gap ~+0.01–0.02 on draw
means alone; (b) the two rejected head-to-head calls (glm +0.03, mimo +0.04) were
discarded on schema grounds despite containing valid ratings — **protocol waste
that disproportionately hit the Scotland–England pairing** (2 of 3 re-rate direct
pairings rejected; overall rejection 10/30). The pilot had the same pattern
(Scotland lost 2 of ~10 calls to rejection). Head-to-head evidence is therefore
thinner than the pairing design intended, but what survives is unanimous.

**Answer:** the +0.008 is a real signal *filtered through deliberate compression*.
It is not evidence that raters see the nations as identical; they consistently
don't (+0.04). Nor is the pilot's +0.068 evidence of true divergence; it was
asymmetry-inflated. The truth on current dossiers is in between.

### 5.4 Rubric-scope flag for the supervisor (decision required; no action taken)

The challenge's strongest point — Scotland's lower conservatism — is currently
**unmeasurable by design**. If "visitor safety" is meant to include street-level
hostility (probability a stranger, venue worker, or officer responds hostilely),
then Scotland-specific trans-attitude surveys and per-capita trans-incident rates
are needed (candidates: Social Attitudes Survey Scotland boost, Eurobarometer
Scotland cut, Police Scotland/COPFS per-capita derivation from the 104 charges
with a population denominator — the dossiers already note the raw count exists
but the rate was never computed). That would be a **rubric expansion affecting
every country**, not a Scotland patch, and should be decided once, globally.
This investigation recommends *against* a Scotland-only climate patch (it would
bias the cluster exactly as the pre-repair asymmetry did).
