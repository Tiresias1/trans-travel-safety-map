## Calibration note

The severe levels move from recurring coercive targeting and imprisonment at 0.15 toward near-certain imprisonment and likely execution at 0.05. The high-risk levels place sustained police or organised coercion at 0.25 below unremedied institutional exclusion or substantial targeting at 0.35; 0.45 and 0.55 represent elevated risk, with universal unremedied exposure or widespread discrimination especially salient at the lower level. Reduced risk at 0.65 and 0.75 reflects increasingly effective protection and acceptance; low risk at 0.85 and 0.95 adds stronger, more consistently documented acceptance and protection without demanding perfection.

The hardest distinction is 0.35 versus 0.45: the same tier-one rule can differ in scope, severity, coexisting targeting and effective remedy, and the rubric does not prescribe exact decimal cutoffs. Likewise 0.55 versus 0.65 depends on the demonstrated frequency of visitor-facing hostility and how reliably remedies actually change outcomes; these targets are illustrative judgments, not mechanically unique scores.
