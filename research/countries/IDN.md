## Visitor-focused summary
Indonesia is high-risk for trans visitors — with a directly documented trans-traveler death
in custody and a presidential decree classifying LGBTQ+ as a national security threat.
Rodrigo Ventocilla, a Harvard trans activist from Peru, died in Bali police custody in 2022
after arrest at Bali airport on his honeymoon (family alleges police beating; police deny
violence). In Aceh province, Sharia law criminalises same-sex relations with public caning
(applies to all persons in Aceh, including tourists). A new penal code effective January 2026
effectively criminalises all same-sex conduct nationally. President Prabowo in October 2025
classified "promotion of LGBTQ+ culture" as a national security threat alongside terrorism —
and vigilante mob attacks on trans women escalated after this decree (10 attacks in Bogor).
Police conduct discriminatory raids on gay gatherings (75 arrested in Bogor, June 2025).
BUT: Bali is a major tourist hub where many trans visitors enter discreetly without incident;
Indonesia has no death penalty; and Rodrigo's death is disputed (police deny violence) rather
than clearly documented state torture.
