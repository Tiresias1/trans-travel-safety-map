## Visitor-focused summary
India is elevated-risk for trans visitors, with a complex mix of constitutional protection
and real practical gaps. On paper, the Supreme Court's 2014 NALSA judgment affirmed a
constitutional right to self-determined gender identity and the 2019 Act provides third-
gender recognition. But a 2026 amendment bill (passed by parliament) has been called a
"huge setback" by Human Rights Watch — it removes the self-identification right and narrows
the definition. Police abuse is documented (a transwoman was groped by a railway-protection
constable to "check gender"; TheProbe documents police atrocities), airport security has
discriminated against a trans student (Chennai CISF), passport authorities have demanded
unwarranted medical exams, and a new penal code removed safeguards protecting trans people
from sexual violence. Enforcement is uneven: only 11 of 36 states have transgender
protection cells. India has no trans-murder toll comparable to Brazil and courts are
protective, but the police-abuse + amendment-setback + safeguard-removal + enforcement-gaps
place it in the Elevated-Risk band. **Huge Tier-A ADM1 variation expected** (liberal metros
like Bangalore/Delhi/Kerala vs conservative rural north/central states).
