## Visitor-focused summary
Argentina has one of the world's most progressive legal frameworks — the 2012 Gender Identity
Law allows administrative self-identification (no medical requirements), offers an X marker
for non-binary, funds transition healthcare, and same-sex marriage has been legal since 2010
(first in Latin America). Buenos Aires is "one of the safest major cities in LatAm for LGBTQ+
visitors." For a tourist, the legal framework and tourist-area safety are strong. BUT the
situation is deteriorating sharply under the Milei government (from Dec 2023): the president
has called "gender ideology" "child abuse," issued decrees modifying the Gender Identity Law
(banning minors' hormone therapy/surgery; a prison-housing decree), and hate crimes hit a
record 227 in 2025 (up 62%, one every 38 hours — trans women bearing the brunt). Police
institutional violence against trans/travesti women is documented (arbitrary raids, drug-law
criminalization, searches only on travestis). The violence targets local vulnerable
communities rather than tourists, but the trajectory is concerning.

- Below ITA (0.76): Argentina's 227 hate crimes (record, +62%, one every 38 hours) + Milei

  women + prison-housing decree + "chaotic, desperate" community situation exceed Italy's

- Above BRA (0.55): Argentina's legal framework is genuinely stronger (self-ID 2012 +
  marriage equality 2010 + funded healthcare + anti-discrimination) and its violence is lower
  in absolute terms (227 hate crimes vs Brazil's ~71 trans murders/yr + general crime).
  Brazil's trans-murder toll is the world's highest; Argentina's is high but not at that
  level.
- The world-leading self-ID law keeps Argentina in Reduced Risk, but the record violence +
  Milei deterioration + police institutional violence pull it to the bottom of the band.
