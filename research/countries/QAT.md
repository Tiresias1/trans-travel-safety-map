# Qatar (QAT) — Research Note (revised with trans-specific evidence, 2026-08-21)
Score: **0.14** · Band: Do Not Travel

## Axis findings (trans-specific, traveler-focused)

### A. Border/transit
- No directly-documented trans-visitor border-arrest case (the Guerrero Aviña case is a
  GAY man arrested via Grindr entrapment — NOT trans-specific; removed from this note).
- BUT: HRW documents that Preventive Security Department arrested people "in public places
  based solely on their gender expression" — a visibly trans visitor is at risk of street
  arrest by the same forces. [hrw.org]

### F. Police interaction (SEVERE — documented, trans-specific)
- **HRW (Oct 2022): interviewed 4 Qatari transgender women, all arrested by the Preventive
  Security Department based solely on gender expression:**
  - One arrested on the street in Doha, accused of "imitating women" because of her gender
    expression; beaten in the police car until lips and nose bled, kicked in stomach.
    Detained 3 weeks without charge; repeatedly sexually harassed; required to attend
    conversion-therapy sessions with a psychologist who "would make me a man again."
  - One arrested in public for **wearing makeup**: officers gave her hand wipes to remove
    it, used the stained wipes as evidence, took her photo, **shaved her hair**, made her
    sign a pledge not to wear makeup again.
  - One detained twice — 2 months in solitary confinement underground + 6 weeks; beaten
    daily, hair shaved, forced to remove shirt for photos of her breasts; ongoing
    nightmares and fear of public spaces.
  - All forced to unlock phones; officers screenshotted private photos/chats to identify
    other LGBT people.
  - All forced to sign pledges to "cease immoral activity."
- Police used Law No 17 of 2002 on Protection of Community ("violating public morality") —
  allows up to 6 months' detention without charge or trial. [hrw.org]

### G. Law
- No specific cross-dressing statute in the Penal Code, but Wikipedia/HDT: trans people
  "can be arrested for the crime of 'impersonating the opposite gender'" — via morality
  and community-protection laws (not a dedicated statute). Same-sex conduct separately
  criminalised (Penal Code art 296, 1-3 years; art 285 up to 7 years for extramarital
  sex including same-sex; death penalty possible for Muslims in sharia courts, never
  applied). These are climate/situational factors for trans visitors, not direct
  gender-expression criminalisation (unlike Oman's explicit statute or Malaysia's Sharia
  "posing" laws). [wikipedia.org, humandignitytrust.org (from summary)]

### H. Detention
- Underground prison in Al Dafneh, Doha. Beatings, sexual harassment, solitary
  confinement, forced conversion therapy as release condition. Physical detransition
  attempted ("surgical removal of breast tissue" per BBC report cited in Wikipedia).
  [hrw.org, wikipedia.org]

### K. Climate
- Preventive Security Department (Interior Ministry) actively surveils and targets LGBT
  people based on gender expression. Government "categorically rejected" the HRW reports.
  Qatar repeatedly voted against the UN SOGI Independent Expert.
  [hrw.org, wikipedia.org]

- Qatar's evidence is strong but RESIDENT-focused (Qatari trans women arrested in Doha)

  removed from this note as non-trans-specific.
