# Oman (OMN) — Research Note (traveler-outing axes, 2026-08-21)
Score: **0.10** · Band: Do Not Travel

## Axis findings (taxonomy A–K)
- **A Border/transit:** No directly-documented trans-visitor border case. BUT: the 2018
  Penal Code's provisions "extend to foreign nationals and digital activity" — a trans
  visitor's online presence could be grounds for prosecution. [legalclarity.org]
- **B Documents/bureaucracy:** No LGR; no legal recognition of trans identity. No mechanism
  to change gender markers on official documents. [legalclarity.org]
- **C Gendered spaces:** Trans gender expression is explicitly criminalised (2018 Penal
  Code) — any public gender-variant presentation is legal exposure.
- **G Law (explicit criminalisation of gender expression):** Penal Code (Royal Decree
  No. 7/2018): **explicitly and implicitly criminalises transgender expression and identity**
  — criminalising both the gender expression of trans people AND same-sex conduct (up to
  **3 years' imprisonment**). Human Dignity Trust: "Criminalises the gender expression of
  trans people." A 2019 Royal Decree (No. 75) further deepened the discriminatory system.
  No anti-discrimination law. [humandignitytrust.org, cairo52.com, legalclarity.org]
- **D/E Violence:** "Extent of discrimination and violence against LGBT people is not known"
  (HDT — Oman restricts documentation). Cairo 52 UPR report: "systemic legal and institutional
  discrimination." No documented trans-specific killing.
- **F Police:** No documented trans-specific police-brutality case. Cairo 52 UPR: "recent
  legislative developments have deepened criminalisation of gender expression."
- **H Detention/prison:** No documented trans-prisoner case.
- **I Health/medication:** No documented gender-affirming-care-specific issues, but the
  criminalisation of gender expression + same-sex conduct makes HRT carry-in hazardous.
- **K State & media climate (systematically anti-trans legislation):** Cairo 52 + White Tent
  UPR (2025): "systemic legal and institutional discrimination faced by transgender
  individuals in Oman... recent legislative developments have deepened criminalisation of
  gender expression and denied transgender people access to basic rights." Oman rejected
  recommendations on SO/GI in UPR.

## Score rationale
0.10 — Do Not Travel, below QAT (0.14)/KWT (0.15)/ARE (0.13), above SAU (0.05)/IRN (0.09)
marginally, below BHR (0.12).
- Below QAT (0.14): Oman has explicit criminalisation of gender expression (3 years) while
  Qatar criminalises same-sex conduct (7 years) but not gender expression per se. Oman's
  Penal Code specifically targets trans identity/expression; Qatar's targets conduct. For
  a trans visitor, Oman's law is MORE directly applicable (gender expression criminalised
  = any visible trans presentation is illegal). BUT Oman's penalty is lower (3 years vs
  Qatar's 7 years) and Oman has no documented trans-visitor conviction (Qatar has Guerrero
  Aviña).
- Below KWT (0.15): Kuwait's Article 198 ("imitating the opposite sex") was ruled
  unconstitutional (2022) — a positive trajectory Oman lacks (Oman's 2018 Penal Code
  DEEPENED criminalisation). Kuwait has same-sex 7 years + healthcare-illegal; Oman has
  gender-expression 3 years + same-sex 3 years. Different profiles.
- Below ARE (0.13): UAE has the most documented trans-visitor border targeting (X-ray,
  deportations, 1yr prison for cross-dressing) but NO explicit gender-expression criminalisation
  statute (uses general morality provisions). Oman has EXPLICIT gender-expression criminalisation
  but no documented visitor-targeting. Different profiles.
- ~tied with IRN (0.09): Iran has death penalty + state-custody rape + forced transitions
  + Tehran segregation; Oman has 3-year gender-expression criminalisation + deepened
  discrimination. Iran is more severe (death penalty + documented state violence) but Oman's
  explicit trans-expression criminalisation is a direct traveler-facing law Iran doesn't
  have (Iran criminalises conduct, not identity/expression per se). Close.
- Above SAU (0.05): Oman's penalty is 3 years (Saudi has capital framework); no documented
  custodial death (Saudi has Mohammad); no morality police raids (Saudi has CPVPV). BUT
  Oman's explicit gender-expression criminalisation is a specificity Saudi's morality
  provisions lack in codification.
