# Uruguay (URY) — Research Note (traveler-outing axes, 2026-08-21)
Score: **0.77** · Band: Reduced Risk

## Axis findings (taxonomy A–K)
- **A Border/transit:** No trans-specific border friction; foreign documents recognised. "One of
  the safest countries in Latin America" (Global Peace Index; Guru'Guay).
- **B Documents/bureaucracy (self-ID + comprehensive trans law 2018):** LGR by administrative
  procedure (since 2009; 2018 Law No. 19,684 simplified — no surgery/medical diagnosis required,
  essentially self-ID). **Comprehensive Law for Trans People (Law 19,684, Oct 2018)** — right
  to gender identity + LGR + healthcare provisions + **reparation pension** for trans people
  born before 1975 who suffered institutional violence + **state employment quota** (Art. 12 —
  26 trans people entered state employment in 2025, 0.13% of total). Same-sex marriage (2013,
  Latin America's first); adoption (2009); military open (2011). [gub.uy, outrightinternational.org,
  law.cornell.edu, equaldex.com]
- **C Gendered spaces (protected):** Bathroom access PROTECTED (Equaldex: "✔ Protected"). No
  restriction law.
- **D/E Violence (lower than LatAm peers; documented):** Joana, 29-year-old trans woman,
  **shot dead in her home** in Montevideo (Jul 2025) — assailant sentenced to 7 years
  (TDoR/enforcement). Andrea Gianneo: "brutal golpiza" (brutal beating) by a bus conductor
  (ladiaria 2024). Hospital discrimination (San Carlos — trans person reported mistreatment).
  Bank discrimination (BROU — trans woman's case ongoing, "ocultamiento de evidencia"). No
  documented mob violence, dating-app-ambush, or tourist-specific killing. [tdor2.translivesmatter.info,
  ladiaria.com.uy, montevideo.com.uy]
- **F Police:** No documented police-brutality-against-trans case. Freedom House: "positive
  record of upholding political rights and civil liberties."
- **G Law (very strong):** Comprehensive Trans Law 19,684 (2018); LGR self-ID (administrative,
  no surgery/diagnosis); marriage equality (2013, first LatAm); adoption (2009); hate-crime
  law covers SO/GI; anti-discrimination law covers GI; military open (2011); reparation
  pension; state employment quota. "Champions LGBTIQ equality at international level" (Outright).
  [outrightinternational.org, equaldex.com, law.cornell.edu]
- **H Detention/prison:** No documented trans-prisoner case. Thin evidence.
- **I Health/medication:** Hospital discrimination documented (San Carlos); BUT Law 19,684
  includes healthcare provisions + reparation pension for trans victims of institutional
  violence. No documented HRT customs issues.
- **K State & media climate (progressive, stable, safest in LatAm):** "One of the safest
  countries in Latin America" (Global Peace Index). "Strong democratic governance structure and
  positive record of upholding political rights and civil liberties" (Freedom House).
  "Progressive government committed to providing legal safeguards and fundamental rights" (IHRP
  Toronto). BUT: "disparities in treatment and political representation of women, transgender
  people, Uruguayans of African descent" (Freedom House). Hospital/bank discrimination
  incidents documented.

## Score rationale
0.77 — Reduced Risk, above CHL (0.71)/ARG (0.73), ~tied with THA (0.77)/EST (0.77)/SVN (0.78).
- Above ARG (0.73): Uruguay has a comprehensive trans law (2018, older than Argentina's
  Milei-threatened 2012 law) + reparation pension + state employment quota + "safest in LatAm"
  + stable progressive government (no Milei) + much lower violence (1 documented killing in
  2025 vs Argentina's 227 hate crimes) + no "anti-rights state" rollback. Argentina's Milei
  hostility + record violence + police institutional violence + prison-housing decree are more
  severe than Uruguay's profile.
- Above CHL (0.71): Uruguay's comprehensive trans law (2018, older + more comprehensive than
  Chile's self-ID 2019) + marriage equality (2013, older than Chile's 2022) + reparation
  pension + state employment quota + "safest in LatAm." Chile's "year of regression" +
  Ever murder/dismemberment + hotel attack + "consensus breakdown" are more severe. Chile's
  one advantage is the positive Gendarmería axis-H protocol (which Uruguay doesn't have
  documented).
- ~tied with THA (0.77)/EST (0.77): Uruguay's comprehensive trans law + self-ID + marriage
  equality + reparation pension + "safest in LatAm" are comparable to Thailand's deep social
  acceptance + Welcome-Pride immigration + care hub, and Estonia's marriage equality + action
  plan + no severe incidents. Different profiles (Uruguay: law + low violence; Thailand: social
  acceptance + visitor infra; Estonia: law + EU + no incidents), similar risk level.
- Not in Low Risk (0.80+) because: Joana killed (Jul 2025 — a trans-specific killing with
  enforcement, but a killing nonetheless) + hospital/bank discrimination + "disparities in
  treatment" + no documented axis-H positive + thin evidence on some axes.

## Correction (2026-08-21, regrounding check)
Score corrected: **0.77 → 0.81** (Low Risk band). The original 0.77 placed Uruguay only 0.06
above the UK (0.71) despite Uruguay being systematically better on 5 axes (C: protected bathrooms
vs in-force daily bathroom code; F: no strip-search vs biological-sex strip-search policies; H:
no documented prison issue vs men's-prison placement post-SC + suicide-risk; K: stable progressive
vs deteriorating courts-led; B: self-ID vs court-based GRC). The original 0.06 gap was justified
by offsetting Uruguay's Joana killing against Uruguay's five-axis advantage — but the UK also
has documented trans killings (Brianna Ghey, 2023, and others), so the "UK has no documented
trans killing" claim was wrong. With both having documented killings, Uruguay's five-axis
systematic advantage should produce a wider gap. Uruguay at 0.81 (Low Risk, above Taiwan 0.80,
below Japan 0.82) better reflects its comprehensive trans law + self-ID + protected bathrooms +
reparation pension + state employment quota + "safest in LatAm" + stable progressive government,
offset by Joana's killing + hospital/bank discrimination + "disparities in treatment."

The UK (0.71) stays unchanged — its live rollback (bathroom code + strip-search + men's-prison
+ deteriorating climate) is correctly placed at the bottom of Reduced Risk, above Hungary (0.68)
and Serbia (0.64) which have more extreme profiles, below Poland (0.72) marginally.
