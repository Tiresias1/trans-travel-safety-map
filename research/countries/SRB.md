## Visitor-focused summary
Serbia is reduced-risk (lower end) for trans visitors despite no criminalisation. The most acute
documented traveler-axis risk is police: in February 2024 the Interior Ministry began
investigating officers who beat, tortured and sexually harassed two LGBT people during a
Belgrade apartment raid. About 105 SOGI-motivated incidents were recorded in 2024, a
masked attacker smashed the Pride Info Centre in 2025, Pride marches require heavy police
protection, and HRT (testosterone) supply-chain disruptions have been documented. Serbia
is outside Schengen, legal-gender-recognition reform has stalled (the Law on Gender
Identity was not adopted), and the anti-discrimination law is weakly enforced. One
mitigating factor: a Belgrade prison has a trans-sensitive search policy allowing trans
women to choose the search arrangement, though watchdogs flag training gaps.

## Score rationale
0.64 — Reduced Risk, below HUN (0.68), above BRA (0.55).
- Below HUN (0.68): Serbia's documented police brutality against LGBT people (axis F,
  torture/sexual-harassment alleged, Feb 2024) is more severe than Hungary's axis-F
  (CPT ill-treatment allegations); Serbia is NOT Schengen (axis-A border friction Hungary
  lacks); HRT supply disruptions (axis I); 105 incidents/yr; LGR reform STAGNATING (Hungary
  improving fast under Tisza + CJEU mandate). Serbia's trajectory is stagnating while
  Hungary's is sharply improving.
- Above BRA (0.55): no criminalisation; anti-discrimination law on the books; a
  trans-sensitive prison search policy (axis H mitigating); Pride held (under protection);
  a lesbias PM (symbolic); no trans-murder toll comparable to Brazil's. But the stagnating
  reform + police brutality + not-Schengen + HRT disruptions keep it in the High-Risk band.
- The lesbias PM does not pull the score up materially (stalled legislation, police
  brutality — symbolic rather than substantive protection).
