# Philippines (PHL) — Research Note (traveler-outing axes, 2026-08-21)
Score: **0.69** · Band: Reduced Risk

## Axis findings (taxonomy A–K)
- **A Border/transit:** No trans-specific border friction; foreign documents recognised.
  "Strong social acceptance and cultural visibility" at the border/airport.
- **B Documents/bureaucracy (NO LGR at all; no anti-discrimination law):** **No national LGR
  law** — trans people cannot legally change their sex/gender marker on birth certificates
  or official documents (a Simplified LGR Bill is proposed but not passed). **SOGIE Equality
  Bill stalled 25 years** in the legislature — no comprehensive national anti-discrimination
  law covering gender identity. BUT: Safe Spaces Act (2019) + Philippine HIV/AIDS Policy Act
  exist; local anti-discrimination ordinances in some cities (Taguig etc.). [bulatlat.com,
  outrightinternational.org, rollingstonephilippines.com]
- **C Gendered spaces (documented discrimination, no legal protection or restriction):** No
  bathroom restriction law. BUT documented venue-discrimination: trans woman denied entry at
  Isla Reta Beach Resort (Feb 2025); barred from women's fitting room at Taguig boutique;
  hostel owner upset at trans woman using women's restroom (May 2024); trans woman initially
  barred from Basilica del Santo Niño (Jul 2024); trans woman compelled to get haircut for
  school enrollment (CHR statement). CHR investigates but lacks enforcement power without a
  national law. [sunstar.com.ph, philstarlife.com, philnews.ph, gmanetwork.com, chr.gov.ph]
- **D/E Violence & predation (documented, significant):** Trans woman Kierra Apostol, 18,
  **found dead in a Cagayan river** (Jun 2025 — Bahaghari: "another harrowing case of violence
  against Filipino trans women"). Drag artist Taylor Sheesh physically assaulted mid-
  performance (Apr 2024). **Chinese national arrested for raping a transwoman** at a resort in
  Batangas (May 2025 — Manila Bulletin). Taiwanese national arrested for assaulting a trans
  woman in Cebu motel (Apr 2024). Violence targets local trans women but perpetrators include
  foreign nationals — relevant for visitor-context. "Philippines consistently ranks as among
  the most accepting countries in Asia" (Outright). [bulatlat.com, interaksyon.philstar.com,
  mb.com.ph, sunstar.com.ph, outrightinternational.org]
- **F Police:** CHR actively investigating discrimination cases (institutional positive). BUT:
  PNP has no specific trans policy (only 2013 human-rights-based policing guidebook);
  "red-tagging" of LGBTQIA advocates (surveillance/harassment/prosecution under anti-terror
  law). No documented police-brutality-against-trans case (unlike Serbia/India/Pakistan).
  [gmanetwork.com, trust.org guidebook]
- **G Law:** No criminalisation of trans identity or same-sex (never criminalised). BUT: no
  national anti-discrimination law; no LGR; SOGIE Equality Bill stalled 25 years. Safe Spaces
  Act (2019) + local ordinances. "Misgendering can be understood within legal context"
  (lawyers say it can be part of a crime in specific contexts — Rappler). [rollingstone-
  philippines.com, rappler.com]
- **H Detention/prison:** Thin evidence; no documented trans-prisoner case.
- **I Health/medication:** Healthcare access generally available; no documented HRT customs
  issues.
- **K State & media climate (accepting but legally unprotected):** "Among the most accepting
  countries in Asia" (Outright); deep bakla/tomboy traditions; Manila Pride "one of the most
  vibrant in Southeast Asia"; high-profile queer representation. BUT: "strong social acceptance
  and cultural visibility coexist with a near-complete absence of legal protections at the
  national level" (WanderingWithPride). SOGIE bill stalled 25 years; documented discrimination/
  violence; red-tagging of advocates.

## Visitor-focused summary
The Philippines is reduced-risk — one of Asia's most socially accepting destinations, with
deep cultural traditions of gender diversity (bakla, tomboy), a vibrant Manila Pride, and no
criminalisation of trans identity. BUT there is a "near-complete absence of legal protections
at the national level": no national LGR law at all (trans people cannot change their gender
marker), the SOGIE Equality Bill has been stalled for 25 years (no national anti-discrimination
law), and documented violence includes the killing of an 18-year-old trans woman found in a
river (June 2025), a Chinese national arrested for raping a transwoman at a resort (May 2025),
and a drag artist assaulted mid-performance. The Commission on Human Rights actively
investigates discrimination cases (venue denials, bathroom friction, school hair-cut coercion)
but lacks enforcement power without a national law. For a visitor: no border friction, social
acceptance is genuine, but the legal-protection vacuum means no recourse if discriminated
against.

  Different profiles, similar risk level.
- Above ZAF (0.66): Philippines has no criminalisation + deeper social acceptance + no border
  friction + lower documented violence (1 killing vs SA's Grindr-hostage + healthcare-system
  violence + law-vs-practice gap). But Philippines' absence of ANY legal protection (no LGR,
  no anti-discrimination law) is a gap SA doesn't share.
