# Brazil (BRA) — Research Note
Researched: 2026-08-21 · Score: **0.55** · Anchor: MID-SCALE ("law vs practice gap")

## Key findings (with sources)
- Legal framework among the strongest in Latin America: STF 2018 — name and gender
  marker changes without surgery or judicial process; STF 2019 — homophobia/transphobia
  criminalised as racism-equivalent (Law 7.716/1989); same-sex marriage since 2013.
  Marriage-equality repeal efforts exist in Congress but have not succeeded.
  [zsassociados.com], [wikipedia LGBTQ rights in Brazil], [outrightinternational.org]
- Enforcement exists: São Paulo nightclub ordered to pay damages for barring a trans
  woman (TJ-SP 2025); Maria da Penha domestic-violence law extended to trans women
  (STJ 2024); São Paulo municipal anti-discrimination law (2020) with administrative
  sanctions. [conjur.com.br], [latinoamerica21.com], [loc.gov (403; from summary)]
- State institutions take trans violence seriously: São Paulo Chamber CPI (parliamentary
  inquiry) into violence against trans people concluded 2022 with 180+ recommendations;
  public defender guidance affirms trans women's right to women's bathrooms and social
  names. [saopaulo.sp.leg.br], [defensoria.sp.def.br (PDF)]
- Violence data: 57% of physical violence against LGBTQIA+ people in São Paulo city
  occurs at night; dating-app stickups targeting gay men (some fatal) reported by
  Reuters 2024; trans political candidates face death threats incl. an assassination
  attempt (AP 2024). Brazil routinely records the world's highest absolute numbers of
  trans murders (Antra) — though concentrated among local vulnerable demographics,
  not visitors. [apublica.org], [reuters.com (401; from summary)], [apnews.com]
- Travel advisories: Canada — "exercise a high degree of caution" for Brazil due to
  crime (general, affects everyone). Intrepid: "generally a safe travel destination"
  for LGBTQIA+ travellers. [travel.gc.ca], [intrepidtravel.com]
- Visibility: world's largest Pride parade (São Paulo); 3,000+ openly LGBTQ+ candidates
  in 2024 elections; prominent trans public figures. [refugees.org], [apnews.com]

## Visitor-focused summary
Strong law, weak practice. Everyday legality is unambiguous: self-ID documents, court-
affirmed bathroom access, criminalised transphobia, municipal protections and active
remedies. Socially, big-city queer life is vibrant and visible. The offsetting factor
is violence: night-time LGBTQIA+ violent incidents in São Paulo, dating-app robbery
schemes targeting queer men, credible death threats against visible trans figures, and
the world's highest raw trans-murder toll (concentrated in poor/peripheral communities).
A visitor sticking to tourist zones faces mostly harassment-level risk, with elevated
caution warranted at night and on dating apps; general (non-trans) street crime is the
bigger day-to-day hazard.
