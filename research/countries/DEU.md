# Germany (DEU) — Research Note
Researched: 2026-08-21 · Score: **0.85** · Anchor: TOP-OF-SCALE (with rising far-right friction)

## Key findings (with sources)
- SBGG (Self-Determination Act) in force 1 Nov 2024: gender entry + name change by
  simple registry declaration (3-month wait), "diverse"/blank options; replaced 1980
  Transsexuals Act (court + expert assessments scrapped). Amends Passport Act etc.
  [gesetze-im-internet.de/englisch_sbgg, dw.com, loc.gov (403; from summary),
  bundesverband-trans.de, law.cornell.edu]
- Political backlash: post-2025 federal election, CDU/CSU seeks abolition of SBGG;
  governing coalition reviewing the law "until July 2026"; AfD/BSW hostile; heated
  Bundestag campaigns (Liebich case instrumentalised; NRZ interviews document community
  fear). Women's-shelter access for trans women contested by Union parties (Bayerische
  Staatszeitung). [nrz.de, nd-aktuell.de, dw.com (x2), bayerische-staatszeitung.de]
- Street-level hostility rising: Amadeu Antonio Foundation monitoring — record number
  of right-wing extremist attacks/disruptions of Christopher Street Day events: of 245
  CSDs in 2025, at least 110 disrupted or attacked, 53 linked to organised far-right
  networks (ILGA-Europe Annual Review 2026). [ilga-europe.org PDF (fetch failed; from
  summary)]
- Facilities: no legal restriction on bathroom use; venue-level debates only — German
  Sauna-Bund guideline says entry to women's sauna depends on "primary sex
  characteristics" (controversial, non-binding); Berlin saunas mostly relaxed practice.
  [rtl.de, freiepresse.de, tagesspiegel.de]
- Germany issued its own travel warning for trans/non-binary citizens visiting the US
  (2025) — shows official institutional awareness; no inbound trans-travel warnings
  exist. [advocate.com]
- General framework: anti-discrimination law (AGG) covers gender identity in goods/
  services (via gender/sexual-identity case law); hate crime law covers gender
  identity motive; same-sex marriage legal.

## Visitor-focused summary
Low risk. No criminalisation, functioning self-ID framework, hate-crime protections,
and an established queer infrastructure in cities (Berlin, Cologne, Hamburg).
Residuals: a politically contested climate with a serious abolition attempt against the
self-ID law, record far-right disruptions of Pride events, and patchy venue-level
facility policies (e.g. some sauna operators). Eastern federal states show
concentrated far-right street activity.
