# Turkey (TUR) — Research Note (traveler-outing axes, 2026-08-21)
Score: **0.47** · Band: Elevated Risk

## Axis findings (taxonomy A–K)
- **A Border/transit/documents:** Foreign visitors must carry original passport at all
  times; transit checkpoints + routine ID checks in holiday destinations — fines up to 3,000
  Lira or detention for non-compliance. This creates daily **axis-A/B outing risk** for a
  foreign trans visitor whose passport gender doesn't match presentation. No Schengen.
  [nomadlawyer.org]
- **B Documents/bureaucracy:** LGR court-based, medical-gatekept; Health Ministry 2025
  regulation may **profile trans people and restrict transition access** (gender
  reassignment added to official reasons for issuing medical reports → profiling risk).
  New barriers to gender-affirming healthcare. [kaosgl1.org]
- **C Gendered spaces:** No bathroom law, but "Year of the Family" campaign + Diyanet
  religious-affairs rhetoric creating a hostile climate for gender non-conformity.
- **D/E Violence & predation (documented, rising):** Transphobic knife attack on trans
  woman Yaşam in Bakırköy (Aug 2025 — she was then DETAINED, victim-criminalised). Ankara:
  trans woman D.D. life-threateningly attacked, "intentional killing" charge. Kaos GL:
  **"sharp rise in LGBTQ rights violations in 2025"** — increased detentions, bans on
  public events, healthcare barriers. Pride 2025: human rights violations documented
  (Hafıza Merkezi: "You should have behaved yourself"). [kaosgl1.org, kaosgl.org,
  turkishminute.com, hakikatadalethafiza.org]
- **F Police (systemic, severe):** Istanbul July 2025 — **20 trans women detained for ID
  checks in Beyoğlu** (police profiling; "the new law hasn't even passed yet but they're
  already enforcing it on us"). Trans women historically "frequent targets of torture and
  ill-treatment by police forces." Police bonus system (2009) incentivised targeting trans
  sex workers through ticketing. ECtHR: trans woman arrested for "disrupting traffic."
  Victim-criminalisation (Yaşam detained after being attacked). Systemic axis-F.
  [bianet.org, echrcaselaw.com, link.springer.com]
- **G Law (draft criminalization — most alarming):** **HRW (Oct 2025): draft 11th Judicial
  Package would bring CRIMINAL CHARGES against LGBT people** — "one of the most alarming
  rollbacks of rights in decades." Not yet passed, but under the presidency's "Year of the
  Family" campaign. "Agents of influence" law (2024) criminalises civil society documenting
  human rights violations. [hrw.org]
- **H Detention/prison (documented violations):** Kaos GL / academic: violations of LGBTI+
  prisoners' rights; trans women placed in men's facilities; abuse documented; "legal
  regulations needed to eliminate violations." Asylum detention: trans people at particular
  risk in removal centres. [kaosgl.org, mojust.org, unipd-centrodirittiumani.it]
- **I Health/medication (restricted):** Health Ministry 2025 regulation profiles trans
  people + restricts transition access; new barriers to gender-affirming healthcare. HRT
  carry-in: possible friction at customs (passport-carry regulation + profiling climate).
  [kaosgl1.org]
- **K State & media climate (institutionalized, presidential-level):** "LGBTI+ hostility
  became centered on the presidency" (Kaos GL 2025); "Year of the Family" campaign; Diyanet;
  Ministry of Family implementing anti-LGBT policy; "hostility turned into state policy."
  [kaosgl1.org]

## Visitor-focused summary
Turkey presents elevated risk for trans visitors — institutionalised, presidential-level
hostility that has become state policy. Police profile and detain trans women for identity
checks (20 detained in Istanbul in July 2025), trans women have historically been "frequent
targets of torture and ill-treatment," and trans women attacked by civilians have been
victimized again by detention. A draft law leaked in October 2025 would criminalise LGBT
people — not yet passed but under active government consideration. Transphobic attacks and
Pride-event violence are documented, the healthcare ministry is introducing regulations
that may profile and restrict trans healthcare, and foreign visitors must carry original
passports at all times (creating outing risk at the frequent ID checks). If detained, trans
women are placed in men's facilities with documented rights violations. The risk is
concentrated in urban trans-visible contexts (Istanbul's Beyoğlu/Tarlabaşı) rather than
package-holiday tourist zones, but the institutional hostility pervades.

## Score rationale
0.47 — Elevated Risk, below SRB (0.64)/BRA (0.55)/USA (0.52)/MEX (0.50), well above RUS
(0.12).
- Below BRA/USA/MEX: Turkey's hostility is STATE-LED and INSTITUTIONALISED (police sweeps
  of trans women, presidential "Year of the Family" campaign, draft criminalization law,
  health-profiling regulation) — more pervasive and state-mediated than Brazil's
  violence-against-locals, the US's document/entry friction, or Mexico's crime. Turkey
  has a daily passport-carry-ID-check outing risk (axis A/B) that none of those have.
- Above RUS (0.12): no criminalisation yet (the draft law isn't passed); no "extremist"
  designation; no propaganda-justifying-transition criminal offense; trans people exist
  openly in Istanbul/Izmir; Turkey is not at Russia's level of legal criminalization. BUT
  the trajectory is sharply negative (draft law + institutionalised hostility).
- The score reflects the probability×severity frame: high-probability of state-mediated
  outing (ID checks, police profiling) + high-severity-if-outed (detention, transphobic
  attacks, health profiling, draft criminalisation trajectory).
