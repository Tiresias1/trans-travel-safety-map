# United States (USA) — Research Note
Researched: 2026-08-21 · Score: **0.52** · Anchor: MID-SCALE (subnational split)

## Key findings (with sources)
- Federal passport policy (Jan 2025 EO "Defending Women From Gender Ideology Extremism"):
  no more X markers; passports must reflect sex assigned at birth; SCOTUS stayed the
  injunction (Orr v. Trump, Nov 2025), so policy is in force as of 2026. Foreign
  visitors' X-marker passports may trigger questioning (delay/forced disclosure, not
  verified refusals). [travel.state.gov (403; from summary), hrc.org, cntraveler.com,
  apnews.com, legalclarity.org]
- Border: CBP states gender identity alone does not make a traveller inadmissible; but
  high-discretion environment; documented extra questioning of queer travellers
  (Yasmin Benoit at customs); device searches rare (<0.01%) but social media vetting
  expanding (ESTA social-media proposal Dec 2025). [queercompass.co.uk 2026]
- Several countries (Denmark, UK, Finland, Germany + others; 7 by Mar 2025 per LGBTQ
  Nation) issued trans-specific travel advisories for the US; Iceland MFA developing
  guidelines; Erin Reed (Jul 2026): designates the US "Do Not Travel" for
  non-essential travel without understanding of legal environment, citing visa
  revocation, entry denial, detention risks. [erininthemorning.com, icelandreview.com]
- State patchwork (MAP, Apr 2026): 21 states + DC prohibit public-accommodations
  discrimination on gender identity; 21 states + 4 territories no protection.
  Bathroom bans: ~22 states restrict; some criminal (Idaho felony up to 5 yrs, partial
  injunction), Florida arrest-based, Kansas bounty/civil-suit system, Texas statewide
  ban with detainment reported; South Carolina expanded to universities (2026).
  [mapresearch.org, legalclarity.org, reuters.com, vox.com]
- Erin Reed risk map (Jul 2026): Do Not Travel: FL, ID, KS, TX. Worst: AL, AR, IA,
  IN, LA, MS, OH, OK, ND, SC, SD, TN, UT, WV, WY. High: GA, MO, NE, NH, MT.
  Moderate: AK, KY, NC. Low: AZ, DE, ME, MI, NV, PA, VA, WI, DC. Most protective:
  CA, CO, CT, HI, IL, MA, MD, MN, NJ, NM, NY, OR, RI, VT, WA. (Includes many
  youth/resident-focused laws; used with visitor-relevance filter.)
- Kansas (Mar 2026): invalidated ~1,700 trans driver's licenses + birth certificates;
  bathroom ban; HRW coverage. [hrw.org, apnews.com, cnn.com]
- TSA screening can "out" trans travellers; guidance exists (A4TE). Rare issues
  mostly. [transequality.org]
- No verified mainstream reporting of a tourist refused entry SOLELY for being
  LGBTQ+; risk is friction, delays, questioning. [queercompass.co.uk]

## Visitor-focused summary
The US has become a sharply split legal environment. Federal policy since Jan 2025
(two-sex policy, no X markers, court-sanctioned passport rules) creates entry friction
for trans visitors — especially X-marker passports — and several foreign governments
advise caution. About half the states offer full protections; the other half have no
public-accommodations protection, and a growing group criminalises bathroom use
(felony penalties in Idaho, arrest-based enforcement in Florida, bounty suits in
Kansas). Most-protective states (CA, NY, IL, CO, WA etc.) remain broadly safe.
Everyday violence risk for visitors is low; the primary risks are legal exposure in
restrictive states and document/border friction.
