## Axis findings (taxonomy A–K)
- **A Border/transit/documents:** Accepts foreign X markers; no trans-specific entry
  friction; no foreign advisories against the UK. Low axis-A (decisive contrast with the
  US, axis-1). [gov.uk identity guidance]
- **B Documents/bureaucracy:** GRC system still functions for birth-certificate purpose;
  UK issues M/F only (no X). No tourist document-outing cases documented.
- **C Gendered spaces (in-force, daily, civil):** EHRC statutory Code of Practice for
  Services (in force ~mid-2026) directs single-sex spaces (toilets, changing rooms,
  hospital wards) by biological sex — trans people directed to gender-neutral/birth-sex
  facilities. Affects visitors daily; civil (no criminal penalty). [bbc.com ×2,
  independent.co.uk, jurist.org]
- **D Public/social reaction:** Rising incidents, WorldPride-type tensions; social
  hostility up (TransActual Gendered Spaces Report 2025). [transactual.org.uk,
  nbcnews.com]
- **E Violence & predation:** No documented trans-tourist-rape or organised dating-app
  ambush pattern (contrast Italy). Physical-violence risk low.
- **F Police (degrading, nationwide, post-SC):** Post-April-2025 Supreme Court ruling,
  UK police forces adopted biological-sex search policies — British Transport Police
  strip-searches trans women with **male officers**; Police Scotland + Surrey/Sussex
  "Searching Biological Sex Policy." NPCC/BTP interim guidance challenged by Sex Matters
  (judicial review, 2026). Severe-when-triggered (requires arrest). [theguardian.com,
  scotland.police.uk, surrey.police.uk, judiciary.uk]
- **G Law:** No criminalisation of trans identity; no cross-dressing statute; no
  propaganda law. GRC remains. The rollback is facilities/detention-focused, not criminal.
- **H Detention/prison placement (severe, post-SC, documented harm):** *For Women
  Scotland v Scottish Ministers* [2026] CSOH 59 (Lady Ross, Outer House Court of Session):
  SPS guidance allowing trans women in the women's estate **declared unlawful** — sex
  segregation must be biological per the April 2025 SC ruling; trans inmates **moved out
  of women's prisons**; Scottish government did not appeal. HMPPS framework (E/W):
  presumption against trans women (even with GRC) in women's estate. BBC documents
  suicide-risk litigation over placement by birth sex. Comparable to France/Germany's
  men's-prison issue, now nationally directioned. [bbc.com ×2, ukconstitutionallaw.org,
  scottishlegal.com, theguardian.com]
- **I Health/medication:** Schengen-adjacent; HRT carry-in uneventful.
- **K State & media climate:** Deteriorating but COURTS/EHRC-led (not executive
  demonization — contrast US 2025 EO). UN experts (Aug 2026) warn of institutionalised
  discrimination. [jurist.org]

## Visitor-focused summary
The UK is a reduced-risk destination with a documented, in-force rollback concentrated on
facilities and detention. Bathroom and changing-room use is now directed by biological sex
under the EHRC code (civil, no criminal penalty — affects visitors daily). If arrested,
two severe-when-triggered risks apply post-April-2025: police forces now strip-search
trans women by biological sex (male officers); and trans women are placed in men's
prisons (Scottish guidance ruled unlawful in 2026, with documented suicide-risk
litigation). No criminalisation, no cross-dressing statute, and no trans-specific border
friction (a sharp contrast with the US, where foreign advisories and the federal two-sex
policy create entry friction). The climate is deteriorating but courts/EHRC-led rather
than executive demonization.

## Correction 2 (2026-08-21, LGR/GRC relevance to travelers)
Score corrected: **0.69 → 0.66**. The GRC (Gender Recognition Certificate) requires 2 years'
UK residence — a foreign visitor has no GRC and can't get one. The traveler's gender marker
comes from their home country's passport; the UK's only relevant fact is accepting foreign
documents (axis A). Marriage equality was already flagged as non-scoring (correction 1).
After removing these non-traveler positives, the UK's traveler-facing profile is:

Positives: no border friction + hate-crime-GI (theoretical recourse) + no criminalisation +
functional institutions.

Negatives: daily bathroom restriction (the UK is the ONLY country above 0.55 with a
nationally-legislated bathroom restriction — Peru 0.48 and USA 0.52 are the only others, and
theirs are criminal) + nationwide biological-sex strip-search policy (the UK is the ONLY
country in the dataset with this as a documented nationwide policy) + men's-prison placement
post-SC + Brianna Ghey + other documented killings + actively deteriorating climate.

UK at 0.66 is: tied with Lithuania (0.66: no restrictions + conservative climate) and South
Africa (0.66: strong law + law-vs-practice gap) — three different profiles, same risk level;
0.02 above Serbia (0.64); 0.01 below Romania (0.67); 0.02 below Hungary (0.68: banned-LGR
but no daily restrictions); 0.03 below Latvia (0.69: no active restrictions despite no legal
recourse — Latvia's absence-of-protections is less severe for a visitor than the UK's three
active restrictions).

The key methodological insight: LGR/self-ID is a CLIMATE SIGNAL (axis K — institutional
culture proxy), not a direct traveler protection. A traveler won't apply for LGR. This was
already the implicit treatment in most countries' rationales (where self-ID functioned as a
climate/institutional signal); the UK was the outlier where the GRC was incorrectly cited
as a direct positive. No other country's score changes from this correction.

## Note on LGR as climate signal (applies dataset-wide)
LGR/self-ID correlates with institutional respect for trans identity → lower police/social
hostility → lower axis-D/K risk. It is a PROXY, not a protection. A country can have self-ID
and bathroom restrictions simultaneously (UK: GRC + bathroom code). Conversely, a country
without LGR might have no daily restrictions (Latvia). Score weighting: LGR presence/absence
contributes to axis-K assessment (institutional culture) but does NOT offset active traveler
restrictions (bathroom codes, strip-search policies, prison-placement rulings). This principle
was already implicitly applied to all other countries; the UK was the outlier.
