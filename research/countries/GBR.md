# United Kingdom (GBR) — Research Note
Researched: 2026-08-21 · Score: **0.74** · Anchor: UPPER-MID (recent legal deterioration)

## Key findings (with sources)
- Supreme Court, For Women Scotland v Scottish Ministers [2025] UKSC 16 (16 Apr 2025):
  "sex" in the Equality Act 2010 = biological sex; a GRC does not change legal sex for
  Equality Act purposes. [reuters.com (from summary), apnews.com, commonslibrary
  briefing (403; from summary)]
- EHRC statutory Code of Practice for Services — in force ~June/July 2026 (drafted May
  2025, approved after review; in force "more than a year after" the ruling): single-
  sex spaces — toilets, changing rooms, hospital wards — "should be organised based on
  biological sex"; trans people directed to gender-neutral facilities or those
  matching birth sex; High Court dismissed a challenge to EHRC guidance (2026).
  Affects pubs, gyms, restaurants, hospitals. [bbc.com (x3), independent.co.uk (x2),
  yahoo/reuters code summary, birketts.co.uk]
- UN human rights experts (Aug 2026) warn the revised code effectively presents
  exclusion as a means of legal compliance, risking institutionalised discrimination
  against trans people. [jurist.org]
- Community impact: TransActual Gendered Spaces Report 2025 — fear and avoidance of
  services; NBC/AP interviews — trans people report feeling less safe, turmoil; BBC
  Dec 2025 — "living hell"; Independent Dec 2025 — "I live in a country that hates
  me". Reported increases in hostility/harassment toward visibly trans people.
  [transactual.org.uk (report + Know Your Rights), nbcnews.com, apnews.com,
  bbc.co.uk/c1e6l89wyewo, independent.co.uk]
- Positive framework remainder: gender reassignment remains a protected characteristic
  under the Equality Act (harassment/discrimination because of being trans still
  unlawful); GRC system still functions for birth-certificate purposes; UK passports
  still issue M/F per GRC (no X marker); same-sex marriage legal; police and NHS
  generally function; no criminalisation of any aspect of trans identity.
- No trans-specific entry restrictions or border issues reported.

## Visitor-focused summary
A trans visitor faces no criminalisation, functioning institutions, and continuing
anti-discrimination protection for being trans — but as of 2026 a statutory code
directs single-sex facilities (including public toilets and changing rooms) to be
organised by biological sex, meaning legal exclusion of trans women from women's
facilities and trans men from men's facilities as a compliance default. Practically:
use gender-neutral/accessible toilets where available (widely present in modern
venues); expect occasional challenges, misgendering, and a marked rise in social
hostility, including public harassment. Physical violence risk remains low. Scotland
is administratively distinct but covered by the same Equality Act framework; the
ruling originated there.
