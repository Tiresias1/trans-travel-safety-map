# Colombia (COL) — Research Note (traveler-outing axes, 2026-08-21)
Score: **0.55** · Band: Elevated Risk

## Axis findings (taxonomy A–K)
- **A Border/transit:** No trans-specific border friction documented; foreign documents
  recognised. BUT: US State Dept "reconsider travel to Colombia" (general crime/terrorism).
- **B Documents/bureaucracy (one of LatAm's strongest — self-recognition, no surgery):**
  Constitutional Court LGR since 1993 (no surgery required; **self-recognition**: "No one can
  be required to prove anything other than self-recognition. No medical diagnoses or surgical
  tests can be required" — MinJusticia). **"Trans" and "no binario" ID markers** (Nov 2025,
  Registraduría). **Comprehensive Trans Law being legislated** (Jun 2025, "one of the world's
  most robust" per El País). Marriage equality (2016). Anti-discrimination + hate-crime law.
  Intersex protections (1999). President Petro government pro-LGBTIQ+. [outrightinternational.org,
  minjusticia.gov.co, efe.com, latinamericareports.com]
- **C Gendered spaces:** No bathroom restriction law documented.
- **D/E Violence (SEVERE — armed-group "social cleansing," organized kill-lists):** **Sara
  Millerey: trans woman tortured and murdered in broad daylight** in Bello, Antioquia (viral
  video of torture before death; El País Apr 2025; CNN Aug 2025). **40+ trans women killed in
  one year** (Guardian Jul 2024 — armed-group "social cleansing" campaigns in Caquetá).
  **45 LGBTIQ+ homicides by Jun 2025** (Colombia Diversa: 14 trans women + 20 gay men).
  **Armed-group kill-lists** targeting LGBTQ individuals ("All will be military targets. We
  will not respect ages" — Guajira, Mar 2024). UN Independent Expert (May 2025): "close gap
  between laws and lived realities amid rising violence." Violence concentrated in armed-
  group/conflict areas against local trans communities, not tourists. [english.elpais.com,
  cnn.com, theguardian.com, colombiadiversa.org, jurist.org]
- **F Police:** No documented police-brutality-against-trans case. BUT: armed-group violence
  + impunity (Colombia Diversa: "do not have robust implementation" of laws). State cannot
  protect trans people from armed groups in certain regions. [colombiadiversa.org]
- **G Law (very strong):** Constitutional Court LGR (self-recognition, no surgery since 1993);
  marriage equality (2016); Comprehensive Trans Law being legislated (2025); ID markers (Nov
  2025); anti-discrimination + hate-crime law; intersex protections (1999). Petro government
  pro-LGBTIQ+. [outrightinternational.org, ibanet.org, cgrs.be]
- **H Detention/prison (Constitutional Court protective — positive):** Constitutional Court
  granted tutela for a trans woman prisoner subjected to violence/discrimination — ordered
  prison to apologize and offer searches by someone of her gender (Ámbito Jurídico). A
  positive axis-H response (like South Africa's Equality Court Mokoena ruling). [ambitojuridico.com]
- **I Health/medication:** Healthcare access generally available; no documented HRT customs
  issues.
- **K State & media climate (progressive government, armed-group violence):** Petro
  government pro-LGBTIQ+; Constitutional Court protective; strong legal framework. BUT:
  armed-group "social cleansing" + extreme homicide + impunity + "gap between laws and lived
  realities." The law-vs-practice gap is WIDE (analogous to Brazil but with organized
  armed-group campaigns).

## Score rationale
0.55 — Elevated Risk, tied with BRA (0.55), below CHL (0.71), above TUR (0.47).
- Tied with BRA (0.55): both have strong legal frameworks + extreme trans-murder tolls.
  Colombia's framework is marginally stronger (self-recognition since 1993 + ID markers Nov
  2025 + Comprehensive Trans Law + Constitutional Court prison tutela) BUT Colombia has
  **organized armed-group "social cleansing" with kill-lists** targeting LGBTQ individuals —
  arguably more organized/targeted than Brazil's diffuse violence. The two are genuinely close
  for a visitor.
- Below CHL (0.71): Colombia's 40+ trans murders/year + armed-group kill-lists + Sara Millerey
  torture-murder + impunity far exceed Chile's profile (Ever murder + hotel attack + "year of
  regression"). Colombia has stronger law + Constitutional Court + Petro, but the armed-group
  violence is extreme and organized.
- The score reflects the probability×severity frame: Colombia's armed-group "social cleansing"
  is high-severity (torture-murder, kill-lists) but lower-probability for a tourist (concentrated
  in specific conflict regions + local trans communities, not tourist areas; Bogotá's
  Chapinero LGBTQ district is well-established). A visitor in tourist areas faces lower risk
  than the headline murder figures suggest — but the organized armed-group targeting is a
  distinctive, severe concern.
