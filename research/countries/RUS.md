# Russia (RUS) — Research Note
Researched: 2026-08-21 · Score: **0.12** · Anchor: LOW-SCALE (repressive legal framework)

## Key findings (with sources)
- Legislative timeline: 2013 "gay propaganda" law → Dec 2022 expansion to ALL ages incl.
  "propaganda of gender transition", fines to 400,000 RUB / 15 days' detention; foreigners:
  arrest, 15-day detention, deportation (US State Dept advisory). July 2023: gender-
  affirming procedures and legal gender recognition BANNED entirely (Family Code
  amendments; existing same-sex marriages annulled). Nov 2023: "International LGBT
  movement" designated extremist organisation. [gov.uk CPIN June 2025 (fetched, §8.2-8.4)],
  [travel.state.gov (403; from summary)], [reuters.com (401; from summary)]
- UK Home Office CPIN conclusion (June 2025): LGB persons generally do not face a real
  risk of persecution *solely* on orientation — but "those in the North Caucasus, **trans
  people** and those who are publicly active in LGBT+ advocacy are likely to face
  treatment amounting to persecution." [gov.uk CPIN]
- Enforcement scale: 101 extremism convictions by mid-2025 (HRW) for participation in /
  symbols of the "LGBT movement"; 146 propaganda fines in 2024; propaganda cases up
  18-fold over a decade; 60m+ RUB in fines in two years. Enforcement extends to private
  expression: social-media posts, personal ads, photos with partner — even without
  witnesses (Coming Out report, Mar 2025). [hrw.org], [meduza.io], [CPIN §8.3.8]
- Club raids and intimidation: Pose (Orenburg) prosecutions; Moscow bar raids within 48
  hours of the extremist ruling; "second-class citizen" daily reality (Moscow Times
  2025). [foreignpolicy.com], [themoscowtimes.com], [theguardian.com]
- Travel-relevant case: Andrei Kotov, director of "Men Travel" (tours for gay men),
  arrested on extremism charges and died in custody (Dec 2024/2025 — OVD-Info/AP).
  [apnews.com (x2)], [nytimes.com (403; from summary)]
- Trans-specific: conversion "therapy" practices documented (BBC Siberia case, 2025);
  trans emigration "only means of survival" (Novaya Gazeta 2024); healthcare/medication
  supply disrupted (HRT imports). [bbc.com], [novayagazeta.eu]
- Nuance: Carnegie — criminal cases remain rare; indiscriminate persecution unlikely;
  enforcement targets public expression/activism rather than private existence.
  Same-sex activity legal. [carnegieendowment.org (extraction failed; from summary)]
- Chechnya: parallel de facto Sharia governance; documented anti-LGBT purges history;
  UK CPIN singles out North Caucasus as persecution-level risk. [CPIN §8.1]

## Visitor-focused summary
A discovered-to-be-trans visitor faces acute legal exposure: expressing anything
interpretable as "justifying gender transition" in public is an administrative offence
carrying detention and, for foreigners, deportation; the "extremist movement" framework
is vague enough to criminalise symbols and association; legal gender recognition is
impossible and police detention risks placement by passport sex; anti-LGBT violence is
met with no protection. Enforcement prioritises public expression, but the trend is one
of escalating raids and intimidation. Chechnya and the North Caucasus are in a separate
category of danger.
