# Pakistan (PAK) — Research Note (traveler-outing axes, 2026-08-21)
Score: **0.26** · Band: High Risk

## Axis findings (taxonomy A–K)
- **A Border/transit:** No trans-specific border friction documented (unlike UAE's X-ray
  screening). Foreign home documents recognised. BUT: police continuum of violence (see axis
  F) means any street-level police encounter risks outing/detention.
- **B Documents/bureaucracy (2018 Act, gutted by Shariat Court):** Transgender Persons
  (Protection of Rights) Act 2018 — third-gender legal recognition (Supreme Court 2009;
  census 2017); prohibits discrimination in education/employment/healthcare; self-determined
  gender identity. "Hailed as one of the most progressive pieces of legislation
  internationally" (Dawn). **BUT Federal Shariat Court (2023): declared some provisions
  "un-Islamic"** — gutting the law's gender-identity definitions; trans activists appealing
  to the Supreme Court. Amnesty: "This verdict is a blow... denial of essential rights."
  Legal rollback. [gov.uk CPIN, dawn.com, amnesty.org, nbcnews.com]
- **C Gendered spaces:** No bathroom restriction law documented. No Sharia cross-dressing
  criminalisation (unlike Malaysia's 13-state "posing" laws).
- **D/E Violence (SEVERE — documented killings):** **3 transgender women shot dead in
  Karachi** (gunmen, close range, bodies on roadside — AP/BBC/CBC, recent). Khyber
  Pakhtunkhwa: 267 cases of violence against transgender persons. "Bullet-riddled bodies
  found on highways" (The News). "From bullet-riddled bodies to harassment... rejection by
  landlords... dismissal at hospitals and police stations." Digital harassment → physical
  violence: Laiba (Peshawar trans woman) — man came with a gun after her dance videos
  (Digital Rights Monitor). No documented trans-TOURIST-specific killing (killings target
  local trans communities). [apnews.com, bbc.com, thenews.com.pk, digitalrightsmonitor.pk]
- **F Police (SEVERE — continuum of violence):** JPP policy brief: "Transgender persons
  face a continuum of violence at the hands of law enforcement — from routine harassment in
  public spaces to **arbitrary detention, extortion, and abuse in custody**." Police
  indifference/apathy: "poor implementation of laws." Laiba's case: police arrested suspect,
  recovered phone, but case closed through jirga (tribal) agreement under family pressure —
  perpetrator walked free. Trans people "dismissed at... police stations." [jpp.org.pk,
  tribune.com.pk, thenews.com.pk]
- **G Law:** 2018 Act (gutted by Shariat Court 2023 — legal rollback). Same-sex: Zina
  Ordinance (1979, Hudood) — unclear if applies to same-sex (specifically mentions man+
  woman); penalties include whipping/stoning; **"no known cases of government applying to
  same-sex; no known executions"** (UK CPIN). So same-sex NOT clearly criminalised nationally
  (unlike Malaysia/Indonesia/UAE). No anti-discrimination law beyond the gutted 2018 Act.
- **H Detention/prison:** Only 4 trans prisoners across all of Sindh (The News: "Four
  prisoners, seven years of inaction"). Sindh High Court 2021 case on prison placement.
  Small numbers but documented inaction. Police "abuse in custody" (axis F/H overlap).
  [thenews.pk, jpp.org.pk]
- **I Health/medication:** Healthcare discrimination documented; trans people "dismissed at
  hospitals." Drug laws are severe (Canada travel advisory).
- **K State & media climate (conservative, rollback, jirga):** 2023 Shariat Court gutting
  the 2018 Act — legal rollback. Anti-trans officials + violence reversing progress
  (76crimes Feb 2026). Conservative society: entrenched gender/sexuality beliefs. Jirga
  (tribal) system closing cases under family pressure. BUT: civil society response (SheDrives
  ride-sharing for trans safety in Lahore; Dawn academic defending the Act; activists appealing
  to Supreme Court; TANA report on access to justice). [76crimes.com, thediplomat.com,
  apnews.com (SheDrives)]

## Visitor-focused summary
Pakistan is high-risk — legally progressive on paper (the 2018 Transgender Persons Protection
Act was "one of the most progressive internationally," with third-gender recognition since
2009) but severely undermined in practice. The Federal Shariat Court gutted the Act in 2023
(declaring provisions "un-Islamic"), and transgender people face extreme violence: three
trans women were shot dead in Karachi (bodies on a roadside), 267 violence cases were
documented in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, and police commit a documented "continuum of violence —
from routine harassment to arbitrary detention, extortion, and abuse in custody." The jirga
(tribal) system closes cases under family pressure, leaving perpetrators free. Same-sex
conduct is NOT clearly criminalised nationally (the Zina Ordinance is unclear, no known cases),
and there is no Sharia cross-dressing criminalisation (unlike Malaysia) — but the severity
of social/police violence is extreme. No trans-traveler-specific border friction or death in
custody is documented (unlike the UAE or Indonesia).

## Score rationale
0.26 — High Risk, marginally above IDN (0.25), below MYS (0.28), above ARE (0.13)/RUS (0.12).
- Above IDN (0.25): no national same-sex criminalisation (Indonesia's new penal code Jan
  2026 effectively criminalises same-sex nationally + Aceh Sharia); 2018 Act exists (gutted
  by Shariat Court but still partially in force — Indonesia has no national LGR); no
  trans-traveler death in custody documented (Indonesia has Rodrigo's death in Bali custody);
  no presidential "security threat" classification (Indonesia has Prabowo's decree).
- Below MYS (0.28): Pakistan has no Sharia cross-dressing criminalisation (Malaysia
  criminalises "posing as a woman" in all 13 states); same-sex not clearly criminalised
  (Malaysia has s377A/B, 20 years + whipping). BUT Pakistan's extreme trans-killing violence
  (3 shot in Karachi + 267 cases in KP) + police continuum of violence (arbitrary detention,
  extortion, abuse in custody) + Shariat Court rollback + jirga system are comparable to
  Malaysia's systematic Jawi raids + documented trans killings.
- The probability×severity frame: Pakistan = lower-probability of state targeting (no
  criminalisation, 2018 Act exists) / higher-severity if encountered (police continuum of
  violence + documented killings); Indonesia = higher-probability (national criminalisation +
  Aceh Sharia + Bali border targeting) / high-severity (Rodrigo's death). Pakistan marginally
  above on the lower-probability-of-criminalisation point.
