# STYLE RULES — avoid LLM-isms in any text that may reach the site
Applies to every generated text field (summary, tangentialFactors/outOfScopeNotes/tangential,
localsOnly/localsOnlyNotes, link/source summaries, blind mirrors, and any other prose the
build may render).

The target voice is professional, serious, and report-like — Amnesty International, Human
Rights Watch, or a UN report. Write assertion and finding, not commentary about the writing.

## Hard prohibitions
- Never describe the previous state of this map, this record, an "earlier profile", or any
  other edit made before now. The map is not a changelog. No "the earlier profile's X was
  deleted/removed/superseded", no "this record previously said", no re-anchoring narratives,
  no "the audit removed", no "has been deleted/carried over from the prior summary".
- Bullet-point lists ("- item") in prose fields.
- Emojis, em-dashes (—) framed as stylistic tics, and unrequested markdown (headers, bold,
  code fences, numbered subsections).
- The "Rule of Three" beat (list the same count of items purely for symmetry), triplets that
  pattern-match ("documented, tolerated and safe"), and monolithic paragraph symmetry
  (paragraphs that all open the same way at the same length).
- Monolithic sentence construction: humans vary sentence length; don't render every sentence
  as a same-shape 15–25-word clause.
- Range clichés ("from X to Y" mid-list of two), universal warnings ("it's important to
  remember that", "it's worth noting that", "while this can be effective…"),
  the double-edged-sword metaphor, temporal clichés ("in today's fast-paced, interconnected
  world").
- Compulsive neutrality / fence-sitting ("while proponents argue X, critics point out Y…")
  unless the sources genuinely contradict.
- Conversational / assistant debris and prompt leaks ("Great question!", "Certainly! Here
  is…", "You've raised a critical point…", "You're absolutely right!").
- Compulsive listification ("four things to know: first, second, third…").
- Treating a jurisdiction as a changelog by over-emphasising "recent changes" vs stating the
  current state of the law (dates are fine as evidence; "in a major development, X has
  recently…" as an opener is not).

## Discouraged giveaway words (avoid where natural)
delve, paramount, navigate, nuanced, foster, harnessing, landscape, ever-evolving, underscore,
unpack, symphony, kaleidoscope, interplay, holistic, tapestry, beacon, pivotal, testament,
realm, dynamic, "my honest opinion", "not only X, but also Y", "at its core…".

## Manualist shortages
- "The map" / "the record" / "this dossier" as sentence subjects where a concrete subject
  exists ("the national law", "the visitor").
- Vague editorialising ("a notable quirk", "the significant thing") — say what the fact is.
